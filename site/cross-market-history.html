How to read this page

  1. Start with Current or jump to an episode. Check each source’s observation date. The numbered event lines mark the same dates across every chart in a historical card.
  2. Compare the same bond maturity. Select 2Y, 5Y or 10Y. In the event table, a positive spread change means the US Treasury yield rose more or fell less than the SGS yield. A basis point (bp) is 0.01 percentage point.
  3. Read stocks from a common starting point. A line at 110 is 10% above its base of 100. Each period shows up to five leaders and five laggards from the Stocks BETA 2 watchlist. Open the valuation comparison to line up a share’s price with its P/E and P/B.
  4. Check what looks unusual. In the FRED panels, ±2σ flags a reading to investigate against its own historical baseline. Check its date and unit. Lines moving around the same event can suggest a connection, but timing alone cannot establish a cause.

Research report

When do Singapore bond yields follow US Treasuries?

Compare matched maturities and track the spread as well as the direction. A common move in the ten-year yields can coexist with a large change in the UST–SGS spread. Singapore runs monetary policy through the trade-weighted exchange rate, so SGD funding conditions, local bond supply and currency expectations can affect SGS alongside global yields. MAS policy framework

Use these episodes to frame scenarios rather than to estimate the effect of a single announcement. Each year includes later policy decisions and other shocks. A higher yield points to lower prices for an otherwise identical bond, but the benchmark can change issues and omits the coupon income earned by an investor.

How to read the comparisons

Each chart on a card uses the same calendar bounds and numbered event lines. Historical cards start 12 months before the first event and run through 12 months after the last. Consecutive events less than 12 calendar months apart share a card, so windows of separate cards can overlap. Current ends today in Singapore and covers the trailing year.

Keep SGS month-end yields and daily closes distinct. The historical 2-, 5- and 10-year SGS series uses official month-end observations. UST month-end comparisons use the last available H.15 business-day reading within five calendar days of month end. The current chart adds daily MAS and UST closes separately. Blank periods are left open. MAS via SingStat, M700071 · Singapore Open Data Licence · MAS daily closing levels · Federal Reserve H.15

Rankings start with the 110-listing Stocks BETA 2 watchlist. Eligible US listings must have comparable USD prices on the same two SPX session dates. Weekly provider closes and BOS monthly observations are used separately. The longest cohort with at least six listings and four observations per listing qualifies. At least 90 days must separate the two rank dates. Each end of the ranking shows up to five shares, with fewer when necessary to avoid overlap. Price changes exclude dividends and FX. Prices with adjacent ratios below 0.60 or above 1.67 are screened out as possible corporate actions or extreme moves. Other corporate actions can still distort results. The archive is retrospective and has coverage and survivorship bias. Stocks BETA 2 · SPX source and status

FRED overlays use completed observation periods and revised data. CPI, PCE and payroll growth compare each month with the same month a year earlier. Quarterly CRE delinquency excludes farmland and covers domestic offices of US commercial banks. Data releases can lag the dates plotted. Existing FRED archive and sources · CRE definition

Two standard deviations flags an observation for review. The two series with the largest absolute readings after the first event are shown from a fixed set of labor, inflation, credit and financial-stress measures. Each is measured against its own sample mean and standard deviation before the first event, or before the current window. The baseline uses up to 60 month-end values or 20 quarters, with minimums of 24 months or eight quarters. A large value measures distance from that baseline and is sensitive to how stable it was.
