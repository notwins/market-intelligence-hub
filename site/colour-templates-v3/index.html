01 / Research style

A clear answer. Visible evidence. Useful visuals.

Use the full prompt below as the source instruction. The checklist here makes the workflow easy to scan.

Lead with the answer

Give one to five takeaways, the reporting cutoff and the main research question. Explain what happened, why it matters, the risks and what could change the conclusion.

Keep evidence distinct

Separate verified facts, source opinions, forecasts and your own analysis. Cite sources and distinguish observation dates from publication dates. Include the countercase.

Make each card useful

Pair a takeaway title with a relevant chart, table, diagram or comparison. Add a plain-English “how to read it” explanation, source, date and units. Aim for a balanced mix of meaningful visuals and concise explanation.

Show scenarios honestly

Label targets with their source, date, horizon, assumptions and units. Never invent a forecast path, probability, target or analyst call. Keep actual data separate from forecasts.

Apply one coherent theme

Choose the main-subject palette. Use near-white surfaces, quiet separators and readable text. Preserve the approved share and keyword identities.

Check before publishing

Verify figures, calculations, source links, labels, desktop and mobile rendering, contrast, clipping and file size. Document gaps and complete the publication gate.

Reference style

Use my DBS and STI banks report as the main reference for its card layout, charts, explanations and overall presentation. Inspect it first. Adapt the design to the new topic rather than copying its figures or conclusions.

My colour and presentation standard

Apply my current approved Colour & Tagging Standard. The Excel governs keywords, tickers, aliases, tags and colour mappings. The matching PDF governs visual design, charts and layout. Follow the documented precedence rules and do not invent or overwrite mappings.

Each report can have a different colour theme and background shades, while keeping approved colours for the same companies, terms and concepts consistent. If the current standard cannot be accessed, say so and continue the research without claiming it has been applied.

Research and writing

Start with one to five key takeaways. Explain what happened, why it matters, what the evidence supports, the outlook, risks and what could change the conclusion.

Use plain English, short paragraphs and point form where helpful. Include a bold key takeaway in each paragraph, but avoid making everything bold. Explain unfamiliar terms and make the report easy to understand without sounding condescending.

Separate verified facts, source opinions, forecasts and your own analysis. Include counterarguments and material risks. Cite sources and distinguish the date of the data from the date it was published. Don’t invent figures or present forecasts as guarantees.

Charts and market context

Inspect the charts and data already available on my Market Intelligence Hub and reuse suitable components and verified data where practical. Where relevant to the research, include:

  • Prices of the security, commodity or index discussed.
  • Bond yields, yield curves or credit spreads.
  • Relevant FX pairs.
  • S&P 500 / SPX prices or performance.

Include only charts that help explain the report. Identify the instrument, currency, units, source, data date and whether prices are adjusted or include dividends. Label FX direction and yield tenor clearly. Do not describe dated data as live.

Targets, scenarios and calls

Where the research provides them, add base-case, bull-case or bear-case levels, targets and calls to the relevant chart. Label each with its source, date, horizon, assumptions and units. Clearly distinguish actual data from forecasts.

If only a target level is available, show a labelled target line or marker. Don’t invent a forecast path, missing target, probability or analyst call. Explain qualitative scenarios in a card rather than assigning them invented numbers. Show the reference price and date for any calculated upside or downside.

Card style and visual explanations

Aim for roughly half meaningful charts, tables and diagrams and half concise explanation. Each substantive card should have:

  • A clear takeaway title.
  • A relevant chart, table, diagram or comparison.
  • A short layman explanation of how to read it and why it matters.
  • Source, date and units where applicable.

Choose the visual that best explains the point; don’t add decorative charts just to fill space. Use direct labels and avoid misleading scales, tiny text and clipped headings. Ensure the report works on mobile and remains understandable in greyscale.

File size and checks

Keep files small for later upload to Market Intel. Use lightweight code-drawn visuals and editable charts rather than large embedded images. State the final file size. Check figures, calculations, chart labels, links and the rendered desktop and mobile layouts. Clearly identify any unresolved gaps.

Deliver: [REPORT FORMAT AND DESTINATION].

02 / Exact source colours

All 150 swatches

Five cores per family, each in Light, Original and Dark. These are custom Sanzo Wada-inspired expansions, not historical 15-colour sets from his book.

Shade is not display mode. Light / Original / Dark are shade variants, not light-mode/dark-mode text tokens. Many swatches are unsuitable for normal text on white. Use the prescribed theme foregrounds and test actual contrast.

03 / Theme selection

Follow the question first

83 subject routes and six precedence rules from the V3 master. Choose deliberately; do not infer a report’s meaning from its colours.

  1. Explicit choiceUse the requested article theme.
  2. Primary thesisWhat question is the report answering?
  3. InstrumentBond, share, option, FX or commodity.
  4. SectorWhen the instrument is not primary.
  5. GeographyWhen the location itself is the lens.
  6. Mixed researchNeutral Earth & Slate 03 fallback.

Example: a US AI company’s bond financing report follows the bond thesis, not an AI-equity background. An options report uses Deep Vintage 05; it does not inherit Singapore colours merely because its reader is in Singapore.

04 / Keyword meaning

Tag what the evidence supports

The exact V1.2 canonical tags, aliases, priorities and semantic HEX values remain authoritative.

Resolve context before colour

Use valid whole phrases and exact tickers with company context. Never match US inside USD or Fed inside FedEx. Do not select a security or share class from a company-name mention alone.

Keep ambiguity visible

BOC can mean Bank of Canada or Bank of China. Short codes such as C, SE, AI and DE require context. If identity remains unresolved, leave it unmapped rather than guess.

Respect precedence

Context first, then the longest valid phrase. Numeric priority resolves equal spans only after identity is valid. Preserve the reconciled source precedence: original V1, then specific source extensions, then generic fallback.

Separate the layers

A story may have several independent tags. A text span has one dominant semantic colour. Entity, geography, instrument, editorial status and market direction are separate concepts.

07 / Future publication

The publishing gate

Design rules belong beside evidence checks. Machine validation is a safety net, not a claim that an article’s facts or interpretation are correct.

Checked by the publication validator

  • New authored research HTML must carry the required article contract.
  • Theme and route must agree with the verified V3 standard, or carry an explicit permitted override declaration.
  • Declared presentation tokens and semantic tag colours must match their source.
  • Required visible title, takeaways, source links, cutoff, limitations and chart/card context must exist.
  • The final staged Pages artifact is checked again before deployment.

Still requires editorial review

  • Whether a route fits the actual thesis and whether an alias really identifies the entity.
  • Source reliability, factual accuracy, calculations, contradictory evidence and forecast honesty.
  • Rendered contrast, mobile readability, clipping, greyscale distinction and interactive behaviour.
  • A completed review declaration is not proof that human judgement was correct.

Existing generated watchlist reports retain their separate archive, renderer and receipt checks until migrated; they are not certified as V3. Starting a new report

Download colour-templates-v3/research-template.html.txt, copy it into a new research HTML path and replace its placeholders. It is deliberately incomplete and fails validation until the report and its review are complete. No sample financial claims are supplied. Its initial Earth & Slate / generic bonds route is illustrative, not an automatic classification of the next topic.

Include exactly one <script type="application/json" id="mih-research-contract">. Fields are strict; unknown contract and semantic-tag override fields fail.

  • version: integer 3.
  • theme: exact two-digit theme ID, 01 through 10.
  • route: exact source route ID whose themeId equals theme. The six

precedence-only rows cannot be used as an article route. Alternatively use explicit-user-choice with user_choice_reference: a short public-safe reference to the actual approval, never private transcripts or personal data.

  • route_rationale: why the analytical question fits. Follow explicit user

choice, analytical question, instrument, sector, geography, then neutral Earth & Slate 03. Human review resolves competing routes. Options and derivatives route to 05; shares/equities to 04. Singapore geography is 06, but does not override an instrument-led credit or derivatives thesis.

  • presentation_tokens: copy the chosen theme's complete tokens object

exactly from the reference JSON. These are presentation declarations, not replacements for canonical semantic text colours.

  • cutoff: ISO 8601 timestamp with timezone. cutoff_id: visible element

containing that exact timestamp. title_id, limitations_id: nonempty visible elements. takeaways: 1–5 visible element IDs.

  • source_ids: nonempty list of visible HTTP(S) anchor IDs. Keep source dates,

observation dates and citations close to claims; presence is not verification.

  • semantic_tags: explicit list, empty if no approved tags are applied.

Each entry has id, element_id, context, light, dark, lightChip, darkChip. The four colours must equal the source canonical tag values. Explicit inline color/background-color on marked elements must use those exact HEX values (stylesheet-based rendering still needs review). The element must have data-mih-semantic="<canonical-id>". Every such marker must be registered exactly once. Unknown IDs and colour overrides fail. Context must explain ambiguities; a theme never authorizes a semantic tag.

  • visuals and cards: explicit lists. Empty lists need no_visuals_reason

and/or no_cards_reason. Do not add decorative charts just to fill a quota.

  • editorial_review: reviewer plus booleans evidence_checked,

semantic_context_checked, accessibility_checked, all true after actual review. Use a public-safe reviewer/role identifier, not private information.

Each visual has id, kind (chart, table, diagram, image), title_id, date_id, instrument_id, units_id, source_id, how_to_read_id, finding_id, limitations_id. Each card has the same fields except kind, instrument_id and how_to_read_id. Mark containers with data-mih-visual or data-mih-card. Each label must be a nonempty visible descendant of its container, and its source must be a registered source link. Use a meaningful “not applicable” explanation where a diagram has no numerical units.

Every marked card/visual and every SVG, canvas and table must be registered. For raster charts mark the containing figure; images are not automatically classified as charts because icons and logos are not data visualizations. Duplicate IDs, obvious placeholders and inline-hidden labels fail. For a substantial card containing a chart, register both containers and their respective labels. Retain exact values in a companion table where necessary.

What automation does not certify

The gate checks declared themes and semantic tokens plus structural label presence. It does not compute browser CSS, enforce all external styles, prove contrast on actual backgrounds, assess prose quality, verify facts/URLs, prove permissions, choose a route, infer tags, inspect rendered raster charts or confirm a reviewer's attestation. A declaration alone is not full V3 compliance. Review final desktop/mobile rendering and downloaded artifacts.

Before attesting, check exact applied HEX and V1.2 precedence/context guards (longest valid whole phrase; US not USD; Fed not FedEx; ambiguous short tickers remain unmapped without evidence), 4.5:1 text and 3:1 essential chart contrast, non-colour distinctions, gentle surfaces, correct dates/units/FX direction, zero-based ordinary bars, honest line scales, actuals versus projections, source-linked values, uncertainty and counterarguments. Separate company, listing, share class, headquarters and index membership. Never substitute unverified brand guesses or use semantic identity colours as profit/loss signals. Canonical dark text tokens are not Wada “Dark” swatches; V3 does not supply a complete dark-page theme. Never claim unperformed checks.
