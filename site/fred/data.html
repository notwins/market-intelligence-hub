What the colours mean.

Extreme σ ranks the largest absolute distances first: −4σ comes before +3σ. It combines all main groups; select Keep main groups to rank within each group. σ (sigma) measures how far an observation is from its own recent average. A positive sign means above average; a negative sign means below. Below 1σ is neutral, 1σ to below 2σ has a soft tint, and 2σ or more has a stronger outline. The direction is not a judgement of whether a reading is good or bad.

Calculation, missing history and source limits

For each dated observation: σ = (heat metric − prior mean) ÷ prior sample standard deviation. The baseline contains only nonmissing earlier observations within the chosen 5, 10, 15 or 20 calendar years, excluding that observation. Sample SD uses N−1; at least 12 prior values and nonzero SD are required. Each homogeneous series, country, category and measure has its own baseline. Publication-history tables use the latest publication for each observation period; every release remains in the native records. These are revised observation histories, not point-in-time vintage histories. Frequency is never changed to manufacture more observations.

Recommended heat metrics are disclosed in the table and detail view. Native level uses the original measure. Year-over-year percent change uses (current ÷ year-ago value − 1) × 100 and requires a positive base. Change from year ago subtracts that base in the source’s units. Monthly, quarterly and annual comparisons require the matching source-period date one year earlier. Daily, weekly and irregular series use the latest dated source row on or before that year-ago date, at most 10 days earlier; a missing matched value stays missing. No interpolation is used.

Baseline years describe a maximum window, not a promise of complete history. An unavailable σ is labelled with its reason. Values, observation dates, source update dates and retrieval dates are different concepts. This saved archive can contain publication lags and revisions; it does not recreate what was known at a historical trading date. The overview uses each dataset’s stated default series and measure.
