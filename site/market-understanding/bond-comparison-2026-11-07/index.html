Compare the 2041 bonds before adding more duration

Temasek 2041 is the closer maturity comparison to the 2041 Treasury. The 2070 bond is a substantially longer risk position, even if its extra quoted yield looks small.

Three takeaways

  • Match the cash flows, not just the maturity year. Two bonds both labelled 2041 can have different coupons, maturities, duration and call terms.
  • The quoted yield gap is not a clean credit spread. These observations have different timestamps; an exact spread needs aligned market inputs and appropriate cash-flow discounting.
  • More maturity is not automatically better value. The 2070 quote gives 15bp more yield than Temasek 2041 but about 65% more modified duration.

These are historical indicative ask observations, not executable offers. The source displayed no timezone. They were checked at 13:06 Singapore time on 6 November in the original report. Clean prices exclude accrued interest; fees, FX and dealer markup can change the cost.

The arithmetic gap between the two 2041 yields is 30.1 basis points. Calculation: (5.347 − 5.046) × 100 = 30.1bp. This is a non-synchronous quoted-yield difference, not an option-adjusted or matched-cash-flow credit spread, and not evidence of current cheapness.

The 2070 adds much more price sensitivity

Duration approximates the price response to a small yield change. A duration of 19 implies approximately a 19% price fall for a one-percentage-point yield rise, before convexity and other effects. It does not mean a guaranteed loss, a maturity date or a holding period.

The maturity extension increases quoted duration by 64.87%. Calculation: (19.042 ÷ 11.550 − 1) × 100. The corresponding ask-YTM increase is only 0.150 percentage points, or 15bp. This comparison is descriptive, not a complete valuation model.

Corporate yield changes contain more than Treasury-rate changes. Interest-rate risk, credit spreads, liquidity and call-option effects can move in different directions. These source durations are not independently reconstructed effective durations or option-adjusted estimates.

A fair same-maturity comparison takes four steps

1 · Match currency
Compare USD cash flows with a USD benchmark.
2 · Align timestamps
Use the same quote time, side and settlement.
3 · Match cash-flow risk
Account for coupon, maturity, duration and calls.
4 · Compare net outcomes
Include accrued interest, fees, spread and FX.

The Treasury par curve is a benchmark, not a menu of executable securities. There is no official 15-year point in the standard US Treasury par table. Interpolating between 10Y and 20Y is an approximation, not proof that a specific bond is undervalued. The 2070 also has no matching 44-year Treasury benchmark.

Issuer strength does not remove credit or currency risk

Temasek bonds are not Singapore government bonds. They are issued by Temasek Financial (I) with a Temasek Holdings guarantee, not a Singapore Government guarantee. Check Temasek’s bond disclosures and the relevant issue documents, including call terms.

A USD gain is not necessarily an SGD gain. Without hedging and intermediate cash flows, SGD return = (1 + USD investment return) × (1 + USD/SGD change) − 1. For example, a hypothetical 2% USD gain and 3% USD depreciation produce −1.06% in SGD before costs.
