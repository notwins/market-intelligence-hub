A staged approach to bonds, with separate views on CHF and gold

6 November 2026 · Research update: 04:42 UTC / 12:42 Singapore time; each observation is separately timestamped

There is a reasonable case for adding high-quality US Treasury duration after the election. Yields have fallen, markets are becoming more defensive, and government money-market assets have increased sharply. The more defensible approach is a measured, unlevered entry into intermediate maturities rather than an unconditional bet that settlement cash will lift bond prices over the next few days.

The distinction matters. A rally has already occurred, some of the good news is already in the price, and the US employment report is due later today. CHF and gold can provide diversification, but they are separate positions with separate risks.

Five takeaways

  1. Treasuries have already rallied. The official 10-year par yield fell from 4.95% on 3 November to 4.84% on 5 November. A new buyer needs a further favourable move to earn additional price gains.
  2. The clearest broad flow evidence is into government money-market funds. Their assets rose US$117.80 billion in the week ended 4 November. IEF and TLT share changes are mixed. This does not establish a delayed wave of purchases of long-dated Treasury bonds.
  3. Settlement can stagger cash availability, but does not predict returns. Most relevant US brokerage securities use T+1. Investors can trade or hedge before cash arrives, while fund redemptions can also require asset sales.
  4. CHF strength and gold strength are not interchangeable. CHF was firmer against USD in early reporting on 6 November. Spot gold was lower on the day at the latest observation used here, despite a strong weekly gain.
  5. Position size, maturity and currency exposure matter more than a broad “buy bonds” label. The 2–5-year area is a more cautious income choice; 5–10 years expresses a stronger tactical duration view. Payrolls, inflation, oil and auctions create two-way risk.

What the post-election market actually shows

Democrats won both the House and Senate in the 3 November US midterm election, according to Reuters. Investors interpreted divided government as a possible constraint on further fiscal expansion. The Reuters election-day follow-up also identified lower oil prices as a support for Treasuries.

Lower yields mean higher prices for otherwise comparable fixed-rate bonds. These observations establish repricing, not the identity of buyers or a cash-flow total. Official par yields are modelled benchmark yields, not executable prices for a specific security.

An election is not automatically bullish for bonds. The 2024 presidential election produced the opposite initial response as inflation and borrowing concerns lifted yields. Policy implications and starting valuations matter.

Where the money is moving

Chart 2. Weekly asset changes, US$ billion, week ended 4 November 2026; published 5 November. Source: Investment Company Institute. Changes are primarily driven by flows and serve as a proxy for net new cash. They are not a direct measure of Treasury purchases.

ICI reported total money-market assets up US$114.46 billion to approximately US$8.02 trillion. Government funds accounted for US$117.80 billion of the increase, while prime funds declined US$4.03 billion and tax-exempt funds rose US$693 million.

This supports a defensive cash-allocation interpretation. It does not prove long-duration buying. Government funds can hold cash, Treasuries, agency securities and repo. The measurement week also includes days before the election, and cash already subscribed may already be invested.

The separate ICI long-term mutual-fund series showed bond-fund outflows of US$11.07 billion for the week ended 28 October. That predates the election and excludes ETFs. It cannot be combined with the money-market series to claim a single net flow into or out of Treasuries.

Newly checked iShares data supply a narrower, mixed confirmation. From 4 to 5 November, IEF shares outstanding fell from 487.1 million to 485.8 million, a decrease of 1.3 million, while TLT shares rose from 597.1 million to 599.3 million, an increase of 2.2 million. On 5 November, IEF NAV was US$91.97, up 0.39%, and TLT NAV was US$81.90, up 0.20%, using the issuer's daily NAV changes. These are actual rises in dated Treasury-fund net asset values, not prices inferred from the par curve.

Sources, as retrieved 6 November; linked product pages and latest-holdings files can change: IEF 4 November holdings file, IEF 5 November key facts, TLT 4 November holdings file and TLT 5 November key facts.

The share-count changes are consistent with net redemptions from IEF and net creations in TLT. They do not identify the investors, source of proceeds or whether baskets were delivered in cash or securities. They are fund-level evidence, not a total for all Treasury buying. Price rises and trading volume alone do not establish cash inflows.

Settlement is a mechanism, not a forecast

Diagram 3. A possible cash sequence, not an observed flow. Source: SEC T+1 guidance, SEC settlement discussion and Fidelity settlement rules. Analytical illustration dated 6 November 2026.

Illustrative saleStandard T+1 settlementWhat it does not establish

Thursday 5 NovemberFriday 6 NovemberThat proceeds will buy Treasuries
Friday 6 NovemberMonday 9 NovemberThat weekend delay creates new demand

Actual fund cutoffs, settlement dates and cash availability vary. Money-market funds can settle the same day; other funds and cross-border transfers can take longer.

Investors may move from risky funds to bonds over several sessions. But institutions can establish exposure before cash settlement using existing liquidity, repo, credit facilities or futures. Known settlement dates can also be anticipated by markets. Conversely, withdrawals can force funds to sell liquid assets.

The missing evidence is the size, destination and timing of any uninvested cash. Without it, settlement alone is not enough to say a Treasury rally is likely over the next few days.

Over a few days, price changes dominate carry. In this illustration, yield increases of approximately 4.52bp, 1.99bp, 1.18bp and 0.67bp respectively erase seven calendar days of gross carry. Costs reduce that cushion further. This is a carry breakeven, not an inflation breakeven.

Bills and money-market funds offer liquidity and income, but little upside from a duration rally. For income with lower price sensitivity, 2–5-year Treasuries can be a more cautious starting point. A 5–10-year allocation is a stronger tactical duration position, still with less sensitivity than long bonds or STRIPS. Corporate bonds introduce credit-spread and issuer risks that are not part of this Treasury thesis.

The calculations assume face value 100, exact remaining maturities, semiannual coupons equal to the yield input, and an initial coupon date. Exact repricing determines the scenario returns. Actual securities require their own coupon, price, maturity, accrued interest and settlement inputs.

CHF and gold: specify the currency and the catalyst

Reuters on 6 November reported CHF 0.2% firmer against USD as equity risk aversion increased. It also reported December 25bp Fed-hike odds near 70%, down from more than 80% on Wednesday. Less expected tightening can help duration, but this is still a tightening-risk backdrop. Hammack and Barkin favoured gradual rate increases on 5 November.

CHF strength against USD means USD/CHF falls. CHF strength for an SGD investor means CHF/SGD rises. They need not occur together:

The SNB’s September assessment, summarised on 22 October, retained a 0% policy rate and flexibility to intervene in FX markets. MAS manages SGD against a basket, not a fixed bilateral USD or CHF rate.

At 02:48 UTC on 6 November, Reuters reported spot gold at US$4,063.39 per troy ounce, down 0.6% on the day but up 4% over the week. Higher oil and inflation concerns were weighing on prices.

The constructive gold case is lower real yields, a weaker dollar or demand for protection. The contrary case is higher real yields, tightening, cash-driven liquidation or reversal after a bounce. Falling nominal yields do not by themselves prove falling real yields. Gold also has no coupon.

Softer jobs or inflation, continued defensive demand and orderly auctions could extend the rally. Strong wages, renewed oil inflation, hawkish policy, weak auctions or forced selling could reverse it.

The view: selectively constructive on intermediate Treasuries, with a staged entry and explicit currency-risk decision. The evidence supports considering high-quality duration. It does not support a guaranteed settlement-driven rally, or an assumption that Treasuries, CHF and gold must all rise together.

Before acting, check the actual security, executable yield, fees, spread, liquidity needs, duration and intended USD exposure. A USD yield is not an SGD return. Review the thesis after the employment release rather than treating an earlier market observation as current.

  • 5 November chart point: ask 96.897, bid 96.575. The newer ask is 0.046 price points, or 0.0475%, higher. This is a quoted-price comparison, not a completed trade or a total return.
  • 5 October to 5 November: chart ask prices rose 92.860 → 96.897, a 4.35% price-only gain. Coupons are excluded. Over six months, the ask fell from 99.605 on 5 May to 96.897 on 5 November, down 2.72% before coupons. The rebound is not a gain across every lookback period.

The displayed headline price-change field has no clearly verified comparison period, so it is not used to calculate these returns. The dated chart points provide the baseline.

Does the yield curve support buying it?

It supports considering the 2041 as a deliberate longer-duration position, but does not prove it is cheap or that today is the best entry. On 5 November, official Treasury par yields were 4.46% at 2Y, 4.59% at 5Y, 4.70% at 7Y, 4.84% at 10Y, 5.27% at 20Y and 5.25% at 30Y. Treasury source.

The 2041 has about 14.27 years remaining, rather than being a new 15-year benchmark. Its quoted 5.053% yield is roughly 21bp above the previous day's 10-year par yield and 22bp below the 20-year par yield. These are different instruments and timestamps, not a precise relative-value spread. There is no published 15-year point in the standard Treasury par table, and an interpolation is not an executable quote.

What supports a purchase today is the combination of a roughly 5% indicated USD yield, a confirmed recent price recovery, defensive demand and the possibility of further yield declines. What weakens the case is that some of the rally is already in the price, major US data remain ahead, and roughly 10 years of modified duration means a meaningful loss if yields rise. This is more rate risk than the original report's 5–10-year core preference.

The 4.75% coupon is not the 5.053% yield to maturity. Buying below par adds potential pull-to-par to the coupon stream if the bond is held and paid as promised. The simple annual coupon divided by the clean ask is about 4.90%; yield to maturity uses all scheduled cash flows and their timing.

BSM durations are dated 5 November. Exact factsheets: Treasury 2041, Temasek 2041, Temasek 2070.

The Temasek 2070 offers only 15bp more indicated yield than the Temasek 2041, at the same displayed quote time, but about 65% more modified duration. That is a substantial increase in price sensitivity for a small extra yield. It is not automatically the better buy because its clean price is near 50 or because it matures much later.

Both Temasek issues are obligations of Temasek Financial (I), guaranteed by Temasek Holdings. They are not Singapore government bonds and do not carry a Singapore government guarantee. The issuer describes the bonds as Aaa/AAA-rated, but ratings do not eliminate credit, liquidity or price risk. Temasek issue information and government non-guarantee.

For the USD 2041, compare a maturity- and duration-aware USD Treasury benchmark plus an adequate credit/liquidity premium. For the USD 2070, the US Treasury curve has no matching 44-year benchmark. Subtracting a 30-year Treasury yield does not create a clean matched-maturity credit spread. Comparing either USD issue directly with an SGD government yield also mixes currency risk.

The Temasek 2070 has a make-whole call provision and a par call from 6 April 2070; the 2041 also has call terms. Ask for yield to worst and the actual dealing spread, not just yield to maturity. A low clean price is not proof of undervaluation: low coupons and very distant principal repayment explain much of the discount. For immediate cash income, coupon divided by the clean ask is about 4.90% for the Treasury, 3.39% for Temasek 2041 and 4.95% for Temasek 2070. These simple current yields are not yields to maturity.

Calculations use the quoted ask yields, semiannual scheduled coupons, assumed settlement on 9 November 2026, Actual/Actual for the Treasury and 30/360 for Temasek. They exclude elapsed-time carry, fees, FX, default and the value of call options. They treat the Temasek cash flows as continuing to maturity, so they are not option-adjusted effective-duration results.

For Temasek, the yield change combines interest rates and credit spread. A 25bp fall in Treasury rates can be more than offset by a 50bp increase in the credit/liquidity premium, leaving the bond's overall yield 25bp higher and its price lower.

At the quoted bid/ask levels, buying and immediately selling at unchanged quotes would cost approximately 0.38% of the clean ask for the Treasury, 0.68% for Temasek 2041 and 0.77% for Temasek 2070, before bank markup, other fees or FX. This matters greatly for a trade intended to last only a few days.

Practical conclusion: the Treasury 2041 is a plausible higher-duration choice if the goal is USD income plus participation in further yield declines. Temasek 2041 is the closer maturity comparison. Temasek 2070 needs a separate, much stronger willingness to accept long-horizon rate and credit risk; its small additional yield over Temasek 2041 does not by itself justify that risk. A staged purchase can spread entry timing, but cannot remove losses or make an unsuitable bond suitable. All three are USD bonds: a 3% fall in USD/SGD would turn a 2.56% USD bond-price gain into approximately a 0.52% SGD loss, before coupons and costs.

Bank confirmation of the executable ask, accrued interest, settlement, minimum denomination, fees and FX exposure remains necessary. No transaction is proposed as already completed.
