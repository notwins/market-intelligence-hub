01 / What the earlier Treasury reports were really testing

Lower yields are useful. The reason they fall matters.

The reports contained several different bond theses. One concerned duration, another credit quality, and a third the ability to finance a position through volatility. Treating them as one “buy bonds” call would hide the main risks.

Three risk channels, three different questions.

Interest-rate riskWhat happens to the bond’s price if its required yield changes?
Borrower riskWill the issuer pay, and is the additional spread enough?
Financing riskCan the investor meet interest and collateral requirements without selling?

Government bonds are not an automatic hedge against every equity decline. The Fed’s 16 October research argues that inflation dynamics and the type of economic shock influence safe-haven behaviour. An inflation shock can hurt bond and equity valuations together. [2]

The counterargument to buying the longest maturity is straightforward: yields can remain high, or rise further, if inflation or term-premium concerns persist. A higher yield is compensation to examine, not proof of a bargain.

Coupon Treasuries and STRIPS

Choose the cash-flow profile before choosing the maturity.

InstrumentCash-flow patternUseful decision question
Coupon TreasuryPeriodic coupons and principal at maturityDoes the income and duration fit the holding period?
Principal STRIPA single payment at maturityCan the investor tolerate high sensitivity and no interim coupon?

How to read: at the same yield and final maturity, concentrating cash flow at the end generally increases duration relative to a coupon-paying bond. A long STRIP is not an income substitute.

02 / The bonds discussed in the previous editions

Keep the issuers. Recheck the price.

The earlier reports’ useful comparison was borrower quality, not the AI label. Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, P&G and Main Street appeared in the archived issuance discussions. Below is the analytical role of each, rather than a recycled quotation presented as current.

Microsoft

Corporate-bond comparison

Use it as a benchmark for the premium another issuer pays. Compare similar maturities, contractual ranking and yield conventions before treating a higher yield elsewhere as extra value.

What to check next: current bid/offer, exact maturity, issue versus secondary spread, and the effect of capital expenditure on future free cash flow.

Meta

Investment versus cash generation

Focus on whether future cash flow supports the funding programme. A financing announcement, a priced issue and a completed settlement are different stages. An orderbook is demand, not the final amount borrowed.

What could change the view: better cash generation, lower capital intensity, wider compensation for risk, or deterioration in creditor protection.

Oracle

Refinancing and spread discipline

A large spread deserves a larger due-diligence effort. The 2 November SEC filing exists, but its full pricing text could not be re-opened in this review. Earlier quoted terms are retained only in the labelled archive below, not promoted to current verified prices. [6]

What to check next: cash interest coverage, debt maturities, liquidity, contractual obligations and whether the new funds refinance old debt or increase leverage.

P&G

Non-AI comparison

Keep a non-AI issuer in the comparison set. The question is whether the difference in yield adequately pays for different business, leverage, maturity and liquidity risks. An unrelated business model is not, on its own, a guarantee of lower risk.

What to check next: same-date yields at comparable duration, not just coupons printed in different offering documents.

Main Street Capital

Lender and funding risk

A lender’s bond requires analysis on both sides of its balance sheet. Examine the loans it owns and the borrowing it uses to finance them. A bond replacing a revolver changes the funding profile; it does not necessarily add the same amount to net debt.

What to check next: nonaccruals, cash versus capitalised interest, portfolio marks, funding maturities and secured versus unsecured claims.

Corporate debt is not project debt

A famous tenant is not the same as a debt guarantee.

Customer or tenantPays for computing capacity, equipment or space under a contract.
Project or equipment borrowerOwns or finances the assets and owes lenders under separate debt documents.
Secured and unsecured creditorsHave different claims on cash flow and collateral if the borrower cannot pay.

Read the legal claim, not just the company name beside the project. Construction delays, power access, utilisation, equipment resale values and customer concentration can matter independently. The relevant protections must be verified in each transaction’s documents.

03 / What repayment evidence can and cannot tell us

Prices can move before payments fail.

Exhibit 2 • What a credit spread measures

A credit spread is compensation for risk, not a default count.

Lower-risk benchmarkA Treasury yield is the reference rate for the comparison.
Extra compensationThe spread reflects credit, liquidity and other risks beyond that benchmark.
Borrower outcomesA price change alone does not prove an improvement or deterioration in actual debt payments.

Read spreads alongside borrower cash flows and repayment evidence. A narrow spread is not proof that every borrower is safe, just as a wider spread is not a default count.

Payment stress needs a precise vocabulary.

SignalMeaning and limitation
30 / 60 / 90+ days lateDifferent depths of arrears. Report the population, definition and denominator.
NonaccrualInterest is no longer accrued normally. Not the same as a realised investor loss.
PIK interestInterest is capitalised or paid in securities rather than current cash. It need not be a contractual default.
Distressed exchangeA restructuring that may count as default under the relevant agency’s methodology.
Payment defaultA contractual failure, subject to the debt terms and applicable grace period.
Charge-offA lender records a loss. Fed charge-off rates are annualised and net of recoveries.

Fed bank-series definitions include 30+ days past due and accruing plus nonaccrual; they differ from borrower-panel transition measures. [5] Other rows are general analytical definitions, not findings of a new default.

Practical watchpoint: investigate cash interest, refinancing needs and the ability to cure arrears together. Do not turn one PIK figure or one rating downgrade into a claim that the whole credit market is defaulting.

Financing amplifies losses and can trigger a collateral call.


Loan L = US$700,000; initial equity = US$300,000.
Illustrative maximum loan / collateral ratio h = 75%.
Equity = V − L. Borrowing headroom = h × V − L.


Yield shockInvestor equityBorrowing headroom
0bpUS$300,000+US$50,000

At +100bp, the investment loses US$145,000. That is US$145,000 ÷ US$300,000 = 48⅓% of initial equity. The separate US$58,750 headroom deficit must not be added to that loss.


Two different remedies: repay US$58,750 of the loan using external cash, or add collateral worth exactly US$235,000 ÷ 3 = US$78,333⅓ if that added collateral receives the same 75% advance rate. Different collateral recognition produces a different requirement.


Funding cost is another calculation. A 1 percentage-point annual rate increase on a US$700,000 unchanged loan adds US$7,000 of annual interest. It is not another bond-price loss.


Original illustrative maths. The 75% limit is not an OCBC or other bank’s actual rule. Actual margin calculation, cure period, haircuts and cash treatment must be confirmed with the lender. Repayment here assumes external cash, not a sale that also reduces collateral.

−7 × 0.015 + ½ × 60 × 0.015² = −9.825%.
