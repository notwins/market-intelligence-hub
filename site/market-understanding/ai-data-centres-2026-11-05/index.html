01 / Five takeaways / investment judgement

AI data centres: from power to profit

A company and financial guide to the machinery behind generative AI, built around durable core earnings and sensible entry prices.

1. Start with the business that survives a slower AI spending cycle. For a focus on established earnings, Alphabet and Microsoft remain the first core candidates to assess. Their existing distribution and cash-generating franchises support AI investment; spending itself does not establish shareholder value. [S001] [S002]

2. Diversification must reach beyond the same customer budgets. GPUs, HBM, switches, optics and cooling are different products, but their orders can depend on the same cloud buildouts. Amphenol, Schneider Electric and Eaton deserve attention for broader end markets. Their price, acquisition funding and changing revenue mix still matter.

3. Follow cash after investment, not the largest growth or margin figure. The financial appendix states seven financial measures for all 40 original companies and adds 12 targeted power/connectivity spotlights. Definitions are preserved; unlike cash-flow measures are flagged.

4. The physical constraints are real, but specifications need dates and units. A deployed rack, a future platform roadmap and an entire campus are different things. InfiniBand is an industry standard; NVIDIA acquired Mellanox. The 600 kW claim in the notes is qualified in the technology review. [S047][S048]

5. Buy in stages against evidence of returns. Prefer understandable earnings, defendable customer relationships and a valuation cushion. Treat high AI dependence as a shared portfolio risk; require improving utilisation and cash conversion before increasing exposure.

02 / From power to profit

The full system—and where investors get paid

Physical inputs support a digital service. Power operates the equipment; cooling removes heat; processors run calculations; memory and networks keep data moving. Software schedules the work and turns model output into something customers use. All layers must work together, so the slowest constraint can delay revenue.

Cash does not flow automatically to every layer. Contracts, pricing power, working capital and financing determine who retains the value. A manufacturer can ship record volumes while funding inventory; a cloud operator can show rising sales while construction absorbs cash. Those are different economic risks.

Layman’s summary: Think of a factory with reliable power, production machines, conveyors and an order book. Fast machines help only when the complete factory delivers useful output that customers pay for.

What happens when someone asks an AI a question?

TermPlain explanationWhy investors should care

Architecture, context length, batch size and precision change the bottleneck. Avoid a single universal energy-per-query or tokens-per-second number. [S049][S061]

Layman’s summary: Training is preparing the factory’s process. Inference is using that process to fulfil orders. Better machines, memory and scheduling can each improve the result.

05 / From power to profit

Electricity: scale matters, location matters more

A global energy total cannot tell you when one campus will connect. Local transmission capacity, transformers, permits and dependable supply determine the delivery schedule. Proposed grid-connection queues can contain speculative or duplicate projects, so announced megawatts are not the same as funded, operating demand. [S042]

MeasureWhat it meansDo not confuse it with
kW / MW / GWPower: the rate of energy use or supply. 1 GW = 1,000 MW = 1,000,000 kW.kWh / MWh / TWh, which measure energy over time.
PUETotal facility energy ÷ IT equipment energy.The efficiency of the model, or useful output per token.
Nameplate capacityA design or connection rating.Actual average use, customer utilisation or revenue.
Illustration—not a project forecast: 100 MW of IT load at PUE 1.25 requires 125 MW at the facility level. If held constant for 8,760 hours, that equals 1,095,000 MWh, or 1.095 TWh. Lower PUE reduces overhead; it does not guarantee efficient computation.

Investment implication: Equipment qualification, service capability and delivery reliability can be valuable. The test is whether orders convert into cash at an adequate return on the additional factories, inventories and warranties needed to fulfil them.

09 / From power to profit

Memory and storage are complementary, not interchangeable

Capacity and bandwidth solve different problems. Capacity determines what fits; bandwidth affects how quickly data can move. A larger context or batch can consume more memory, while faster memory helps only when data movement is limiting performance. Neither removes all compute or network constraints. [S049][S061]

The three established HBM suppliers are a starting supplier map, not a permanent guarantee of scarcity. Micron, SK hynix and Samsung disclose HBM businesses. Generation qualification, yields, customer allocations and future capacity determine pricing. An undated “sold out” label is insufficient evidence of durable excess returns. [S063][S065][S066]

Update the storage company boundary. Sandisk separated from Western Digital in February 2025 and trades as SNDK. WDC and STX represent HDD and mass-capacity storage exposure; they should not be credited with the separated Sandisk NAND/SSD business. [S067][S068]

Layman’s summary: HBM is a very fast workbench beside the processor. SSDs and hard drives are storage rooms. They solve different problems and have different pricing cycles.

12 / From power to profit

The economic engine: useful output, utilisation and price

QuestionEvidence to seekWhat it protects against
Is output useful?Task success, quality, latency, paid adoption and retention.Token growth with little customer value.
Is capacity productive?Utilisation, delivered sites, power availability and workload scheduling.Paying for installed assets that cannot earn.
Does price cover cost?Realised pricing, service margin, support and replacement investment.Price competition outrunning efficiency improvements.
Who funds the expansion?Customer advances, cash flows, leases, debt and stock issuance.Profitable accounting supported by fragile funding.

Lower cost per task can expand demand without guaranteeing higher profit. Customers may consume more, competitors may reduce prices, or the mix may shift toward harder tasks. The useful test is the return earned on additional capital after these changes, not a fixed energy cost attached to one query.

Layman’s summary: A cheaper factory process helps. Shareholders benefit when enough customers buy the output at a price that also pays for the machines, finance and future replacements.

Layman’s summary: Depreciation spreads the cost of an asset across years. Extending that period can lift reported profit, but it does not refund the original purchase. A disclosed estimate change alone does not establish misconduct.

15 / From power to profit

Growth, profit and cash can tell different stories

Coherent’s working-capital investment absorbed cash despite positive earnings. FY2026 net income was US$786.884m, while CFO of US$79.514m less US$1,102.909m cash PP&E produced −US$1,023.395m. The filing attributes weak cash conversion mainly to inventory growth; this is a funding question, not merely a margin question. [S070]

Fabrinet shows a similar distinction at a different margin level. FY2026 net income was US$473.027m; CFO less cash PP&E was US$4.222m. Inventory, receivables and factory investment matter. Its net income also includes an investment gain, so operating performance deserves separate attention. [S072]

Lumentum’s large GAAP loss is a different case. A US$7,756.6m noncash convertible-debt extinguishment charge dominates its FY2026 net loss. Operating profit and CFO were positive. The proposed Marvell combination now adds exchange-ratio, approval and buyer-share-price risks. [S071][S103]

17 / From power to profit

Original companies: questions before committing capital

Business quality is a starting point, not a price target

Alphabet and Microsoft have established cash-generating franchises. Their distribution, customer relationships and existing earnings make them useful starting points for studying AI investment. Search durability, enterprise demand and the cash needed for infrastructure still need separate tests. This public edition makes no current buy or sell call.

TSMC, ASML and KLA offer manufacturing and process capabilities. Their roles differ, but their customers, investment cycles and policy/geographic risks overlap. Assess the combined exposure rather than treating each company as an independent risk bucket.

NVIDIA and Broadcom have strong positions with concentrated infrastructure exposure. Customer spending, financing commitments, inventory and the pace of technology changes matter alongside growth. Broadcom’s financing structure deserves specific scrutiny. [S115]

Refresh valuation independently. The private research included dated third-party valuation estimates. Those tables and rankings dependent on them are not republished here. Before any transaction, update the price, normalise earnings, assess financing needs and compare expected cash generation with the price paid.

Decision boundary: The report does not know a reader’s existing holdings, liquidity needs, investment horizon or loss tolerance. A business-quality comparison is not a personalised allocation.

Additional companies: breadth and execution risks

Understand the source of earnings before choosing an entry price

Amphenol serves more than data centres. Its connectors and sensors span communications, industrial and other markets. Acquisition funding, changing customer mix and integration affect the investment case. The FY2025 table predates the January 2026 CommScope transaction. [S073] [S104]

Schneider Electric and Eaton provide broader electrical-system exposure. Buildings, industry and utilities broaden the demand base, but fast-growing data-centre orders can increase common AI exposure. Schneider’s pending PTC agreement adds financing and integration questions; a proposed transaction is not completed operating performance. [S122] [S092]

GE Vernova and Siemens Energy require execution and cash-flow checks. Service franchises and grid demand can help, while customer advances create delivery obligations. GE Vernova’s proposed Trench transaction remains pending. Siemens Energy’s next annual results are scheduled for 11 November 2026, after this research cut-off. [S081] [S083] [S123] [S124]

Generation businesses require a separate power-market thesis. Contract terms, regional electricity pricing, capital requirements and regulation differ from selling connectors or electrical equipment. A shared AI demand story does not make their cash flows interchangeable.

Valuation boundary: This public edition omits the bank/analyst price-and-fair-value screen and its associated purchase order. It retains the operating-business comparisons without implying any current price is attractive.

Stress the shared demand driver before adding more names

Qualitative scenarios—not numerical forecasts

ScenarioWho feels it first?Evidence / investment response
Cloud capex slowsAccelerators, HBM, networking, optics, servers and cooling.Watch cancelled/deferred orders, backlog conversion and customer concentration. Review total portfolio exposure to the same buyers.
Sites lack power / permitsCloud operators and suppliers waiting for deployment.Separate booked orders, shipments, acceptance and paid capacity; avoid equating backlogs with current revenue.

Backlog is a bridge to future revenue, not cash without conditions. Orders may depend on power, financing, customer acceptance or cancellation terms. Customer advances help fund equipment suppliers but carry obligations to deliver. Those obligations remain even when reported operating cash flow looks strong. [S081][S083]

Distinguish a durable moat from temporary shortage pricing. A component can be indispensable and still earn lower margins after supply expands or architecture changes. The question is who controls qualification, intellectual property, customer relationships and replacement demand after the shortage eases.

Layman’s summary: The most useful stress test is simple: if the largest AI buyers cut their next construction budget, which of your companies still have customers, cash and a reason to exist?

29 / Definitions / prevent false comparisons

How to read the financial tables: earnings and attribution

IssueTreatment in this report
Reported vs normalised profitReported net income is shown. Investment marks, tax releases, legal charges and sale gains can make it a poor recurring-profit proxy. No pretax gain is subtracted mechanically from after-tax profit to invent an adjusted EPS.
Parent / common / consolidatedThe original rows retain their source scope. INTC: −267 parent vs +26 consolidated. HPE: +57 parent vs −59 common. ORCL: 17,087 consolidated vs 16,984 common. The power and connectivity tables state their separate owner scopes.
Gross-profit classificationAMZN leaves fulfilment and technology/infrastructure below gross profit. ORCL’s derived GP excludes separately shown amortisation. Compare operating economics as well as headline GP margins.
R&D is not total AI investmentAMZN’s technology/infrastructure expense is broader than R&D and is not inserted into that column. Vertiv is ER&D. SK hynix reports expensed R&D of KRW6,465,637m versus gross spending 6,732,527m.
Accounting basisTSMC uses Taiwan IFRS consistently; ASML uses US GAAP consistently. Do not combine ASML IFRS CFO with US GAAP income/capex or splice alternative TSMC reporting bases.
Share compensationCFO adds back noncash stock compensation. This helps cash flow but can dilute owners. Snowflake/Atlassian stock compensation exceeds their simple cash residuals.
Source precisionExact source amounts are retained. MediaTek capex is less precise than its earnings; Foxconn mixes presentation and finer R&D disclosures. Derived arithmetic cannot make the inputs more precise.

Annual periods must stay visible. NVIDIA FY2026 ended in January, Microsoft in June and Apple in September. Siemens Energy FY2026 results were still pending at the research cut-off. The appendix uses reported full years, while the current-update section explicitly labels quarterly or trailing evidence.

Layman’s summary: “Profit” can refer to different owners and include unusual gains. First establish whose profit, which period and which accounting basis—then compare the companies.
