Slide 1

Key Message

Begin with durable existing earnings, then test the return on new investment and the entry price.

Talking Points

Alphabet combines advertising, distribution and cloud. Microsoft combines enterprise productivity, cloud, security and developer tools. Existing customer relationships support the thesis; they do not guarantee profitable AI investment.

Capital expenditure is an investment commitment, not AI revenue. Total R&D is also not a separately disclosed AI-only spending measure.

GPUs, memory, networking and cooling can depend on the same customer budgets. Diversification requires different economic drivers, not just different product names.

Alphabet and Microsoft are research candidates because they have substantial existing businesses. This is the author’s analytical judgement about business durability. It does not establish an entry price, allocation or expected return.

How To Read

Treat the three rows as separate tests: core earnings, investment payback and price/concentration. The counterargument challenges the core-business thesis.

Assumptions / Risks

AI may change search behaviour and software pricing. Established distribution can lose value if customer behaviour changes.

Cash investment can rise faster than revenue. Investment valuation gains can also inflate accounting profit.

No portfolio weights are assumed. Refresh prices and cash-flow evidence before dealing.

Slide 2

Key Message

An AI answer relies on a complete operating system. Faster chips alone cannot ensure faster or cheaper service.

Talking Points

Conventional language-model inference tokenises a prompt, processes it during prefill and generates tokens during decode. It reuses trained weights for each response.

CPUs handle general and control work. GPUs and custom ASICs such as TPUs accelerate suitable workloads. Software schedules work. Power supplies equipment and cooling carries heat away.

HBM is stacked DRAM for weights and working state. Capacity determines what fits. Bandwidth affects data movement. The KV cache is stored data, not a chip category.

Local scale-up links and wider scale-out fabrics serve different roles. InfiniBand is an industry standard. RoCE carries RDMA over Ethernet. NVIDIA acquired Mellanox, not the standard.

How To Read

Centre: calculation. Top: software control. Sides: electricity and heat. Lower double-headed links: data communication. These arrows show functional relationships rather than a serial pipeline.

Caveats

CPU-only deployments may not need HBM. The diagram omits host DRAM, cache, storage and other support systems.

Low-batch decode can be memory-bandwidth-limited. Long-prompt prefill is often compute-intensive. Batch size, model design and workload can change the bottleneck.

No networking protocol wins universally. This diagram supplies no rack-power, energy-per-query or performance estimate.

Key Message

The IEA projects a large increase in global data-centre electricity use, but the investment opportunity depends on where and when usable capacity arrives.

Talking Points

The IEA estimates 485 TWh of global data-centre electricity consumption in 2025 and projects 950 TWh in 2030. These are the exact source-stated values used in the chart.

The series covers all data centres, including AI and other computing workloads. It must not be relabelled as AI-only electricity demand.

The forecast strengthens the case for examining power delivery, grid equipment, cooling and reliable operations. It does not establish the margins or returns that any individual supplier will earn.

TWh measures energy over time. It is different from the kW, MW or GW ratings of equipment and campuses. This chart cannot establish the energy used by one query or one rack.

No percentage or multiple has been rounded onto the slide. The comparison uses the exact source-stated 485 and 950 TWh values.
