FIVE MEMOS / ONE INVESTMENT DISCIPLINE

Conviction needs
a price.

An attractive investment needs a credible route from today’s price to tomorrow’s cash. Howard Marks’s latest five written memos connect long-term interest rates, private credit and AI through that question. This report pairs his arguments with dated primary evidence and a practical review framework.

Five takeaways

  1. Long maturities stayed more expensive to fund. From 22 September to 5 November, the two-year Treasury yield fell 25 basis points and the 30-year fell 4.
  2. Access to cash deserves its own review. Borrower repayment, loan protection and fund withdrawal terms answer different questions.
  3. AI’s usefulness leaves the purchase-price question open. A business can grow while investment spending absorbs its operating cash.
  4. The strength of a contract depends on its terms and dates. A committed lease, a conditional guarantee and an optional renewal create different claims.
  5. The funding plan affects how long an investor can wait. Align asset cash flows, borrowing obligations and the currency of future spending.

01 / memos

Five letters ask increasingly connected questions

The archive’s latest five written memos span 6 November 2025 to 22 September 2026. The latest listed title remains the September letter at the research cutoff. The summaries below preserve the date and status of Marks’s views.

22 September 2026 · Long-term rates

Shall We Repeal the Laws of Economics – Part III

Policy can improve market functioning while underlying borrowing pressures persist. Marks argues that persistent inflation, fiscal borrowing and competing capital demands help explain higher long-term yields. He questions whether bond buybacks can durably offset these pressures. The memo acknowledges Treasury’s stated liquidity-support purpose, so yield suppression is Marks’s interpretation of the policy. He distinguishes nominal dollar repayment from preservation of purchasing power. His fiscal concern has an uncertain timetable. He recognises the dollar’s reserve role and the strengths of US businesses, and he sees risks in a large move away from US assets. Investors with non-dollar spending needs may have a reason to diversify currency exposure.

DECISION QUESTIONWhat return compensates for inflation, future borrowing and the time you commit your money?

Reading qualification. Treat numerical macro claims as observations cited in a September memo until checked against their primary releases.

9 April 2026 · Private-credit ownership

What’s Going on in Private Credit?

Credit quality, valuation and access to cash require separate checks. Marks argues that competition weakened direct-lending terms. Infrequent valuations can conceal changing credit risk. He connects software disruption, withdrawals and refinancing, while distinguishing sentiment from borrower performance. Redemption restrictions had helped avoid forced sales. He also sees opportunities as lenders become more selective and loan terms improve.

DECISION QUESTIONCan the borrower pay, can the lender enforce, and when can the investor receive cash?

Reading qualification. Oaktree’s comments about its own underwriting and relative positioning are the manager’s assessment.

26 February 2026 · Technology conviction

AI Hurtles Ahead

Greater confidence in AI’s usefulness still leaves its investment value uncertain. Marks becomes more confident in AI’s usefulness and demand for inference, the work a trained model performs. He still questions future capacity, revenue quality and investment pricing. He sees a continuing role for judgment in unfamiliar situations and retains his preference for moderate, selective participation.

9 December 2025 · AI investment pricing

Is It a Bubble?

A transformative industry can produce poor returns at the wrong price or financing terms. Marks questions which AI businesses will earn durable returns after competition, investment and financing costs. He highlights capacity, obsolescence and debt risk, while recognising real demand and profitable established participants. He declines to make a definitive bubble call and favours selective exposure at prices supported by cash flows.

DECISION QUESTIONHow much future success is already required by the entry price and funding terms?

Reading qualification. Historical bubble comparisons express a risk framework. They supply no crash date, probability or price target.

6 November 2025 · Underwriting quality

Cockroaches in the Coal Mine

Benign conditions can hide weak underwriting until scrutiny rises. Marks uses First Brands and Tricolor to discuss how plentiful money, relaxed due diligence and incentives to deploy capital can leave weaknesses undiscovered. He treats defaults as part of sub-investment-grade lending and declines to infer a system-wide breakdown from these episodes. The First Brands discussion concerns allegations, opaque financing and receivables control. He describes credit research as an accumulated assessment of evidence and uncertainty. He also acknowledges that Oaktree has suffered defaults and frauds, which matters when reading its successful case study.

DECISION QUESTIONWhich independent evidence establishes asset ownership and the route of collections?

EXHIBIT 03 · POLICY & COUNTEREVIDENCE

Better liquidity is a real potential benefit

01Treasury operationBuy back older long-dated securities
02Trading effectGive dealers an outlet for inventory
03Investment testAssess the return required to hold long-dated bonds

The stated policy objective was liquidity support. Treasury’s 19 August announcement increased the maximum per-operation size in the 10–20-year and 20–30-year nominal sectors from US$2 billion to at least US$4 billion, effective 9 September through 4 November.

Dealer feedback supplies an important counterargument. Minutes of the 3 November meeting reported at least modest liquidity and market-functioning improvements for most dealers. Effects on handling client flows were mixed. The minutes were published on 4 November.

EXHIBIT 04 · ANALYSIS

The policy needs more than one scorecard

Three questions for the same policy
QuestionEvidence to seek
Is the market easier to trade?Bid–ask spreads, depth, dealer inventory and execution quality.
Is the long-term return adequate?Entry yield, inflation compensation and the value of waiting.
Has the debt burden changed?Net borrowing, refinancing dates and the interest bill.

Improved trading and persistent long-term yields can coexist. The curve alone cannot establish a buyback effect. Treasury also explains that new issuance replaces repurchased securities. A change in which securities investors hold can therefore occur without a corresponding reduction in net borrowing.

The portfolio implication depends on the holding period. An investor meeting dated cash needs can compare maturities with those dates. An investor seeking a capital gain needs a view on the yield at the future sale date, alongside a plan for an adverse move.

03 / credit

Income quality and exit terms need separate tests

Private credit combines a borrower, a contract and an investment vehicle. Looking through all three makes it easier to distinguish business deterioration from investor withdrawals. It also identifies the documents that can answer each concern.

EXHIBIT 05 · CASH-FLOW DIAGRAM

Follow the cash through three layers

01BorrowerPays interest from its business
02LoanSets collateral and enforcement rights
03FundSets distributions and repurchases

Regular distributions and an available exit have different funding needs. A fund may collect interest over time while an investor requests the return of principal now. The amount available to meet those requests depends on cash, new subscriptions, asset sales and the fund’s rules.

EXHIBIT 06 · REPORTED FUND TERMS

The exit window can be smaller than the holding

5%of fund NAV per quarter
Intended aggregate repurchase cap

The Fed reported this intended cap for most perpetual-life BDCs. Business development companies (BDCs) are business-lending investment vehicles. NAV means net asset value, broadly assets less liabilities. The cap concerns aggregate fund repurchases. Managers retain discretion over how much they accept.

The Fed’s stability assessment was specific to further redemption requests. It judged those risks manageable at publication. The May 2026 report used a general information cutoff of 23 April. That is a dated system assessment alongside the restrictions an individual investor faces.

EXHIBIT 07 · ANALYSIS

Ask for the evidence behind the reported number

A practical evidence checklist
LayerEvidence worth checkingWhat would weaken confidence
BorrowerCash collected, interest paid, customer retention and refinancing schedule.Income rises while collections weaken or interest is repeatedly capitalised.
LoanSigned security, collateral ownership, seniority and covenant terms.Another creditor has an earlier claim or the recovery estimate relies on unclear title.
FundRepurchase policy, accepted requests, cash buffer and concentration.Withdrawals rise while available cash falls and assets take longer to sell.

Independent confirmation helps test the lending story. For receivables-backed lending, reconcile the customer’s obligation, the borrower’s records and the lender’s claim. Trace where collections actually land. A document with a familiar name becomes more informative when the counterparty confirms it independently.

Each weakness suggests a different response. A timing mismatch calls for a liquidity plan. An uncertain collateral claim calls for legal and operational clarification. Deteriorating cash coverage calls for a new repayment assessment. Treating them separately makes a review more precise.

04 / ai

AI adoption leaves a cash-return test to pass

The investment question becomes clearer when growth, cash generation and valuation are examined separately. The following corporate example tests the timing of cash. It supplies a measurable exhibit alongside the more qualitative debate about technological change.

EXHIBIT 08 · ANALYSIS

A useful technology must pass three investment tests

01Useful technologyDoes it solve a problem people care about?
02Durable businessCan the provider collect cash after competition and costs?
03Attractive investmentDoes the price leave room for a satisfactory return?

These tests can produce different answers for the same company. A service can become widely used while competing suppliers reduce its price. A business can earn money while its shareholders receive a disappointing return because the purchase price already assumed stronger growth.

EXHIBIT 12 · ANALYSIS

Ownership and lending carry different cash claims

The same industry creates different financial claims
ClaimWhat the investor receivesWhat matters most
Equity ownershipCash left after operating needs, investment and prior claims.The price paid, competitive position and future cash available to owners.
LendingContractual interest and principal subject to repayment.Coverage, legal priority, maturity and recoverable collateral.
Leased infrastructureRent and any residual asset value under the agreement.Lease commitment, counterparty strength, useful life and renewal economics.

The practical next step is to read the claim you are buying. Identify what is owed, when it is owed, who pays and which conditions change the amount. Then ask what evidence supports the collateral value or earnings estimate at the date you would need to exit.

05 / funding

Match the currency and the payment dates

For a Singapore-based investor, the currency of future spending gives the analysis a concrete anchor. The same asset can have a different purpose when held for USD spending, SGD spending or repayment of a CHF loan. Keep each cash flow in its own currency until conversion is required.

EXHIBIT 13 · CASH-FLOW DIAGRAM

A funding plan determines the freedom to wait

01USD assetCoupons, distributions and sale proceeds
02CHF borrowingInterest reset and principal repayment
03SGD spendingThe currency and dates of household needs

The asset, loan and spending plan can react differently to the same market move. A bond may keep paying its stated coupon while its sale price changes. A revolving loan may reset earlier than the asset matures. Converting the proceeds adds another market price to the eventual cash result.

A workable holding period includes the dates when cash is required. List scheduled payments, possible collateral calls and the amount available without selling the investment. That schedule shows where the investor has flexibility and where a market move could create a forced decision.

EXHIBIT 15 · ANALYSIS & ACTION

Review the next cash date before increasing the position

Review the funding schedule alongside the investment thesis
Position or needDates and terms to alignEvidence that requires a fresh review
Bond holdingCoupons, maturity, intended sale and currency.A change in required yield or a cash need before maturity.
Private-credit fundDistributions, repurchase window and settlement.Weaker collections, changing NAV methods or reduced accepted withdrawals.
AI-related equityEntry valuation, cash reinvestment and investment horizon.Demand, competition or replacement spending changes the cash outlook.
CHF loanReset date, principal due, collateral and recognition terms.Funding cost, exchange rate or collateral requirements change.
SGD spendingPayment dates and liquid funds already held in SGD.Expected receipts arrive later or in a less favourable currency amount.

The useful output is a dated cash schedule and a documented investment thesis. Record which observations would support holding, adding, reducing or asking for further evidence. An explicit review condition makes it easier to update a view when the facts change, without needing to predict the exact market turning point.

The margin of safety has both a valuation and a financing dimension. An attractive entry price helps absorb forecast error. Adequate accessible cash helps the investor remain in control of the sale date. A position can satisfy one condition while leaving the other exposed.

06 / outlook

Use conditions that can change your mind

The central conclusion is a method for making a decision. Start with the cash claim, test the assumptions behind it and compare those assumptions with the price and funding plan. This supports a more selective review across assets.

EXHIBIT 16 · QUALITATIVE SCENARIOS

Set review conditions before the next headline

Qualitative monitoring conditions · analyst framework
AreaSupportive conditionsAdverse conditionsWhat to monitor
Long-duration bondsLower inflation compensation and credible fiscal funding plans.Higher required compensation or an earlier need to sell.Entry yield, inflation measures, auction demand and the holding-period cash plan.
Private creditResilient collections and refinancing on workable terms.Borrower stress combined with limited investor liquidity.Cash coverage, payment-in-kind interest (added to the loan balance), recoveries and accepted repurchases.
AI investmentsPaid usage and durable margins grow into the asset base.Capacity, competition or obsolescence outpaces monetisation.End-user payments, utilisation, replacement spending and free cash flow.
Multi-currency fundingReceipts arrive when the liability falls due.Currency moves or a loan reset erodes the available cash.Asset and debt currencies, reset dates and collateral headroom.

These conditions describe what would strengthen or weaken an investment case. They are a monitoring framework. The five memos provide qualitative judgments that require security-specific valuation and funding data before becoming a trade decision.

EXHIBIT 17 · COUNTERARGUMENTS

Give the opposing evidence a fair test

Counterarguments to keep in the review
Cautious observationThe strongest counterargumentWhat would settle the issue
Long yields remain elevated.Market functioning can improve even when yields stay high.Trading evidence alongside inflation, issuance and required-return data.
Repurchases can be restricted.Restrictions can protect a fund from forced sales at poor prices.Borrower performance, liquidity resources and actual investor requests.
Capital expenditure absorbs cash.Spending can create assets that produce attractive future returns.Paid demand, asset utilisation and cash returns over an adequate horizon.
Contract terms are complex.Well-designed conditions can allocate risks to capable counterparties.The actual contract, enforceability and counterparty capacity.

A balanced conclusion changes when the evidence changes. A strong business result, better contractual protection or a lower entry price can improve the expected return. A weaker cash result or an earlier funding obligation can reduce it. The discipline is to update those inputs explicitly.

Ask who pays, what price is justified and whether you can wait. Those questions connect the memo series to a portfolio review while leaving room for opportunity when the evidence, price and terms improve.
