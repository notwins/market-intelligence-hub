CHAPTER 01

Can the company pay you back?

Creditworthiness is about meeting obligations on time, including when the business disappoints.

A practical guide to company survival, bond recovery, default risk and credit spreads. Learn the method, work through Apple, then apply it to AI infrastructure financing.

The questionThe evidenceThe investor decision
Will it pay?Cash generation, liquidity, debt maturities and stress scenarios.Choose an issuer and a maturity you can defend.
What if it does not?Collateral, creditor priority, guarantees and enterprise value in distress.Estimate loss severity for the exact instrument.
Am I paid enough?Yield, benchmark spread, duration, liquidity and embedded options.Compare compensation against expected loss and uncertainty.

Three ideas to carry through the report

1. A profitable company can run out of cash. A low-margin company can survive if its cash flows and funding are resilient.
2. A strong company can issue a risky long-duration bond. Bond price losses do not require default.
3. A wide moat or attractive stock valuation is not a promise that a particular bond is safe.

CHAPTER 02

How credit analysis is built

There is no single universal creditworthiness formula.

LayerAsk thisEvidence that changes the answer
Business resilienceWill customers keep paying through a downturn?Revenue concentration; switching costs; pricing power; cyclicality; regulation.
Cash conversionDoes accounting profit become spendable cash?Operating cash flow; receivables; inventories; supplier terms; recurring vs one-off effects.
Debt burdenHow much has already been promised?Borrowings; leases; guarantees; pensions; purchase commitments; debt at subsidiaries.
Liquidity and timeCan the next payment be made without a friendly market?Unrestricted cash; committed facilities; covenant headroom; maturity calendar.
Financial policyWho receives surplus cash?Buybacks; dividends; acquisitions; willingness to preserve ratings or deleverage.
Instrument protectionWhere does this bond stand in a failure?Issuer entity; guarantors; collateral; seniority; covenants; governing law.

Analysts combine historical ratios with forecasts and judgment. Agency methods differ by sector and adjust accounting data differently. A bank, a property company and a software issuer cannot be ranked fairly using one unadjusted debt/EBITDA threshold.

Do not invent an agency grade. Record the actual agency, issuer or instrument, publication date, outlook and watch status. A rating outlook is not an additional notch. A rating is an opinion, not insurance.

Basis: independent teaching framework; rating-process distinction supported by Morningstar DBRS [5]. Credit ratings are forward-looking committee opinions, not mechanical scores.

CHAPTER 03

The six calculations worth learning

Ratios are questions to investigate, not pass/fail rules.

Metric / formulaWhat it tells youMain trap
Net debt = borrowings - eligible cashDebt after a realistic liquidity offset.Restricted cash, overseas taxes and securities haircuts matter. State whether leases are included.
Debt / EBITDADebt relative to operating earnings before D&A.Adjusted EBITDA can exclude recurring costs; EBITDA is not cash available for principal.
EBIT / gross interest expenseOperating earnings buffer over financing expense.Do not divide by net interest income. Capitalized interest and leases complicate coverage.
CFO - cash capexSimple cash left after investment in fixed assets.Not standardized FCF. Lease principal, acquisitions and shareholder payouts may be additional uses.

Build the bridge: cash from operations minus capex minus dividends minus buybacks minus acquisitions minus debt principal, plus new funding and asset sales. Track starting and ending cash separately.

For a simple liquidity forecast using CFO, do not subtract the same cash interest or working-capital outflow a second time. If starting from EBITDA instead, explicitly deduct interest, taxes, working-capital needs and capex.

Definitions here are classroom definitions, not agency-adjusted measures. There are no universal healthy cutoffs. Use consistent periods, currency, accounting basis and peer business models.

CHAPTER 04

Default risk: probability is not certainty

Expected loss combines the chance of default with the loss if default occurs.

Expected loss = probability of default (PD) x loss given default (LGD) x exposure.
If recovery is 40% of face value, LGD is 60%. For a $1,000 claim and a one-year PD of 2%, expected credit loss is $12. This is an average across uncertain outcomes, not a guaranteed $12 deduction.

One-year PD20% recovery40% recovery60% recovery
1%$8$6$4
2%$16$12$8
5%$40$30$20
10%$80$60$40

Illustrative sensitivity table. Exposure $1,000; expected loss = PD x (1 - recovery) x $1,000. No issuer estimate or historical default-rate forecast. Recoveries ignore discounting and expenses here.

Time matters. With a constant independent annual default probability of 2%, five-year cumulative default probability is 1 - 0.98^5 = 9.61%, not exactly 10%. Real conditional probabilities change with the economic cycle and the company.

A rating-based historical default rate, a model estimate and a market-implied probability are different objects. Historical averages depend on rating category, cohort, horizon and default definition. A market-implied probability embeds risk pricing and should not be presented as the actual chance of bankruptcy.

What counts as default? Missed payments, bankruptcy and some distressed exchanges can qualify, depending on the agency or contract. A covenant breach can cause a funding problem without being identical to a missed coupon.

Sources: corporate default basics [1]; rating opinions [5]. All numerical scenarios and formulas on this page are independently constructed teaching examples.

CHAPTER 05

Recovery: who gets paid first?

The value left for your claim matters more than the original asset price.

Recovery is the value received after default relative to the claim or other stated denominator. It may arrive as cash, new debt, equity or a mix, often after delay. A trading price soon after default and ultimate recovery are not the same measurement.

Illustrative waterfallClaimCash paidRecovery
Net distributable value after costs$60mAvailable poolNot book value
Secured creditors$30m$30m100%
Senior unsecured creditors$50m$30m60%
Subordinated creditors$20m$00%
Common equityResidual$00%

Stress the asset value. If the distributable pool falls to $40m, the same senior unsecured class receives $10m / $50m = 20%. If it rises to $80m, that class receives 100%. A bond label alone cannot determine recovery.

This simplified example assumes secured claims are covered by collateral, the remaining priority rules are as shown, and there are no competing administrative, employee, tax or other priority claims. Real insolvency outcomes depend on law, collateral, guarantees and the specific corporate structure.

Structural subordination: a parent-company bond may have access only to the residual value of a subsidiary after that subsidiary pays its own creditors. Consolidated cash does not necessarily belong to the bond issuer.

Illustration, not a predicted recovery or legal advice. General creditor-priority concepts: SEC Investor.gov [1]. For an actual bond, read the indenture and guarantee/security documents; do not use this stylized ordering as a legal opinion.

CHAPTER 06

Credit spread: separate the two prices

A bond yield can rise because Treasury yields rise, spreads widen, or both.

Simple benchmark spread = bond yield - comparable benchmark yield. One percentage point equals 100 basis points (bp). A 6.50% yield minus a 4.90% benchmark gives 1.60%, or 160bp. These rounded numbers are illustrative.

MeasureUseDo not confuse it with
CouponContractual interest as a percentage of face value.Yield earned at today's purchase price.
Yield to maturityDiscount rate equating price to promised cash flows under stated conventions.A guaranteed realized return if default, early sale or reinvestment differs.
Treasury / G-spreadYield difference to a specified Treasury or interpolated government curve.An option-adjusted spread or a same-day par-curve observation.
Z-spreadConstant spread added to spot rates to value promised cash flows.A direct standalone probability of default.
Option-adjusted spreadModel-based spread after accounting for embedded options.The raw spread of a callable bond.

Spread compensation reflects expected default losses, uncertainty around them, liquidity, risk appetite, supply-demand and bond-specific features. Tax and benchmark conventions can matter. Comparing a 30-year callable bond with a five-year noncallable bond by raw spread alone is misleading.

Useful shortcut, dangerous forecast: spread / LGD is a rough risk-neutral hazard-rate approximation under restrictive assumptions. At 160bp and 60% LGD, it gives about 2.67% per year. It is not a reliable physical default forecast and not a conclusion about Meta.

Sources: CFA Institute yield/spread concepts [6]. Numerical examples are independent teaching calculations; no live pricing or OAS estimates are supplied.

CHAPTER 07

A bond can lose money without default

Longer duration magnifies sensitivity to both interest rates and spreads.

First-order price change (%) is approximately -duration x yield change in percentage points. For a credit bond, use rate duration for benchmark changes and spread duration for spread changes. Duration is not the same as maturity.

Illustrative shock3-year duration7-year duration15-year duration
Yield +0.50 percentage point-1.5%-3.5%-7.5%
Yield +1.00 percentage point-3.0%-7.0%-15.0%
Yield -0.50 percentage point+1.5%+3.5%+7.5%

Hypothetical modified durations; no actual security. Approximation excludes convexity, coupon carry, default, accrued interest and transaction costs. Large moves require full repricing; callable bonds may behave differently.

Offsetting forces example: benchmark yield falls 50bp while credit spread widens 100bp. If rate duration and spread duration are both 7, approximate price change is +3.5% - 7.0% = -3.5%, before carry and convexity.

Holding a solvent bond to maturity can avoid realizing an interim market-price decline, but it does not remove default risk, inflation, opportunity cost or the risk that you need cash before maturity. A bond fund has a portfolio and no single promise to repay your original purchase price on a specified date.

Refinancing channel: a company with fixed-rate debt is not immediately paying more on every bond when yields rise. The higher cost reaches floating-rate debt, new borrowing and maturities that must be refinanced. This is why the maturity ladder matters.

Sources: yield and option considerations [6]; SEC bond basics [1]. All calculations are illustrative and independently constructed.

Practical Apple workflow
1. Open the requested Morningstar AAPL Financials page. Select annual rather than quarterly or TTM, confirm USD units, and note the fiscal end date.
2. Compare cash-flow, balance-sheet and income-statement numbers with the same Apple filing.
3. Read liquidity, debt, leases and commitments notes. Add the latest 10-Q/8-K events before making a current decision.
4. Read the exact bond prospectus and indenture. A stock page is not a bond term sheet.

Morningstar equity stars concern valuation relative to estimated fair value and uncertainty. An economic moat concerns durable competitive advantage. Morningstar DBRS credit ratings are a separate credit-opinion process. Do not convert stars or moat labels into default probabilities.

Calculate it yourself: 111.482 - 12.715 = $98.767bn. Cash capex consumed 11.41% of CFO. CFO / borrowings = 1.13x. These are unadjusted classroom ratios, not an agency score.

At 27 September 2025, cash and equivalents were $35.934bn. Adding current marketable securities gives $54.697bn; adding noncurrent securities gives $132.420bn. Borrowings were $98.657bn, including $7.979bn commercial paper and $12.350bn current term debt.

Interpretation: broad liquid investments exceeded borrowings by $33.763bn, but calling all securities immediate cash would overstate precision. Test availability, maturity, market risk and operating cash needs. The strong cash-generation profile does not establish a numerical default probability or make every Apple bond attractive.

CHAPTER 14

AI infrastructure: find the real obligor

Off-balance-sheet borrowing can still create economic exposure for the sponsor.

Funding routeWho borrows?What to examine
Corporate bondsOperating company or parent.Use of proceeds, maturity, new leverage and cash-flow coverage.
Leases / capacity contractsOwner or project vehicle; user promises future payments.Cancellation terms, minimum payments, commencement timing and guarantees.
Project / private creditSPV with pledged project assets and contracts.Customer concentration, construction risk, collateral obsolescence and lender priority.
Equity or joint ventureShareholders supply risk-bearing capital.Dilution, control, capital calls and whether debt has sponsor recourse.

BIS Bulletin 140, published 27 October 2026, describes growing debt funding and complex vehicles for AI infrastructure. Commitments by large firms can support vehicle credit quality, and banks may be exposed both to vehicles and to investors in their debt. Its observed Treasury and investment-grade spillovers on AI-financing news days were small and short-lived; that is not proof of no future risk. [7]

Stress the chain: weaker AI demand -> lower utilization -> less project cash -> pressure on fixed contractual payments -> refinancing or collateral-sale risk. A technologically successful sector can still contain overleveraged borrowers.

NVIDIA illustrates why models differ: FY2025, ended 26 January 2025, generated $64.089bn CFO versus $3.236bn purchases of PP&E and intangibles. Its fabless design model is not comparable with a data-center owner, and the period largely covers calendar 2024. Low own capex does not eliminate customer concentration or demand risk. [13]

R&D is an expense category; capex is investment in long-lived assets; acquisitions and lease commitments are different again. None is a clean, consistently disclosed total for AI spending. Do not add them blindly or call all infrastructure investment AI-only.

CHAPTER 16

Your repeatable survival checklist

Finish with a dated, conditional judgment, not a single reassuring ratio.

CheckRecord before deciding
Identity and timeExact legal issuer, guarantors, bond identifier, currency, financial period and pricing timestamp.
Cash engineThree-year CFO history; working-capital drivers; capex split; leases; recurring vs one-off items.
Funding calendarCash available today; committed facilities; debt due in each year; covenant headroom.
Downside caseRevenue/margin shock; cash conversion; refinancing rate; capex flexibility; cash after unavoidable payments.
Recovery casePriority, collateral value after costs, competing claims, guarantee coverage and recovery delay.
Price of riskComparable maturity and currency; spread definition; duration; call schedule; bid-ask and trading size.
Decision and triggerWhy compensation is sufficient or not; what new fact changes the conclusion; next filing date.

Example decision language: "On the stated historical data, the issuer has substantial cash-generation capacity. I would still need updated liquidity, its maturity schedule and this bond's spread and protections before judging whether the bond offers adequate compensation."

Red flags to investigate: persistent CFO weakness despite profit growth; rising receivables; large near-term maturities; aggressive EBITDA adjustments; expensive refinancing; weak covenant headroom; dividends funded with debt; guarantees or purchase commitments outside headline borrowings.
