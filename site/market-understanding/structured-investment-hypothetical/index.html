03 / READ THE TERMS

The term sheet supplies the contract

These examples deliberately stop short of defining an investable product. They have no issuer, actual quote, observation calendar, coupon conditions or settlement formula. Nothing here proves that a particular combination could be issued at the illustrated coupon.

Shares→KI→Strike→KO→Coupon→Full terms

Underlying shares

The shares named in the basket. Some real contracts use the weakest-performing share; others work differently. Confirm the actual payoff design.

Tenor & example date

Tenor is explicitly in months here. Example trade dates are invented labels, not evidence of a transaction, quote date or observation schedule.

Strike

A reference level used in the contract’s settlement mechanics. Shown as a percentage of a hypothetical initial reference level. It is not a guaranteed exit price.

Knock-in (KI)

A barrier, expressed as a percentage of the hypothetical initial reference level, that may activate downside mechanics. Level, observation style and exact consequences must all be defined by the issuer’s contract.

Knock-out (KO)

A threshold, expressed as a percentage of the hypothetical initial reference level, that may trigger early termination. A level alone does not specify which shares must meet it, when it is observed or what gets paid.

Coupon

Shown as an assumed annual rate for this illustration. Payment conditions, frequency, accrual, fees and early termination can change the amount actually received.

Rounding is a display choice, not protection

For invented inputs, 72.2% strike becomes 73%, 99.2% KO becomes 100%, 57.4% KI becomes 58%, and 5.9% coupon becomes 5%. Values already at a whole percentage point stay unchanged. Rounding a barrier can change contractual outcomes; do not use rounded examples for execution.
