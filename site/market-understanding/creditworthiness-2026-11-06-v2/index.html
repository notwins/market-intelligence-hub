01 · Cash first

Repayment depends on cash and timing, not profit headlines. Apple’s FY2026 operating cash flow less cash PP&E was US$136.056bn; it is not cash available after every obligation.

AI investment can outrun internally generated cash. Oracle’s FY2026 cash PP&E exceeded operating cash flow by US$23.686bn. Financing access and payment timing are therefore central.

03 · Read the claim

Creditors rank ahead of equity, but bondholders do not all rank equally. Collateral, issuer entity, guarantees and other priority claims change recovery.

A resilient issuer can still offer a poorly priced bond. Compare matched instruments and distinguish Treasury-rate, credit-spread, liquidity and FX risks.

Default is only one way a bond can disappoint

Creditworthiness is the capacity and willingness to pay the promised cash flows
RiskWhat can go wrongWhat to inspect
Issuer defaultInterest or principal is missed, or debt is restructured under distressed conditions.Cash generation, liquidity, payment schedule, covenant terms.
Interest ratesThe benchmark yield rises and the bond’s price falls even if repayment remains credible.Duration, maturity, coupon and holding horizon.
Credit spreadRequired compensation above the benchmark increases.Issuer news, peer spreads, supply, risk appetite, liquidity.
Trading liquidityA sale requires a discount or a wide bid-offer spread.Issue size, trade frequency, dealer quotes and settlement.
FX and optionalityCurrency moves reduce home-currency returns; calls change cash-flow timing.Currency exposure, hedges, call schedule, yield-to-worst.
ValuationYou pay too much for the risks and contractual cash flows.Comparable instruments, market price, fees and alternatives.

Default risk versus loss

Default probability and loss severity are different. For a common horizon, expected credit loss = exposure at default × probability of default × loss given default. A legal recovery paid years later also has a time value.

Ratings are opinions, not insurance

A credit rating is not a price target or a guarantee. Agency methods, incentives, timing and issue-specific protections matter. Ratings do not remove market-price or liquidity risk. This report omits unverified issuer ratings.

In everyday terms
The borrower can keep paying while your bond’s screen price falls. A promise being honoured and a price staying still are not the same thing.

No single ratio calculates “survivability”

A defensible probability of default requires a calibrated model. A mechanical score is a screening device, not a certified rating.

The legal claim can matter as much as the company

General teaching framework; actual rights depend on the instrument and applicable law
Legal entityWho owes the money?
→
CollateralWhich assets support it?
→
PriorityWhich claims rank ahead?
→
CovenantsWhat can change or accelerate?
StructurePlain-English meaningWhat to check
Secured debtRights over specified collateral, rather than a general promise alone.Collateral ownership, valuation, lien priority, other liens, enforcement costs and shortfall treatment.
Senior unsecured debtA general claim without specified collateral; senior within the contractual ranking stated.Other secured or priority claims, guarantees and the legal issuer. Senior does not mean no losses.
Subordinated debtContractually ranks behind defined senior claims.Exactly which claims rank ahead, payment-blocking provisions and any deferral or conversion terms.
Parent versus subsidiaryCreditors of operating subsidiaries can be structurally ahead of a parent-only bond.Which entity owns the assets and earns the cash; upstream dividend restrictions and guarantees.
EquityResidual ownership after creditor claims in the simple waterfall.Equity value is not the same as recoverable assets available to bondholders.

Covenants are contractual promises and restrictions

Read the definition, not just the headline ratio. Covenants can require reporting, restrict liens or asset transfers, and impose financial tests. Cure periods, exceptions, waivers and acceleration rights matter. A covenant breach is not automatically the same event as a missed cash payment. Oracle’s defined coverage test, for example, is not the simple EBIT/gross-interest ratio in this report.

A guarantee can change where a bondholder has a claim. A famous group name is not a substitute for checking the issuer, guarantor, ranking and enforceable documents. Do not apply a single simplified priority diagram to every jurisdiction or capital structure.

In everyday terms
Two people can lend to the same group and hold different promises. One may have security over an asset; another may rely on whatever reaches the parent after other creditors are paid.

What the formulas say

Op. margin = operating income / revenue
Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities
Surplus / debt = (OCF − cash investment) / year-end book borrowing
Coverage = operating income / gross interest expense

What the formulas do not say

Flow-to-stock ratios are not repayment forecasts. A 1.00× surplus/debt ratio does not promise repayment in one year. Existing commitments, business cyclicality, the location of cash and refinancing dates remain material. Negative net debt means cash exceeds this debt definition, not zero risk.

The strongest signals here are cash generation and available balance-sheet liquidity. AI credit risk follows contracts, not just company logos

BIS Bulletin 140 · 27 October 2026 · official abstract only
DemandCloud / model customers
→
CommitmentsCapacity, leases, guarantees
→
VehiclesAsset owners and projects
→
FundingBonds, banks, investors

The risk case

A growing share of AI infrastructure is debt-financed. The BIS abstract describes long-term borrowing and off-balance-sheet structures, with commitments from a few large firms supporting vehicle debt and bank exposures. The economic burden can extend beyond the issuer’s simple bond balance.

Evidence against a blanket crisis claim

The verified abstract does not establish Treasury funding stress. It reports small and short-lived US Treasury and investment-grade spillovers on AI-financing news days in 2026, while warning that sustained issuance could change the outcome.

Question to askEvidence to request
Who must pay if demand disappoints?Take-or-pay obligations, lease terms, termination rights, guarantees and customer credit.
Is the debt truly non-recourse?Legal borrower, guarantors, collateral, support agreements and contingent obligations.
Can assets be redeployed?Alternative tenants, power connection, equipment age, resale cost and technological obsolescence.
Who funds a delay?Committed liquidity, sponsor support, construction contingency and refinancing assumptions.

This is not a reproduction of the full BIS study. The full PDF and annex could not be retrieved. No event-count, issuance-share, basis-point estimate or chart from inaccessible material is included. The bulletin reflects its authors’ analysis, not a guarantee or a policy forecast.

In everyday terms
A lease promise can help someone else borrow. The debt may sit in another company, but the promise can still bring the bill back to the business you are analysing.

A wider spread is meaningful only after the comparison is controlled

A bond’s yield, its credit spread and its default probability are different quantities
Benchmark yieldBase curve for the currency
→
+ SpreadCredit, liquidity and risk pricing

Bond yieldWith options and convention caveats
Match or explainWhy it changes the comparison
Currency and benchmarkUSD Treasury, swap and other currency curves are not interchangeable. A Treasury par yield is not every individual benchmark bond.
Date, timestamp and quote sideDifferent sessions, bid/ask prices, accrued interest or settlement assumptions create artificial differences.
Tenor and rate / spread durationA long bond typically carries different sensitivity and future uncertainty from a short bond.
Issuer entity, rank and collateralParent, subsidiary, guaranteed, secured, unsecured and subordinated claims can have different expected losses.
Embedded options and covenantsCallable, puttable, perpetual and reset securities require the relevant valuation convention, often model-dependent OAS.
Liquidity, issue size and technical supplyNew-issue concessions, trading activity and investor demand affect spreads without an equivalent change in default risk.

Simple illustration, not an issuer estimate

Spread = 200bp = 0.02
Assumed recovery = 40%; LGD = 60%
Hazard ≈ 0.02 / 0.60 = 3.33% per year
5-year model PD = 1 − exp(−0.033333 × 5)
= 15.35%

Why that is not a default forecast

This is a simplified risk-neutral model calculation. It assumes constant hazard and recovery conventions and ignores liquidity and other premia. A physical probability of default requires further modelling and calibration. The same observed spread can support different interpretations.

In everyday terms
Comparing bond spreads without matching the instruments is like comparing insurance premiums without checking what is insured or the deductible.

A solvent issuer can still produce a negative bond return

Hypothetical examples separate cash repayment, market price and home-currency returns

Rates: price falls without a default

A five-year 5% annual-coupon bond priced at a 5% yield is worth 100. At an instantaneous 6% yield, the same contractual cash flows are worth 95.7876, a 4.21% price loss. No time passage, coupon receipt or default is assumed.

+ 100 / (1+y)^5

A 5% USD return is only 1.11% in SGD in this example. Start FX = 1.3500 SGD per USD; end FX = 1.3000 SGD per USD. These are invented teaching inputs, not current market quotations.

SGD return = (1 + 5%) × 1.3000 / 1.3500 − 1
= 1.1111%

Duration is a sensitivity, not a credit rating

Separate example: modified duration = 7 years
Yield rises 100bp: first-order ΔP/P ≈ −7 × 0.01 = −7%

The approximation ignores convexity and assumes a small parallel yield move. The seven-year duration is a separate input, not the measured duration of the five-year bond above. For a corporate bond, benchmark yields and spreads can move in different directions.

HYPOTHETICAL + FORMULA AUDIT

The corrected waterfall pays both claims in full at US$100

Simple single-pool illustration; real legal structures and recovery timing can change outcomes
ScenarioGross assetsCostsDistributableSenior paid / 80Junior paid / 20Equity
Full payment$100$0$100$80 / 100%$20 / 100%$0
Costs first$100$10$90$80 / 100%$10 / 50%$0
Asset loss + costs$70$10$60$60 / 75%$0 / 0%$0
Value above debt$120$0$120$80 / 100%$20 / 100%$20
A = max(gross realized assets − paid costs, 0)
Senior paid = min(senior claim, A)
Junior paid = min(junior claim, max(A − senior paid, 0))
Equity residual = max(A − senior paid − junior paid, 0)
Recovery rate = amount paid / claim; LGD = 1 − recovery rate

If US$100 is already distributable, costs have already been dealt with. Do not subtract them again. In the first row, 100 = 80 + 20 and both creditors recover their entire claims. In the second, 100 = 10 costs + 80 senior + 10 junior. All rows conserve value and cap payments at the claim.

How analysts estimate the pool

Book assets are not liquidation proceeds. A going-concern valuation may use stressed cash generation and a valuation multiple, or an asset sale may use realizable cash values. Deduct costs and claims at the correct legal entity, then allocate by enforceable priorities.

Why actual recovery varies

Collateral and seniority are not universal shortcuts. Jurisdiction, administration costs, liens, guarantees, subsidiary claims, accrued interest and timing matter. Senior unsecured bonds can still lose money. Equity ranks last in this simple model.

In everyday terms
There is no junior loss when the available pot exactly covers both debts. The loss begins only when costs or a smaller pot leave less than the total claims.

ANALYSIS + PRIMARY EVIDENCE

Use three separate decisions before buying a bond

Issuer resilience → bond protection → price and portfolio suitability

1 · Can the issuer pay?

Test sustainable cash generation under stress. Read business risks, cash conversion, leases, commitments, debt maturities and committed liquidity.

2 · What does this bond own?

Identify the legal claim. Issuer entity, guarantees, collateral, ranking, covenants, call terms, currency and settlement all matter.

3 · Is the price worthwhile?

Compare matched risks and your holding needs. Check executable price, YTM/YTW, duration, OAS where appropriate, fees, FX and concentration.
