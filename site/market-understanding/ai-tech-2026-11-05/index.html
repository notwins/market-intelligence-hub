01 My central test

I look for an established business that can fund investment, survive competition and turn growth into shareholder cash. The largest announced AI budget is not itself an investment merit.

Microsoft offers several established revenue engines. Alphabet combines Search, YouTube and Cloud, but advertising and investment payback remain central. TSMC, ASML and KLA provide different capabilities within a shared semiconductor spending cycle. Meta adds another company, but limited diversification of the advertising driver.

Business quality does not settle the purchase price. This public edition retains the operating analysis and issuer financials, while omitting the third-party valuation/rating dataset and the chart derived from it. It therefore does not present a current buy-price ranking. External research stays with its publisher.

03 What changed after late 2022

The AI buildout transformed the scale of selected suppliers. Each comparison uses a dated 2022-era baseline and the latest reported annual period. These periods differ in length and fiscal label. The bars show cumulative revenue change, not stock returns and not revenue caused solely by AI.

The first wave rewarded scarce compute and supporting infrastructure. Accelerators, high-bandwidth memory, advanced manufacturing and networking expanded as cloud platforms built capacity. New product launches and customers appear in the company timelines. Revenue growth alone does not establish how much of the change was caused by AI.

The next investment question is cash payback. Cloud and platform companies now have much larger infrastructure commitments. Model efficiency can lower the cost per task and expand usage, but it can also pressure pricing. Software vendors face both an opportunity to charge for useful automation and a threat to established seats and workflows.

04 Follow the demand dependencies

Many companies sell into the same infrastructure budget. A GPU supplier, foundry, memory maker, switch vendor and cooling provider can all grow from one hyperscaler order. Adding their reported revenue would double-count economic activity along the chain.

The categories describe economic roles, with important overlaps. Intel designs and manufactures chips; it is not fabless. Hon Hai assembles electronics and servers; it is not a silicon-wafer foundry. Arm’s 2026 direct-silicon strategy extends beyond its historical licensing-only description.

Revenue breadth needs a profit check. Meta has several apps but one dominant advertising engine. Amazon has broad sales while AWS contributes disproportionately to operating profit. Samsung’s device breadth did not prevent semiconductors from producing nearly all Q3 operating profit.

05 R&D is not total AI investment

R&D is a corporate expense, not an AI-only spending disclosure. It includes research across established and emerging products. Amazon’s broader technology-and-infrastructure expense cannot be substituted for pure R&D, and omitting Amazon would distort an infrastructure-spending discussion.

Cash capex purchases long-lived assets. The core cash screen here is operating cash flow less the stated cash capital expenditure line. Company-defined free cash flow is shown separately when it adjusts for leases, incentives, financing receivables, intangible assets or other items.

Investment commitments are future obligations or intentions. They are neither current revenue nor current-year cash spending. Noncash lease additions, cash lease principal and outright equipment purchases must remain separate. Adding every press-release investment headline would double-count parts of the same supply chain.

07 Cash generation versus reinvestment

The annual cash screen shows who can fund investment internally. OCF less the explicitly defined cash-capex line is useful, but it is not a fully harmonized measure of distributable cash. Leases, incentives, intangible assets and financing operations require separate treatment.

Historical annual surpluses can coexist with current cash deficits. Alphabet, Amazon and Meta entered the latest infrastructure ramp with strong operating businesses. Their latest cash disclosures show how quickly reinvestment can absorb those surpluses. Microsoft’s cash capex and lease-inclusive capex are different measures.

Working capital is especially important in hardware. Supermicro’s audited FY2026 net income was positive USD 2,230.453m while OCF was negative USD 6,809.886m. Inventory absorbed USD 8,876.747m of cash. Growth needs both margins and cash conversion.

11 Financing links and correlated risk

Broadcom’s October arrangement adds a credit and collateral dimension. The announced USD 63bn financing supports an SPV that buys equipment and leases it to Anthropic. Broadcom provides residual-value support equivalent to the first-lien portion; this is not a USD 63bn ordinary loan on Broadcom’s balance sheet.

The adverse scenario is correlated. Weaker AI returns could reduce new orders, weaken the lessee’s cash resources and lower used-equipment prices simultaneously. The risk is not captured by looking only at current semiconductor margins.

Marvell has a different special risk: a pending major transaction. The 2 November Lumentum agreement implies USD 115bn enterprise value and is expected to close in the first half of 2028, subject to approvals. Transaction completion, financing and integration remain separate uncertainties.

12 Risk scenarios and buying discipline

The useful stress test follows the source of earnings. These are qualitative scenarios, not probability-weighted price forecasts. They help decide which evidence to watch and which risks are already duplicated across holdings.

Use staged purchases only while the evidence remains intact. A lower price is attractive when normalized earnings power is preserved. It is not automatically attractive when the business model, balance sheet or cash-payback case has deteriorated.

For a Singapore investor, keep currency exposure explicit. SGD total return = (1 + foreign-currency total return) × (ending SGD per unit of foreign currency / starting SGD per unit) − 1, before fees and taxes. Listing currency, financial reporting currency and underlying revenue exposure are different.

The action order is business, value, then portfolio fit. Check existing overlap, refresh the quote and stale models, inspect the next results for the stated triggers, then decide whether the added exposure improves the portfolio. No allocation weights or leverage assumptions are made here.

15 Methodology, definitions and sources

Cutoff: 5 November 2026, Singapore time. Financial results released by the cutoff are used. US releases scheduled after that day’s market close fall on 6 November Singapore time and are outside this report.

Fiscal periods: the latest reported annual result is not necessarily the latest fiscal year that has ended. HPE’s FY2026 had ended but was not yet reported. NVIDIA’s FY2026 ended in January; Apple’s and Micron’s ended in September. Comparisons label each period.

Currency and precision: annual monetary inputs preserve source values in native-currency millions. No exchange rate or common-currency ranking is assumed. Ratios use exact numerator/denominator inputs; charts visualize their relationship without creating more source precision. A source-rounded billion or trillion figure remains labelled as such.

Cash-flow definitions: simple FCF = OCF − the stated cash-capex line. This is not fully harmonized owner earnings. NVIDIA and Arm have additional financing/intangible deductions; Micron uses incentives in adjusted capex; IBM excludes financing-receivable movements; Equinix uses a broader investing-flow definition. Intel’s cash PP&E line excludes financing PP&E payments.

Accounting basis: TSMC’s primary profit row uses Taiwan-IFRS parent earnings, with the different SEC IFRS result disclosed separately. ASML uses US GAAP. Some companies’ net income includes noncontrolling interests; the source definition is retained. Oracle’s derived gross profit precedes separately classified intangible amortization.

Remaining data limits: some debt balances were not captured, including Arm, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron and Hon Hai; Arista has no conventional borrowing line in the retrieved audited balance sheet, without implying every lease obligation is zero. MediaTek Q3 revenue is derived from official monthly data because the listed quarterly PDF was inaccessible. No missing input is assumed zero. R&D and AI-revenue definitions are not standardized across issuers.

Colour and labels: the current Colour & Tagging Standard V1.2 Merged pair was verified. Existing company mappings are preserved, with unmapped issuers in ordinary text. Reported facts, management forecasts and analytical judgments are identified by wording and labels; colour does not encode an unverified rating.

Decision scope: this is a stock-selection screen for the stated preference for established core businesses and revenue resilience. Existing holdings, loss tolerance, investment horizon and SGD cash needs are not supplied, so portfolio weights, leverage and personalized return targets are not assumed.
