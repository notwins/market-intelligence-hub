01
THE US CLOSE

How the rally reversed

The divergence started during the rally

On 4 November the S&P 500 gained 1.6% as the election result reduced uncertainty, yet the Philadelphia chip index fell 3.1%. The next day the S&P 500 closed at 7,353.73 and all 11 sectors declined. The market had already begun to distinguish equipment suppliers from their customers before the broad selloff.

Sources   [1] Reuters · [2] Reuters · [3] Reuters via CNA
ANALYSIS

Why the election mattered to investors

Democrats won both chambers of Congress. Local votes and opposition to data-centre projects raised questions about permitting, power and water. A longer approval process can push a chip order into a later quarter. The size of that delay remains unknown.

Amazon shows why investors weighed both sides

AWS sales grew 39.3% year on year in the September quarter. At group level, Amazon reported a US$42.4 billion free cash outflow over the trailing twelve months. Microsoft reported 48% year-on-year growth in Azure revenue in its September quarter. Sales growth and cash available after investment answer different questions.

Sources   [9] Amazon · [10] Microsoft
ANALYSIS

Who was selling?

Managers interviewed by Reuters described institutional selling and hedge funds reducing leverage. Qualcomm’s smartphone-chip sales missed estimates, while Arm guided next-quarter revenue slightly above consensus. Positioning helps explain why selling spread across companies with different earnings news. The interviews give no seller-by-seller flow totals.

03
CROSS-ASSET MARKETS

Treasury yields fell fastest at the front end

Treasuries, gold and the Swiss franc provided three different price signals. They were observed at different times and reveal no dollar amount transferred from equities.

ANALYSIS

The curve bull-steepened

The 10-year minus 2-year spread widened by 6 bp. On 4 November, 4.90% − 4.58% = 0.32 percentage points, or 32 bp. On 5 November, 4.84% − 4.46% = 0.38 percentage points, or 38 bp. Both yields fell, with the short yield falling faster. One basis point equals 0.01 percentage point.

Sources   [11] US Treasury

Gold gained, then gave back part of its rise

Spot gold reached US$4,146.63 per troy ounce during 5 November trading. At 2:05 pm New York time Reuters put it at US$4,100.27, up 1% on the day. The high and the later quote describe two different moments.

Sources   [12] Reuters

Yes, CHF strengthened

In early Asian trading on 6 November, CHF gained 0.2% against USD. The yen also gained 0.2% before giving back part of its move. The US dollar index was roughly flat at 101.55. For someone funding USD assets in francs, a stronger CHF increases the debt measured in USD.

Sources   [13] Reuters
04
SECTORS AND SINGAPORE

Singapore’s link runs through the chip supply chain

The sector spread shows how differently businesses traded on the same day. For a Singapore holding, its customers and order book are more informative than the exchange where its shares trade.

The regional exposure is tangible

In Asian trading on 6 November, Reuters reported Samsung Electronics down nearly 10% and SK Hynix down 12% during the session. In Singapore, Micron broke ground in January on a NAND flash-memory fab, with about US$24 billion planned over ten years and wafer output scheduled for the second half of 2028.

Sources   [4] Reuters · [14] Micron
ANALYSIS

What to check in a Singapore holding

Start with the company’s customers. A supplier of specialised parts may see orders move if a fab delays equipment deliveries. A Singapore bank would need a different transmission route, such as borrower or collateral exposure. A manufacturer’s investment plan alone gives no estimate of the STI’s sensitivity.

05
PORTFOLIO REVIEW

Review the same risk across shares, notes and loans

An investor can hold a chip company directly, own it again through a fund and write downside exposure on it through a structured note. Each position responds differently to the same fall.

01 · Find repeated holdings

List direct shares, look through fund holdings and record each structured note’s underlyings. Keep funded equity exposure and contingent note exposure in separate columns. Summing all note notionals as if they were shares would overstate or understate the immediate price sensitivity.

02 · Locate the weakest share in each fixed coupon note

For a worst-of basket, divide each share’s spot price by its own initial fixing and rank the results. Then check the barrier, observation method, strike and maturity in the term sheet. If the share is above the barrier, the fall from today’s price to the barrier equals 1 − (barrier price ÷ current price). A prior knock-in can still lead to par redemption under some terms.

Sources   [15] Standard Chartered Singapore

03 · Match Treasury duration to the cash need

A STRIP makes one payment at maturity and pays no interim coupon. A holder needing cash earlier can compare the current bid on the actual STRIP with the proceeds and reinvestment yield available at a shorter maturity. Shortening duration reduces sensitivity to a renewed rise in long yields but introduces earlier reinvestment risk.

Sources   [11] US Treasury · [16] TreasuryDirect
ANALYSIS

04 · A CHF loan can magnify a USD drawdown

With USD/CHF quoted as francs per dollar, USD value of CHF debt = CHF principal ÷ USD/CHF. A lower USD/CHF raises the USD liability. If pledged US stocks fall at the same time, collateral also shrinks. Check the facility’s recognised collateral values, loan balance, liquidity and margin threshold. For SGD borrowing, run the comparison using USD/SGD.

Sources   [13] Reuters · [17] DBS Singapore

Next evidence to watch

Compare data-centre approvals with actual equipment orders, cloud revenue with cash capex, and the returns of suppliers with those of buyers. Order deferrals and cancellations have different implications for future sales.
