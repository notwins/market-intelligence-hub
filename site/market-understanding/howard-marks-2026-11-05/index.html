Suppose Marks is right that AI changes the economy. Who gets paid? A software customer might save money, a chip buyer might overspend, and a lender might collect every coupon until its borrower needs to refinance. Believing in the technology tells us little about which of those positions to own. The same question runs through his recent writing on government bonds and private loans.

  2. The price

  3. The impact

MEMO 01 / 22 SEPTEMBER 202603 / 18

What can a Treasury buyback change?

Marks asks whether buying long bonds can durably lower the interest rate investors demand.

US Treasury DepartmentUS TreasuriesFiscal deficitLiquidity

The Treasury Department and Marks are asking different questions. On 19 August, Treasury announced larger buybacks in the 10–20 and 20–30 year nominal sectors. The maximum per operation would rise from US$2 billion to at least US$4 billion, effective 9 September for the rest of that refunding quarter. Treasury gave liquidity support as its objective. Marks interpreted the intervention as an effort to bring down long yields.

  1. Policy action
    Treasury bids for older bonds.
  2. Market mechanism
    A dealer gains a buyer for inventory.
  3. Investor impact
    The long yield still reflects the terms buyers require.

A bond can become easier to sell while the country’s long-term borrowing cost stays high. Liquidity concerns the ease of trading. A long yield also reflects expected short rates, inflation and compensation for bearing duration. Treasury can help a dealer clear a position today while investors still demand a sizeable return to hold decades of fixed payments.

Marks’s deeper concern is about the maturity of the liability. If more bills are issued alongside the purchase of long debt, a portion of government funding must reset sooner. Lower interest expense today then has to be weighed against the future refinancing bill. His proposed remedies address the fiscal balance through revenue, spending discipline and productivity growth.

A point of disagreement

A November Treasury advisory presentation found no clear evidence that AI-related issuance was crowding out Treasuries or non-tech credit. Marks outlined a plausible competition for capital. The advisory presenter’s reading of the market gives a specific reason to keep that mechanism open to revision.

The interest bill Marks anticipated

The completed fiscal year gives us one part of the September memo we can check directly.

Marks expected annual net interest outlays to exceed US$1 trillion and the defense budget. The fiscal year ended on 30 September. The yearly financing gap was US$1,992.47 billion on the same presentation. US$7,395.80 billion of net outlays less US$5,403.33 billion of net receipts gives US$1,992.47 billion. Net interest is already inside outlays. Gross Treasury issuance also refinances maturing debt, so its total answers a different question.

A growing interest bill works its way through the budget over time. A higher auction yield affects newly issued or refinanced debt first. The cost of previously issued fixed-rate bonds stays contractual. That lag explains how pressure can build without immediately appearing across the entire debt stock.

What if the real concern is the value of the dollars received? When a friend asked whether he should sell his US stocks, Marks asked where the proceeds would go. Moving into a USD deposit changes the exposure to share prices but leaves the spending power of those dollars at issue. He also recalls that his own concerns about lasting dollar weakness after the 2008 crisis did not play out as feared. The size of a balance sheet alone gives little guidance about currency timing.

Portfolio implication

The budget result confirms a fiscal cost, while the next move in long yields remains open. A buyer of a long Treasury needs to judge the yield on offer and the consequences of being early. Recession or falling inflation expectations can still support long bond prices while the deficit remains large.

MEMO 02 / 09 APRIL 202606 / 18

What a smooth loan valuation can hide

Marks follows the money from private equity sponsors to lenders and then to the investors seeking their cash back.

Private creditAILiquidity

The first direct lenders had bargaining power. Private equity sponsors needed acquisition financing after the global financial crisis. Lenders could negotiate both the spread and the conditions under which they could intervene. As more managers competed for deals, borrowers could shop for a cheaper loan or fewer restrictions. A record earned on the early loans says little about the protections in the next one.

The Sharpe ratio rewards a quiet valuation history. It divides excess return by the volatility of returns. An infrequently priced loan can show little movement even as its borrower loses customers. For a software borrower exposed to AI competition, ask when customers can cancel or renegotiate, then compare the cash left after operating costs with the interest due.

The structure changes what an investor sees when confidence falls. The owner of a listed BDC can sell shares to another investor, potentially at a discount to net asset value. In a non-traded BDC, the investor may instead face a limit on how many shares the vehicle will buy back. A wider listed discount or a limited repurchase can appear before a borrower misses a payment.

  1. Borrower
    Customer renewals support cash interest payments.
  2. Lender
    Covenants determine when a lender can intervene.
  3. Fund investor
    Repurchase terms determine when an investor can exit.
Portfolio implication

A quarterly exit window still has a capacity limit. A non-traded business development company (BDC) may accept only part of a repurchase request. An investor with a dated cash obligation needs the prospectus terms as well as the loan performance report.

The Federal Reserve’s May 2026 report measures two fund structures that give individuals access to private credit.

In 2026 Q1, accepted repurchases exceeded new subscriptions for the first time in the Fed’s series. That finding covered the 14 largest perpetual BDCs, representing 86% of segment assets. Most perpetual BDCs disclosed an intended quarterly cap of 5% of net asset value, subject to manager discretion. Repurchases count accepted withdrawals. Investor requests can be higher.

A persistent outflow could reach borrowers through fewer new loans. A fund can use cash, collect principal, draw on a bank line or sell liquid assets to meet withdrawals. Keeping more resources available for investors can leave less money for a borrower coming back to refinance. The Fed judged the financial stability risks from further requests manageable at the time.

MEMO 05 / 06 NOVEMBER 202508 / 18

Who else has a claim on the same invoice?

First Brands and Tricolor gave Marks a reason to question how lenders verify the collateral behind a loan.

Private creditCredit risk

Two bankruptcies raised a question about how widely the same weakness might recur. First Brands supplied auto parts and had both public and private debt. Tricolor sold used vehicles and financed subprime buyers. When Marks wrote, allegations included the repeated pledging of First Brands receivables. He saw grounds to revisit underwriting across portfolios. His distinction was between losses clustering after loose lending and one failure transmitting losses through contractual links to others. Two cases can justify wider checks while leaving that second mechanism unproven.

The subsequent First Brands indictment made the alleged mechanism more specific. On 29 January 2026, US prosecutors alleged that executives used fictitious or inflated invoices and pledged assets more than once. Marks had already contrasted US$5.9 billion of debt disclosed in July with US$9.3 billion of debt included in US$11.6 billion of total obligations reported in the bankruptcy process. A lender needs the complete list of claims to estimate what reaches its account. The January allegations are later evidence and were unavailable when Marks published the memo.

  1. Invoice
    The receivable must exist.
  2. Ownership
    The lender must have a valid claim.
  3. Collection
    The customer’s payment must reach the lender.

Three checks a credit committee can request

QuestionEvidence to inspect
QuestionDoes the receivable exist?Evidence to inspectConfirm selected invoices and balances directly with customers.
QuestionWho has the prior claim?Evidence to inspectReconcile assigned invoices across lenders and factors, then check security filings.
QuestionWhere did cash arrive?Evidence to inspectTrace customer remittances to bank statements and the controlled collection account.
What would change the conclusion?

The same failure at unrelated borrowers would make the pattern more persuasive. Compare lenders, collateral agents and servicing platforms across cases. Shared controls can create a concentration that an industry allocation report misses.

AI LETTERS / 9 DECEMBER 202509 / 18

Who keeps the value AI creates?

In Is It a Bubble?, Marks separates the economics of the buildout from the prices investors pay to join it.

AIEquitiesPrivate creditData centres

There are two ways to be early and still lose money. An operator can build more capacity than it can profitably rent. An investor can buy a successful operator at a price that assumes even better results. The second mistake remains possible if the first never happens. Marks uses railways and communications infrastructure to show how useful assets can survive the loss of their original investors’ capital.

  1. A real advance
    Computing gets cheaper or more capable.
  2. The customer wins
    Users may capture the savings.
  3. The investor’s test
    Can this particular owner retain enough profit?

Marks asks, “Who will be the winners, and what will they be worth?” A shareholder in the eventual winner participates in its growth. A lender who identifies exactly the same winner generally collects the contractual coupon and principal. A spectacular winner in a loan book therefore offers limited help against a failure elsewhere. In assessing a loan, the surviving cash flows and collateral matter more than the size of the industry’s prize.

The strongest case for optimism deserves a place in the analysis. The presence of customers and profitable companies financing investment from operations distinguishes parts of this buildout from ventures living entirely on external capital. The harder question concerns the next project, especially if the tenant needs another financing round to honour its commitment.

What changes the decision

For a data centre, ask who pays the rent if computing prices fall faster than its costs. A tenant with a binding commitment and the capacity to honour it offers different protection from a long demand forecast. The contract, counterparty and price paid determine how much that difference is worth.

AI LETTERS / 26 FEBRUARY 202610 / 18

What changed his mind about AI?

In AI Hurtles Ahead, stronger evidence of capability changes Marks’s view of the technology while leaving the valuation question open.

AISoftwareAI capexEquities

Marks asked Claude for a tutorial and then challenged its conclusion. The account he published draws on a 10,000-word essay from the model, prepared using prompts developed with advisers. He saw a meaningful difference between a system that answers a question and one that breaks down a task, uses tools and checks its work. He also describes its errors and the difficulty of relying on patterns when a situation has no close precedent.

The distinction between training and inference changed the investment question. Training creates the model. Inference runs it when a customer makes a request. Heavy use today provides evidence of demand for inference capacity. When Marks pressed the argument, the missing variable was future supply. A queue today can disappear after multiple operators finish building at the same time.

  1. Observed
    Customers use the tools today.
  2. Unresolved
    Rivals can add capacity together.
  3. Impact
    Future utilisation and margins determine what owners earn.
What the observation supportsWhat the investor still needs
What the observation supportsA tool performs a valuable task.What the investor still needsWho pays, how often and at what price?
What the observation supportsCustomers use today’s available capacity.What the investor still needsHow much is being built, and when will it open?
What the observation supportsA model can compare public data quickly.What the investor still needsDoes the resulting judgment retain value once competitors can make the same comparison?

For an investor, the tutorial is also a lesson in when to stop delegating. A model can extract debt covenants and compare filings. Someone still has to decide whether a sponsor will support a borrower in distress, and take responsibility for the capital placed at risk. Marks also raises the prospect of dislocated work and lost purpose. He offers these as social concerns rather than measurable labour-market forecasts.

What changed

His confidence in AI’s capabilities rose, but the tutorial supplied no valuation of the securities. A revision to the operating thesis warrants a fresh look at price, remaining uncertainties and position size.

EVIDENCE 05 / META HYPERION12 / 18

Three clocks inside one data centre

Meta’s Hyperion joint venture shows why a lease, a renewal option and a guarantee deserve separate lines in a credit review.

MetaData centresPrivate credit

A small equity share can sit beside a substantial contractual obligation. Meta disclosed a 20% membership interest, with 80% held by an affiliate of Blue Owl-managed funds. It also disclosed an initial lease commitment of approximately US$12.3 billion. The four-year lease includes renewal options that could extend occupancy to 20 years in total.

The residual-value protection has a trigger, a cap and a declining threshold. For the first 16 years of operations, specified conditions following non-renewal or termination can trigger a payment if the property’s value falls below the agreed threshold. The obligation differs from a promise to make every payment on the project’s debt. The amount depends on the asset value and contractual threshold at the relevant time.

The underwriting question

What cash is enforceable between the end of committed rent and the final repayment date? Read the borrower’s actual maturity schedule alongside the lease, guarantee and recourse terms. The two ownership percentages tell the reader who owns the venture. They do not establish how much a creditor would recover.

CROSS-MEMO ANALYSIS / ONE TECHNOLOGY, THREE CLAIMS13 / 18

The same AI success can help one borrower and hurt another

A portfolio can contain opposite business exposures that depend on the same source of finance.

AISoftwarePrivate creditEquities

Follow a customer choosing between old software and a new AI tool. If the new tool genuinely lowers the customer’s cost, the customer benefits first. The incumbent may face weaker pricing or lower renewals. The new supplier may gain users while competition forces it to pass the savings on. A successful technology can produce all three outcomes at once.

PositionSoftware lenderWhat has to workThe borrower retains customers and cash after replacement spending.First evidence to requestPaid renewals, churn by cohort, cash interest coverage.
PositionCompute ownerWhat has to workUtilisation and realised prices cover power, replacement and capital costs.First evidence to requestNew orders converting to paid usage, contracted rates and contract length.

The connection becomes important when financing is scarce. An incumbent needing time to redesign its product and a new operator needing money to finish construction might both approach lenders when appetite is weak. Exposure to different companies can still leave the portfolio dependent on the same refinancing window.

Evidence that would change the view

Track paid customer retention and contract cash, then look at the terms on which fresh capital is actually available. A fully funded borrower with sticky customers presents a different risk from one whose plan depends on raising money every year. Technology labels alone cannot distinguish them.

PORTFOLIO APPLICATION / SINGAPORE14 / 18

The bond matures in USD. The bills arrive in SGD.

For a Singapore investor using CHF financing, a single headline yield leaves three cash-flow questions unanswered.

SingaporeUS TreasuriesUSD/SGDCHF financing

Start with the dated payments of the asset. A conventional fixed-rate US Treasury has a contractual USD coupon and principal schedule. A Treasury STRIP pays at maturity and provides no periodic coupon to meet financing costs. A low-coupon bond bought below par can offer a yield to maturity above its coupon rate because part of the prospective return comes from the move toward redemption value.

ExposureWhat to put on the sheetWhat can interrupt the plan
ExposureUSD bondWhat to put on the sheetDirty purchase price, coupons, maturity proceeds, prospective sale price at the chosen horizon.What can interrupt the planA higher market yield lowers the sale value of a fixed-rate bond before maturity.
ExposureCHF loanWhat to put on the sheetDrawn amount, reset dates, contracted interest, principal due and collateral terms.What can interrupt the planRefinancing rates rise, CHF appreciates or eligible collateral value falls.
ExposureSGD spendingWhat to put on the sheetDates and amounts needed in SGD, with FX conversion shown at each relevant date.What can interrupt the planA weaker USD/SGD can reduce spendable proceeds even when every USD coupon is paid.

Positive carry and a positive total return answer different questions. Interest received can exceed financing cost while the bond’s market value falls. A stronger CHF can simultaneously raise the SGD value of the liability. If the lender requires more collateral before the bond matures, the investor’s ability to wait becomes a source of risk in its own right.

Practical next step

Place the nearest loan reset, spending need and bond cash receipt on the same calendar. Then identify which cash or committed liquidity covers the earliest shortfall. This is a review framework. A numerical return would require actual prices, FX rates, funding terms and cash-flow dates.

The best test is one that can genuinely disappoint you. High usage, a smooth net asset value or a large government buyer can each be true without settling the investment question. State which missing observation would connect that fact to the cash you expect. A useful file note records the source, the date and what your conclusion would be if the next reading went the other way.

The five-memo conclusion

Marks changes his confidence when the evidence changes, and keeps asking what the price already assumes. For a portfolio review, write one sentence per holding naming who pays, when payment is due, and what has to remain true until the investor receives it. Where the sentence depends on a friendly refinancing market, that is the risk to examine first.
