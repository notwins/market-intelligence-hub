    Sources and methods

    Each column measures the cumulative change from the last available observation before the reaction date to the latest qualifying observation by the 1st, 5th, 20th or 60th S&P 500 session. A reaction date can follow the announcement when it was made after US market hours or on a weekend. Singapore and US markets close at different times, so the same date can reflect different information.

    Shares, sector proxies, spot gold and FX are shown as percentage changes in their quoted currencies. Share price changes exclude dividends and currency conversion. Treasury and SGS changes are in basis points. USD/SGD rises when the US dollar strengthens against the Singapore dollar. Timing alone cannot establish that an event caused a price move.

    W marks a weekly result and M marks a monthly one. Shares use the source’s dated weekly periods. Hover over a result for the two observation dates. Weekly data qualify from five sessions onward and monthly data from 60. Daily observations may lag a cutoff by up to four calendar days, weekly by eight and monthly by 35. A dash means comparable observations are missing. Pending means the benchmark session has yet to appear in the saved data.
