Who is buying gold?

Central-bank buyers, sellers and what drives a Singapore investor’s return.

Market Intelligence Hub  ·  Edition 2  ·  Checked 6 November, 16:40 SGT
  1. China and Poland led disclosed Q3 buying. Roughly 63% of total estimated demand cannot be attributed to named buyers.
  2. A central bank may sell gold to raise currency, rebalance a portfolio or meet a policy obligation. The reason changes how to read the sale.
  3. For Singapore investors, both the gold price and USD/SGD determine the unhedged return. Bank forecasts also have different dates and horizons.
Market snapshot Spot gold traded at US$4,047.19/oz on 6 November at 15:18 Singapore time. Reuters
01Official demand

Central banks bought more gold as the price weakened

Reported factsOfficial buyers stepped up sharply after a weak first quarter. The November data show a net 57.7 tonnes in Q1, 296.3 in Q2 and 321.2 in Q3. The last figure was 42% above a year earlier. Yet the World Bank reports that gold prices fell 5% from Q2 to Q3.

Data revisionThe estimate changed enough to alter the story of the year. The first Q1 release put buying at 244 tonnes. The Q2 release revised that sharply downward, and the November table puts it at 57.7. Combining April’s Q1 estimate with November’s Q2 and Q3 figures would exaggerate the level of demand and hide the rebound.

Research interpretationOfficial demand is one influence on the market price. A reserve manager can add to a holding while other investors sell enough to push the price down.

In plain EnglishA large buyer can support demand while other investors sell enough to push the price down. Both sides of the market matter.

02How to read the data

Who bought the remaining gold?

Source estimatePublic filings identify only part of Q3 buying. The World Gold Council puts disclosed net purchases at 120 tonnes, compared with roughly 321 tonnes after Metals Focus incorporates estimates. The gap covers late reporting and trades that never appear in time for publication. It cannot be reliably assigned to China, a Gulf state or another named buyer.

Coverage comparisonThe answer to “is buying up this year?” depends on the series. For January to September, reported purchases fell 7% year on year to 228 tonnes. Estimated demand rose 6% to 675 tonnes. Both comparisons can be right because their coverage differs. A monthly change in disclosed reserves is evidence about that balance sheet, while imports can also reflect households, jewellers, banks and funds.

In plain EnglishThe total estimate is larger than the list of named buyers. Keep that unknown portion visible when explaining who bought gold.

03Disclosed buyers

China picked up the pace as Poland approached its target

Reported factChina added 63 tonnes in Q3, more than half its purchases for the first nine months. Its September statement gives a direct check on the quantity. Holdings rose by 740,000 ounces to 77.47 million ounces, the 23rd consecutive monthly increase. The announcement gives no price target or motive for that month’s purchase.

Reported factPoland’s governor confirmed on 5 November that reserves had reached the 700-tonne goal. Quarterly additions slowed from 51 tonnes in Q2 to 40 tonnes in Q3. He left open further purchases as overall reserves grow.

Source commentaryUzbekistan illustrates how purchases can coexist with a willingness to sell. The central bank added 14 tonnes in Q3. Its governor had discussed selling at favourable prices as part of reserve management. A willingness to sell gives the bank flexibility and leaves the timing open.

In plain EnglishChina accelerated its buying, while Poland reached a stated goal. The next purchase depends on each institution’s policy.

04Disclosed sellers

Gold supplied cash when some official holders needed it

Issuer explanationRussia’s governor linked recent reductions mainly to the finance ministry’s transactions under its fiscal rule. The National Wealth Fund holds assets that the ministry can use under that rule. The governor also cited metal used for coin production. The published explanation does not allocate the quarter’s 19-tonne decline across those uses.

Issuer explanationTürkiye used gold to obtain foreign currency when demand for cash rose. In March, the central bank both disposed of metal and arranged gold-for-currency swaps. Under a swap, the bank agrees to reverse the exchange at maturity. Adding the swap amount to outright sales would overstate the gold permanently sold.

Reported balancesAzerbaijan’s sovereign wealth fund reported the same gold stock in March and September. Its year-end to September holdings fell by 21.9 tonnes. The fund states its diversification policy and allocation limits. Its holdings disclosures leave the specific reason for the reduction open.

In plain EnglishSelling a reserve asset can mean using it for its intended purpose. First identify the transaction and the reason behind it.

05Reading the global register

The largest vaults tell a different story from the largest buyers

Coverage noteA ranking by tonnes held identifies disclosed ownership at each holder’s reporting date. The World Gold Council’s register covers the top 100 disclosed holders and provides a separate file of changes. Most November entries refer to September, while some refer to June or earlier. The prior Hub report screened that roster. This update verifies selected moves and the cases below against their own disclosures.

Ownership and reportingFive legacy holders still account for about 52% of world gold reserves. Federal Reserve staff identify the United States, Germany, Italy, France and the IMF. Together they have added little since the 1970s. Their existing stock can rise by billions of dollars in value without producing a new purchase.

Reported holdingsGold can arrive in a country’s vault without the country buying it. The New York Fed stores metal for official account holders. South Korea also owns units in a gold fund. These give the central bank exposure to the price, while it reports a separate monetary-gold stock.

In plain EnglishA gold bar in a vault, a gold fund share and a customer’s gold held in custody are different exposures. Compare like with like.

06Historical reversals

Czechia rebuilt its stock while Switzerland held steady

Issuer historyCzechia reversed an earlier decision to sell most of its gold. The Czech National Bank resumed more active buying in 2023. Its own analysis found that a holding near 100 tonnes would make its financial results less sensitive to market swings. That explains the target from the perspective of this particular institution.

Reported holdingsThe Swiss National Bank still holds the same 1,040 tonnes it held at the end of 2008. Its gold is stored in Switzerland, London and Canada to preserve access in a crisis. All three locations belong to the same Swiss stock. Counting the location of bars as a purchase would create a trade that never took place.

Policy historyEurope’s coordinated sales wound down before the agreement expired in 2019. The ECB said signatories had sold little for nearly a decade. A world in which legacy holders sell less and other reserve managers buy more can show net purchases even when few institutions reverse their own policy.

In plain EnglishStopping sales leaves the quantity steady. Building a new position increases it. Those actions have different effects on demand.

07Case study · Ghana

A gold export programme can earn dollars and lose money

Reported programme dataGhana turned locally mined gold into foreign currency during a debt crisis. When access to external borrowing tightened, the Bank of Ghana could buy gold from local producers in cedis and export it for dollars. In 2025 it purchased and exported about 104 tonnes of artisanal and small-scale gold worth US$10.9 billion. Most of that metal passed through the programme.

IMF analysisThe same programme generated losses of over US$1.7 billion in 2025, equivalent to 1.5% of GDP. The IMF identifies the biggest contributor as the gap between the exchange rate used to buy the gold and the reference rate used in the bank’s accounts. Assay fees, service charges and discounts to buyers added to losses. The estimate combines valuation effects and operating costs. It excludes the cost of sterilisation, the central bank’s operations to absorb the local currency created through reserve purchases.

Policy changeGoldBod took over the programme’s operations and costs in July 2026. The central bank retained a role as fiscal agent and as recipient of monetary gold for reserves. A change in who buys raw metal can therefore occur alongside continued changes in central-bank bullion holdings.

In plain EnglishThe programme exchanged locally bought gold for dollars. Costs and exchange-rate accounting could consume part of that benefit.

08Reserve management

Why hold gold in reserves?

IMF analysisA gold bar has no issuing government that must repay it. For a reserve manager, that feature can matter when access to foreign assets becomes uncertain. A bar held at home reduces exposure to a foreign custodian. It may still have to be moved, sold or pledged before the central bank can supply dollars to local banks. The IMF authors distinguish long-term reserves from the cash needed at short notice.

IMF model illustrationA reserve manager can allow for the risk of selling under stress. For bullion that can be liquidated within two days, IMF staff illustrate a 6–12% haircut using a 99% value-at-risk calculation with stressed volatility over 1998–2025. A haircut reduces the headline value counted as usable liquidity. The statistical model estimates a loss threshold under stated assumptions.

Survey findingsMore respondents expected global gold holdings to grow than expected their own bank to buy. Among respondents to the 2026 World Gold Council survey, 89% expected global official holdings to rise in the next year and 45% expected their own holdings to rise. Diversification, performance in crises and inflation protection featured in their answers. The survey records intentions. It cannot give the date or size of a trade.

In plain EnglishGold can reduce dependence on an issuing government. Ready-to-use currency can be easier to deploy when bills come due.

09Gold and US Treasuries

A higher reserve share can come from the same bars

IMF analysisGold’s share of reserves can rise before a central bank buys another ounce. The IMF authors found that between 2018 and 2025 the market value of official gold rose 268%, while the physical quantity rose about 8.5%. A reserve portfolio is like a household balance sheet. If a home appreciates, property becomes a larger share of wealth even when the family buys no property.

Federal Reserve staff analysisThe ranking also changes when the investor group changes. A September Federal Reserve staff note points out that world gold includes US government gold, while foreign official Treasury holdings exclude the US. At end-2025, gold outside the US was worth US$4 trillion against US$3.9 trillion in foreign official Treasuries. By June 2026 Treasuries were ahead on that comparison even though gold ounces had increased.

In plain EnglishA family’s home can become a larger share of its wealth without another property purchase. Gold’s reserve share works similarly.

10The price of gold

Funds bought gold in October and still lost value

Reported fund dataInvestors added US$2.7 billion to gold funds in October, yet the funds finished the month worth less. The World Gold Council’s specialised ETF report shows that physical holdings rose by 19 tonnes to 4,275 tonnes, while total assets fell 6% to US$542 billion. The lower price of the metal already in the vault outweighed the value of new subscriptions.

Source limitationThe supplied OCBC tracker’s specific positioning and Shanghai-premium observations have been omitted from this public edition because their exact source has not been independently inspected. The linked publisher portal is retained for reference.

Reported demand dataHouseholds responded differently again. The World Gold Council’s Q3 table showed bar-and-coin demand fell 19% year on year and jewellery consumption fell 17%. A higher price can discourage a wedding purchase even while an official buyer pursues a multi-year policy. The amount one group buys gives no mechanical price target for the next trade.

In plain EnglishBuying more ounces does not ensure the whole holding rises in value. The price of all the ounces already owned also matters.

11Market context

The income gold gives up

Reported factsThe U.S. 10-year real Treasury yield was 2.64% on 4 November. The five latest observations available at the research cutoff ranged from 2.60% to 2.65%. This series was updated on 5 November at 15:16 CST, equivalent to 6 November at 05:16 SGT. The observation date therefore precedes the report date.

Research interpretationA positive real yield creates an income hurdle for gold. Inflation-linked U.S. Treasury securities offer a quoted yield above inflation, while unlent physical gold pays no coupon. A higher real yield can make that income alternative more attractive. Central-bank purchases, investor flows, the U.S. dollar and liquidity can still move gold in the opposite direction over a short period.

In plain EnglishThe yield is the income available above inflation from this Treasury alternative. Gold holders give up that income, so changes in the yield can affect how attractive gold feels.

12Bank and agency outlooks

The World Bank and the Reuters poll disagree on 2027

Forecast comparisonTwo October forecasts give opposite answers to the same question. For the average gold price in 2027, the World Bank estimates US$4,250 an ounce, US$350 below its 2026 figure. The Reuters poll puts 2027 at US$4,650, US$155 above its 2026 median. The gap between their 2027 forecasts is US$400. The two sources envisage different annual price paths.

Research interpretationAn annual average can fall even if gold ends the year above today’s price. An average includes all the observations across the year. A high starting point or a few strong early months can lift it. Standard Chartered’s targets instead describe where the bank expects gold to trade at particular horizons. Combining all these figures into a single target would answer no clear question.

In plain EnglishCompare annual averages with annual averages. A target for a particular date asks a different question.

13Dated targets

The target means more when its date is visible

Bank forecastsThe quoted bank targets sit above the 6 November spot reference, across different horizons. Goldman Sachs lowered its end-2026 forecast to US$4,150, citing the Fed’s more hawkish stance. Standard Chartered’s 30 October report remained constructive over six to twelve months while trimming its nearer-term and longer-term targets.

Research interpretationEach target is a level a particular forecast is trying to reach. The dots share a US-dollar price scale to make the levels readable. The banks issued them on different dates and for different horizons. A point target gives no forecast of the route between today and its target date.

In plain EnglishRead the date beside the target. A twelve-month view and a year-end view can differ without contradicting each other.

14Reading the bank papers

What happened to J.P. Morgan’s US$6,000 call?

Forecast revisionThe US$6,000 forecast in the supplied paper was published on 9 June. The detailed table defined it as a Q4 2026 average. On 3 July Reuters reported a new J.P. Morgan forecast of US$4,500 for the same quarter. Comparing the same horizon gives a US$1,500 reduction, or 25%. The November date on the saved copy records when the web page was printed.

Bank research viewThe bank’s change of mind had a stated economic reason. By July it saw softer demand from key buyers and greater sensitivity to real yields. Real yield is the return a bond offers after allowing for inflation. When that return rises, investors give up more income by holding a bar that pays no coupon. Central banks can keep accumulating gold while private investors change what they are willing to pay for it.

In plain EnglishA downloaded paper can carry an old forecast. Check the original publication date and any subsequent revisions.

Accounting basisMAS gives readers both an accounting value and a physical volume. 17The currency translation

A stronger SGD can reduce the local gold return

Research interpretationUSD/SGD measures the Singapore dollars needed to buy one U.S. dollar. A lower reading means the U.S. dollar buys fewer Singapore dollars. With the USD gold price held constant, that change reduces the SGD value of an unhedged gold holding.

Historical FX referenceThe latest published observation in this series was 1.2776 on 30 October. These five observations were released on 2 November, before the report cutoff. They illustrate the quote direction and the conversion mechanism. The dated dealer gold series supplies the more recent local-currency price context.

In plain EnglishGold and currency both affect your SGD result. If the U.S. dollar weakens, each dollar of gold value converts into fewer Singapore dollars.

18The Singapore investor

Translate the gold return into the currency you spend

Currency mathematicsA Singapore investor who owns unhedged gold has two prices to watch. XAU/USD quotes the US dollars needed to buy one troy ounce. USD/SGD quotes the Singapore dollars needed to buy one US dollar. Multiply the two at the same time to get Singapore dollars per ounce. A stronger Singapore dollar means each US dollar of gold proceeds converts into fewer Singapore dollars.

Instrument comparisonUse the actual instrument and matching timestamps at both ends. A physical bar has a dealer spread and may carry storage costs. An ETF has a fee and its trading price can differ from the value of its gold. A derivatives contract can add collateral, leverage or rolling costs. The earlier Hub study traced XAU39344-USD to a derivatives listing. Joining that series to a spot quote would give a misleading return.

In plain EnglishTo find your return in Singapore dollars, combine the change in gold’s USD price with the change in USD/SGD.

19Portfolio decisions

What would change the investment case?

Research frameworkChoose the reason for holding gold before choosing an entry. If the objective is long-term diversification, ask whether the position still helps when it loses value at the same time as shares. If the objective is a short trade, the price of money, the US dollar and leveraged positioning can matter sooner than the next quarterly reserve release. A cash need in SGD requires a separate currency and liquidity decision.

Conditional analysisA useful test is to specify what would change your mind. A stronger dollar and rising real yields can pressure gold even as official demand holds up. Conversely, easier real yields and persistent investment inflows can support a recovery. These are conditional paths. Data observed together can support a narrative without proving which trade moved the price.

In plain EnglishDecide what the holding is meant to do, then watch the evidence that would support or weaken that purpose.

20Reading guide

Which research paper answers which question?

Reading guideThe most useful papers look at different parts of the gold market. The IMF authors study what a reserve manager can do with bullion in a crisis. The ECB and Federal Reserve papers test how much of the rise in gold’s reported importance comes from prices and existing holdings. Bank outlooks focus more closely on the marginal buyer and the next few quarters.

Source scopeThe silver and renminbi papers help define the limits of the gold argument. J.P. Morgan’s silver research discusses solar manufacturers reducing silver use. That industrial channel is much less central to the official gold story. OCBC’s November renminbi note concerns its currency outlook, and its sustainability weekly covers other policy developments. Neither establishes the identity of an undisclosed gold buyer.

In plain EnglishUse the country’s own disclosure for its reported holdings, research papers for mechanisms and dated forecasts for expectations.
