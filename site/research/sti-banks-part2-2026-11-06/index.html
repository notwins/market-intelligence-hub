SGS · T-bills · Bank earnings

The banks’ interest-margin engine

About two-thirds of the three banks’ latest quarterly income came from net interest. Eleven years of reported margins show why the yield cycle matters, and why funding costs, loan resets and hedges decide how much reaches earnings.

Four takeaways

  • Banks remain heavily dependent on the interest margin. In 3Q26, net interest income contributed S$8.302bn of S$12.959bn combined group income. Costs, credit losses and tax determine how much becomes profit.
  • The banks’ own attribution shows how large the rate effect can be. Rates explained S$5.190bn of the S$5.994bn rise in 2023 NII. In 2025, rate effects cut NII by S$2.327bn before offsets.
  • T-bills affect competition for deposits as well as returns on bank assets. DBS reported funds returning from maturing retail T-bills into deposits as rates fell in 2025.
  • The direction of SGS yields is only the beginning of the earnings analysis. The outcome depends on which assets and funding reprice first, their currencies, and the bank’s hedges.

01 / The earnings engine

Most income still comes from the interest spread

Net interest income (NII) is interest earned on loans, securities and other earning assets, less interest paid on deposits and other funding. Net interest margin (NIM) divides that annualised income by average interest-earning assets. It measures the spread earned across the balance sheet.

The identity behind the results

A small change in margin acts on a very large asset base

NII = NIM × average earning assetsFull-year basis. Apply the reporting-period fraction for an interim period.

DBS’s FY2025 numbers make the scale tangible. S$14,500m of NII divided by S$721,393m of average interest-bearing assets gives the reported 2.01% margin after rounding.

NIM can decline while NII rises. Expanding the asset base or adding lower-margin, profitable liquid assets can increase interest dollars despite reducing the average percentage earned.

Why loan rate minus deposit rate does not reproduce NIM

The numerator and denominator cover the full earning balance sheet. Banks hold securities and interbank placements as well as loans, and use wholesale funding, equity and deposits with different costs. The average asset and liability balances differ. Simple subtraction of two quoted customer rates omits these effects.

In plain English Think of NIM as the margin on each dollar of earning assets. NII also depends on how many dollars the bank puts to work.

Auction and secondary-market measures

A 6-month T-bill puts a visible price on household cash

The latest MAS-listed 6M auction cut-off was 1.86% for BS26122V, due to be issued on 10 November. The prior issue’s cut-off was 1.95%, a decline of 9bp. On 5 November, the benchmark’s secondary-market closing bid yield was also 1.86%.

Customers compare that return with deposits. Higher T-bill yields can encourage funds to leave a low-paying account or push a bank to improve deposit offers. When the relative appeal fades and bills mature, some cash can return.

The loan reset follows its contractual benchmark. A floating SGD mortgage linked to compounded SORA responds to its own reset convention. MAS defines SORA from actual unsecured overnight SGD interbank borrowing, with compounded tenors published separately.

03 / Eleven years of reported margins

The low-rate years and the 2023 peak are visible in all three banks

Full-year group NIM rose sharply from 2021 to 2023, then softened through 2025. DBS gained 70bp over that upswing, OCBC 74bp and UOB 53bp. These are realised group margins after the banks’ funding costs and balance-sheet mix.

2015–2019

Margins varied within a relatively narrow range. By 2019, DBS reported 1.89%, OCBC 1.77% and UOB 1.78%. Each bank entered the pandemic with a different asset and funding mix.

2020–2021

Lower rates compressed the spread. By 2021, NIM was 1.45% at DBS, 1.54% at OCBC and 1.56% at UOB. Falling deposit costs could only offset part of the decline in asset yields.

2022–2025

The higher-rate cycle lifted margins before the benefits receded. OCBC’s NIM reached 2.28% in 2023 and fell to 1.91% by 2025. DBS and UOB also declined from their 2023 highs.

In plain English The banks kept their franchises throughout the cycle. The spread earned on those franchises changed substantially, which changed the earnings available to shareholders.
04 / What the banks themselves attributed to rates

The 2023 NII uplift was mostly rate-driven

In the banks’ own decompositions, rate effects contributed S$5.190bn, or 86.59%, of the combined FY2023 NII increase. Volume effects contributed S$804m. These calculations sum each bank’s published group attribution across its assets, funding and currencies.

05 / How the yield move reaches NIM

Follow both the money lent and the money borrowed

SGS and T-bill yields influence bank economics through several channels. The asset side can earn a higher rate while the funding side becomes more expensive. Reset dates, customer behaviour and hedges determine the net effect.

Exhibit 08 · rate transmission map

One market move reaches several parts of the balance sheet

Floating-rate loans

Compounded SORA or another contractual benchmark resets → asset yield changes after the loan’s reset lag.

Deposits and wholesale funding

T-bills and competing offers change the return customers demand → funding cost and the deposit mix change.

Fixed-rate loans and securities

New lending and reinvestment occur at new yields → the asset yield adjusts as old positions mature or reset.

Hedges and currencies

Swaps alter effective fixed/floating exposure → SGD, USD and other rate moves can have different effects.

Asset interest − funding interest = NII
Divide by average earning assets to calculate NIM.

Deposit beta and funding mix

The cost of deposits catches up with market rates

Deposit beta describes how much of a benchmark-rate change passes into the deposit rate. Customers can also shift from current and savings accounts into higher-paying fixed deposits. That mix shift raises the average cost even if each product’s own rate is unchanged.

Low-cost deposits create an advantage until their price or mix changes. CASA means current-account and savings-account deposits. Their stability depends on transaction needs, competing offers and customer behaviour. On the way down, a deposit already paying little interest has limited room to fall.

What to inspect. Average deposit cost, CASA share, fixed-deposit maturities, and funding growth alongside average loan and securities yields.

A direct T-bill example · DBS FY2025

Maturing T-bills helped bring deposits back

DBS reported retail funds returning from maturing T-bills as rates fell. Deposits increased S$64bn, or 12% on a constant-currency basis, to S$610bn. More than two-thirds of the increase came from CASA.

T-bills matureCash returns→Deposits growFunding expands→HQLA addedNII rises

Deploying surplus deposits into high-quality liquid assets lowered the average margin while increasing income. DBS said HQLA grew S$42bn to S$208bn. These assets earned a thinner spread than some loans but still added NII and ROE. This explains why the margin percentage alone can miss an improvement in earnings.

DBS · FY2025 CFO statement

Singapore rates also reflect the currency framework

MAS conducts monetary policy through the Singapore dollar exchange-rate path. With open capital markets, domestic rates are strongly influenced by global rates and expectations for the SGD. A US rate change can therefore transmit to Singapore without matching it point for point. Local liquidity and funding demand add another layer.

MAS · monetary-policy framework
In plain English For a bank, the timing gap matters. Loan income may rise before deposit costs, or funding may become expensive before the loan book catches up. The same yield direction can produce different quarterly margins.
06 / The latest quarter

Funding costs weighed on UOB’s margin

UOB’s group NIM fell 6bp between 2Q26 and 3Q26, from 1.74% to 1.68%. Its public performance highlights attribute the decline to higher funding costs. Loan growth helped offset the effect on net interest income.

Peer checks · 3Q26

Each group absorbed a different mix of rate effects

OCBC benefited from higher SORA in its SGD business. It said SGD asset yields rose faster than funding costs. In USD, funding repriced ahead of assets. Group NIM fell from 1.70% to 1.66%, yet NII rose from S$2,264m to S$2,313m as average earning assets expanded.

DBS’s group margin stabilised while its commercial-book margin declined. Group NIM stayed at 1.87%, while the commercial-book measure fell from 2.21% to 2.16%. Group NII increased from S$3,581m to S$3,711m. Management reported a 1.90% exit margin.

That difference in scope matters when reading a chart. Comparing DBS’s commercial-book NIM with the other banks’ group NIM would overstate the margin gap. The historical charts in this report use group figures throughout.

In plain English A higher yield today can improve future loan returns while hurting this quarter through faster funding repricing. Read the reported asset and funding outcomes before assuming an earnings benefit.
07 / Hedges & interest-rate risk

The sign of the rate exposure can change

Banks manage their exposure using asset mix, deposit behaviour and derivatives. A bank can remain highly dependent on NII even after reducing, delaying or reversing its sensitivity to a particular market rate.

DBS management sensitivity · reported 5 Nov 2026

Singapore and US rates had opposite reported sensitivities

+S$10m/bpSingapore interest rates
−S$2m/bpUS interest rates

DBS’s 3Q26 CEO slide reports positive NII sensitivity to Singapore rates and negative sensitivity to US rates. Those coefficients apply to its prevailing positioning. The slide states S$m per basis point without specifying a separate shock horizon.

Hedging has changed the exposure over time. At end-2025, DBS reported S$210bn of fixed-rate assets, including fixed mortgages, swaps and securities. Its reported USD sensitivity then was −S$4m/bp. The 2026 coefficient belongs to a different date and balance sheet.

DBS · 3Q26 CEO slides DBS · FY2025 CFO statement

A bond valuation loss and NII pressure measure different things

Higher long-term yields can lower the market value of existing fixed-rate assets while improving the rate available on reinvestment. Fair-value classifications determine whether marks appear in profit or other comprehensive income. Interest on amortised-cost assets follows effective-interest accounting.

OCBC separately reported a S$1,062m adverse economic-value-of-equity effect under its FY2025 parallel-up scenario. It associated higher EVE sensitivity with increased fixed-rate bond holdings. Its NII downside test ran in the opposite direction. Combining these two amounts would mix different scenarios and economic measures.

OCBC · Annual Report 2025
In plain English NIM dependence describes how the bank earns. Rate sensitivity describes what a particular rate move does to that earning engine after its funding and hedges are considered.
08 / Valuation & monitoring

Treat the interest margin as a cyclical earnings input

The earnings case for a bank share should state what margin it assumes and why that margin is sustainable. A premium valuation needs more support when the past improvement in return on equity depended heavily on a favourable rate cycle.

Exhibit 13 · the earnings-to-valuation link
NIM changesSpread earned→NII changesInterest dollars→Earnings / ROEValuation input

Costs, provisions, fee income and capital determine the final outcome. Wealth-management growth can provide a valuable offset. Asset growth can stabilise NII. Credit losses can erase the gain from a wider margin. Sustainable earnings therefore require more than a yield-chart direction.

Reading the source boundaries

  • Embedded Hub history. The chart reuses the supplied workbook series through Aug 2026. It retains gaps. Monthly averaging and the original download date remain unverified. The latest official MAS daily observation is shown in its own panel.
  • Annual versus quarterly data. Full-year NIM and annualised quarterly NIM use separate charts. A published quarter-end or exit margin is a different observation again.
  • Rates and volume. Aggregates sum issuer decompositions in S$m. Cross-effect allocation conventions can differ, and the rate component includes multiple currencies, asset pricing and funding pricing.
  • Changing business scope. Acquisitions, geography and asset mix evolve over the sample. OCBC includes insurance. Long-run total-income accounting presentations also changed, so historical charts focus on group NIM and NII.
  • Rounding. Calculations retain reported source precision. Displays use two decimals for rates and percentages. Individual rounded quarters can differ from reported annual or year-to-date sums by S$1m.
