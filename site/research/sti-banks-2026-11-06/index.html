The STI’s bank premium

DBS, OCBC and UOB earned S$6.105 billion in the quarter. All three earned less than in the previous quarter. How much of a recovery is already in their share prices?

Investment view

  • I would trim a bank overweight into strength. An STI holding already places about 57 cents of every dollar in these three names. Direct bank shares can double up that exposure.
  • The sector’s 13.58% year-on-year profit growth flatters the underlying quarter. UOB’s prior-year provisions explain much of the rebound. Combined operating profit before allowances fell 2.41% year on year.
  • The reasons for caution differ by bank. DBS commands the highest price for its book. OCBC has the strongest operational rebuttal. UOB trades closest to book, but earns the lowest return on equity.

01 / Index exposure

Three banks set the pace for thirty stocks

DBS alone represents 29.31% of the Straits Times Index. Singapore’s benchmark weights constituents by investable market value. Its thirty names give a wider impression of diversification than its three largest positions deliver.

The index had already pulled back

The STI closed at 5,296.25 on 5 November, up 13.99% from 4,646.21 at end-2025. At 9.10am SGT on 6 November it traded at 5,175.25, 121 points below the previous close. That intraday reading followed OCBC’s results and a broader sell-off. It cannot isolate the market’s response to OCBC.

02 / Earnings quality

Profit rose on last year. Operating profit fell.

The year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter comparisons point in opposite directions. Lower provisions amplified the rebound from a weak 3Q25. Against 2Q26, all three banks lost earnings momentum.

03 / The three franchises

Each bank has a different earnings test

The same interest-rate move reaches the three banks through different businesses. DBS has a wealth and markets franchise, OCBC consolidates an insurer, and UOB’s comparison is especially sensitive to the credit provisions it booked last year.

DBS

Markets trading income fell from S$469m to S$152m in one quarter. That S$317m revenue decline exceeded the S$266m drop in group net profit. Other lines absorbed part of the hit. The commercial book produced a record S$5.693bn of income.

The deposit and wealth franchises strengthened. Group NII rose 4% both sequentially and year on year. Wealth fees rose 9% year on year to S$864m, and wealth assets reached S$544bn. Those figures explain why investors pay more for DBS than its peers.

The premium leaves little room for a second disappointment. Group NIM averaged 1.87% but exited the quarter at 1.90%. Management’s mid-single-digit 2027 total income growth view assumes average SORA near 1.6%, two further Fed hikes by end-2027 and a stronger Singapore dollar. Follow the realised margin and the trading result separately.

Reading the bridge. S$ million, 2Q to 3Q26. Banking profit rose from 1,750 to 1,895. Great Eastern’s after-tax contribution fell from 471 to 147. Add the two changes to the opening balance. OCBC Results Highlights, slide 5, 6 Nov.

The banking result is the clearest challenge to a cautious sector view. Banking wealth AUM reached S$358bn, NPLs were 0.8% of loans, and insurance new business embedded value rose 14% year on year to S$208m. The investment marks and new insurance business tell different stories.

At twice book, some of that strength is already reflected in the price. FY26 management guidance calls for low-double-digit loan growth, at least mid-single-digit total income growth and flat to slightly lower NII. Loan credit costs of 6bp in 3Q were favourable against full-year guidance of 15–20bp.

A S$1.103bn fall in allowances outweighed the S$807m rise in year-on-year net profit. UOB booked S$1.361bn of allowances in 3Q25, including pre-emptive provisions, versus S$258m this quarter. Operating profit fell 3% over the same period. The allowances are pre-tax and the profit is after tax.

Fees reached S$733m, up 19% year on year, but that did not offset weaker quarterly trading and the absence of prior divestment gains. NIM fell from 1.74% to 1.68% sequentially. At 46.4%, UOB also had the highest cost-to-income ratio of the three.

The case for a recovery depends on earning more from the existing franchise. Management expects 2027 NII above 2026 and mid-to-high-single-digit growth in both fees and operating costs. Its 25–30bp total credit-cost guidance compares with 24bp in 3Q26. Watch the gap between income growth and expense growth.

In plain English DBS’s swing came from trading, OCBC’s from insurance investments, and UOB’s annual rebound from last year’s credit charges. A single sector headline hides all three.
04 / Valuation & distributions

Share prices rose faster than book values

OCBC had the largest price increase, while DBS still trades at the highest multiple. Price to book (P/B) is the share price divided by ordinary book value per share. A high multiple can be earned by sustaining a high return on that equity. The question is how much improvement the price already requires.

Historical perspective · Dividend Titan

Has the 2025 valuation concern survived the earnings?

Willie Keng’s October 2025 essay asked whether earnings could keep up with the price paid for Singapore banks. His concern about interest margins holds up year on year across all three. His rate-cycle explanation needs updating. DBS and OCBC grew quarterly NII in dollars, and DBS reported an improving exit margin. The case for caution now rests more on what investors pay for those earnings than on a blanket prediction that falling rates will squeeze every bank.

In plain English A bank can grow profits and still lose you money if buyers decide to pay less for each dollar of its equity. The dividend offsets only part of that price move.
05 / Earnings history & what to track

What would change the portfolio view?

I would start by trimming the overlap between direct bank holdings and STI exposure. I would revisit that stance if returns on equity rise enough to support the premium, or if share prices offer the same earnings at a more attractive valuation.

Risk to the cautious view

Repricing can eventually help the lender

Funding costsreprice→Loans resetwith a lag→NII canrecover

A higher rate does different things at different times. OCBC reported near-term pressure as US dollar funding costs moved ahead of asset yields. DBS ended the quarter at a higher NIM than its quarterly average. If margins recover while loans grow and credit stays sound, sector earnings could catch up with valuations.
