Research 2. MAS, BoE and BoT: Safer cross-border FX settlement

Project Meridian FX APAC · 13 November 2026 · Joint central-bank experiment · Full Technical and Policy Reports available as official HTML

Project Meridian FX APAC: Technical Report

Project Meridian FX APAC: Policy Report

What the experiment demonstrated: A synchronisation operator coordinated payment-versus-payment settlement across three different simulated wholesale settlement environments, representing approaches explored by the Bank of England, MAS and Bank of Thailand.

It also demonstrated multilateral net settlement, in which offsetting payments can reduce the liquidity required compared with settling every obligation separately. No universal percentage saving was established.

Why payment-versus-payment matters

Without synchronisation

One currency can transfer while the other payment is delayed or fails

Synchronised PvP

Both payment legs are coordinated to complete together or not at all

Illustrative settlement structure. The actual trial used a synchronisation operator, reservation/escrow arrangements and different test-ledger designs.

What was tested: Different settlement architectures, insufficient funds, invalid payment identifiers and system outages. The prototype preserved all-or-nothing outcomes in the tested scenarios.

What was not tested: Live payment volumes, non-overlapping RTGS operating hours, every legal-finality scenario and realistic intraday liquidity demands. The technical and policy reports expressly identify these gaps.

Credit and FX implication: Safer wholesale settlement could reduce settlement risk and improve liquidity efficiency over time. It does not remove the exchange-rate risk of USD assets or CHF borrowings.

Singapore relevance: MAS participated directly, with the RBA and Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas observing. This is a relevant development for cross-border banking infrastructure, not a new yield-bearing investment product.

Plain-language explanation: The project explores ways for two currencies to exchange safely at the same time, reducing the risk that only one side of an FX transaction completes.

Research 5. Bank of England: Quantitative tightening changes sovereign debt supply

Bank of England · Asset Purchase Facility Quarterly Report, 2026 Q3 · Published 10 November · Underlying holdings 30 September · Older than today’s releases, newly highlighted for duration strategy

Asset Purchase Facility Quarterly Report, 2026 Q3

What changed: The BoE’s latest report details the plan to unwind its monetary-policy gilt portfolio, using sales and maturities. During Q3, gilt sales reduced holdings by £4.3 billion, while maturities reduced them by another £29.3 billion.

The central bank intends to retain £120 billion of the longest-maturity gilts for its banknote-backing arrangements and allow £222 billion of shorter-dated holdings to mature. Its proposed treatment of £146 billion of remaining 2035–2049 gilts remains subject to agreement with the UK government.

Methodology: Table A reports APF holdings at original purchase prices, not current market values. Chart 1 contains highly assumption-sensitive estimates of the lifetime fiscal effects of QE/QT.

Bond implication: Central-bank asset sales and natural maturities change how much duration the private sector must absorb. In stressed markets, additional duration supply can raise the compensation investors demand.

Counterargument: Predictable runoff and retention of the longest gilts can reduce market disruption relative to an abrupt liquidation.

Plain-language explanation: Government-bond yields depend on both central-bank interest rates and how much long-term debt private investors are being asked to hold.

New BIS paper: AI econometrics is not a substitute for human judgement

MaRCo@BIS: AI-agentic econometric analysis for central bankers, BIS Working Paper 1395, published 12 November, introduces a system designed to assist economists with model specification, estimation and diagnostic checks.

The authors benchmarked the framework against three structural VAR studies and illustrated five research exercises. They found useful replication capabilities where data and specifications were well defined, but substantial model uncertainty when assumptions were incomplete.

Investment relevance: Economic forecasts depend on model assumptions. This supports using several scenarios rather than treating one interest-rate forecast as definitive.

Japan and broader equity implications

Reuters reported that Japan’s three megabanks raised annual earnings forecasts while warning that a slowdown in data-centre investment could affect activity. MUFG reported approximately ¥3.3 trillion in data-centre credit exposure against ¥180 trillion total credit exposure. That ratio alone does not determine credit losses.

Reuters also reported that the S&P 500 and Nasdaq fell on 13 November, with technology and semiconductor shares under pressure. Market-price declines can increase the cost of raising new equity and reduce collateral value for financed positions.

Counterargument: An equity selloff may improve prospective valuations, and strong hyperscaler balance sheets can absorb considerable investment spending. It is not appropriate to classify all AI-linked debt as distressed.

Final assessment

Duration: Long Treasury yields remain elevated, but inflation uncertainty makes staggered positioning and security-specific duration calculations important.

Credit: The most meaningful risk lies in the distinction between successful fundraising and sustainable debt service. Large commitments, leases and guarantees require reconciliation before assessing borrower leverage.

Liquidity: Bank capital instruments, private credit, equipment financing and leveraged bond portfolios can experience pressure before contractual defaults become visible.

Action: Prioritise obtaining the full November Fed Financial Stability Report, monitoring AI financing and refinancing disclosures, comparing Alphabet’s final SGD debt terms against SGS, and tracking early loan arrears and cash interest collections.

The central principle: The yield printed on a bond is only worthwhile if the repayment source, collateral structure and investor liquidity can withstand adverse market conditions.
