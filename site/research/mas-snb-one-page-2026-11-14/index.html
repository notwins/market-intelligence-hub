MAS–SNB research · Client one-pager

SGD has policy support.
CHF borrowing still carries risk.

Singapore’s appreciating currency policy can support SGD, but low Swiss interest rates do not protect a borrower from a stronger Swiss franc.

  • Two different policy holds: MAS retains currency appreciation; SNB retains a 0% policy rate.
  • Inflation needs context: Swiss underlying pressure remained below headline inflation.
  • Currency risk remains: a stronger CHF raises the SGD cost of repaying CHF debt.

What could change the conclusion?

CHF can strengthen even with low rates. A market sell-off can trigger safe-haven demand or repayment of CHF-funded investments. Broader Swiss inflation or policy surprises can also strengthen CHF.

MAS does not target CHF/SGD. Its currency basket policy does not guarantee SGD gains against CHF.

Before borrowing in CHF: check loan pricing, fees, repayment dates, hedges and collateral headroom. The SNB’s 0% is not your borrowing rate. Falling investments and a stronger CHF can occur together.
