MARKET INTEL · AI FINANCING / CREDIT RESEARCH

AI’s financing loop
raises the cost of disappointment

Circular funding, rising debt-insurance costs and supplier guarantees warrant closer scrutiny. They do not, by themselves, establish that a crash has begun.

Research cutoff: 10 November 2026, 01:53 SGT · Edition 1 · Independent educational analysis

USBISArtificial intelligenceCreditFinancing · Analysis

Five key takeaways

  1. The overlap is measurable. In the BIS sample, circular deals represented 16.1% of AI-to-AI deal count but 46.4% of disclosed deal value during 2021–2025. These are financing statistics, not a share of AI revenue. [1]
  2. Credit markets are signalling a higher price for risk. Dated October reports document stress, especially at CoreWeave. Trading volume and wider spreads alone do not prove banks are exiting all AI exposure. [9] [12]
  3. Guarantees transfer risk back to suppliers. Read the trigger, timing, collateral recovery and legal cap before treating a headline amount as a loss. [4] [6] [7]
  4. A quoted default probability is model-dependent. Recovery assumptions and contract conventions matter; market-implied risk is not an objective forecast.
  5. The practical response is to review overlapping exposures. Assess equity, credit, funds, structured products and financing together. Avoid turning a credible vulnerability into an unsupported market-timing call.

The study tracks relationships, not traced banknotes. Its universe contains 1,246 AI firms and 972 intra-AI investment relationships in 2021–2025. A relationship counts as circular if an investment link and a supplier–customer link coexist at any point within that five-year window. This does not establish that each investment financed a specific purchase. [1]

The full financing round can enter the numerator. If one participating AI investor has a commercial link to the target, the deal is classified as circular and the full disclosed round value is counted once. The paper does not allocate that total among investors. Deal count, relationship count and disclosed value are different measures. [1]

Technical detail · reproduce the headline ratios correctly
Outgoing AI-to-AI share = disclosed AI-to-AI deal value ÷ disclosed value of all deals in which AI firms invest
Incoming AI-to-AI share = disclosed AI-to-AI deal value ÷ disclosed value of all deals in which AI firms receive investment
Circular value share = disclosed circular AI-to-AI deal value ÷ disclosed value of all AI-to-AI deals

Do not manufacture missing totals. The underlying proprietary deal microdata were not obtained. Charts reproduce the paper’s published percentages; they are not an independent re-estimation. Incomplete disclosure, broad relationship timing and sample coverage limit inference.

Economic reasons exist alongside the risks. Suppliers can understand customers better than outside financiers; customers can finance scarce inputs; shared ownership can support specialised investments. The concern is dependence on continuing financing and final demand—not circularity alone. The study notes parallels with vendor finance in telecoms, while distinguishing debt from equity exposure. Analysis

Trace the outside customer’s cash

The loop needs an economic endpoint. An AI firm receives financing, pays for computing capacity, and tries to earn revenue from users. The supplier may book genuine sales while also carrying an investment or guarantee exposure. Financing is not operating revenue for the recipient.

The stress can arrive through several channels. Weak customer cash generation can cut equipment orders, reduce investment values and trigger support obligations. A fall in resale values can increase losses precisely when default risk rises.

A simple reading test. Ask whether end users pay enough, at sustainable margins, to cover operations, replacement equipment and debt service without repeated new funding.

05 / CREDIT SIGNALS

A wider CDS spread prices protection; it does not directly predict default

Keep four quantities separate. CDS turnover is trading activity. The spread is a price for credit protection. Bond yield includes the interest-rate benchmark and other components. A default probability is a model output. Treating them as interchangeable exaggerates certainty.

Full workings and limits of the CDS approximation

With R = 40%: λ = 0.11 / 0.60 = 0.183333… per year; Q = 1 − exp(−0.11 × 5 / 0.60). This gives 60.015034565515265%.

The published ISDA settings include a 40% convention for standard North American corporate senior contracts. That is a pricing convention, not guaranteed recovery. Actual valuation uses the yield curve, dates, accrual, payment timing and contract terms. Risk premia and market liquidity also affect spread. [14] [17]

The sensitivity endpoints of 20% and 60% are author-selected teaching cases. They are not estimates for CoreWeave. No default date, probability distribution for recovery, or trading recommendation is inferred.

Hedging can enable new lending. An institution can buy protection to manage concentration or release risk capacity while continuing to originate loans. Every CDS trade also has a seller. Gross trading volume cannot identify who reduced net exposure; protection can introduce basis and counterparty risk. This is why “smart money is exiting” is stronger than the evidence here establishes.

Analysis

Drawdowns can arrive before defaults

Undrawn does not mean irrelevant. A borrower under pressure can draw a credit line before repayment problems appear. Yet commitments are not expected losses: drawdown, default and recovery must all be considered.

Look through the financial chain. Banks can finance the borrower, the project vehicle and investors holding its debt. Hedging changes who bears loss; it need not remove the exposure from the financial system. [13]

The counterevidence matters. BIS Bulletin 140’s official summary reports small, short-lived spillovers to US Treasury and investment-grade bond markets on AI-financing news days in 2026, while warning that continued issuance could change the result. That evidence does not establish a broad financial crisis already under way. [13]

2007 is a mechanism analogy, not a timetable. Common vulnerabilities include layered exposures, dependence on refinancing and risk transferred to institutions that may be less visible. The BIS paper’s telecom-vendor-finance parallel is more directly related to suppliers funding customers. A historical resemblance cannot establish identical collateral quality, leverage, regulation or eventual losses. [1]

Strategy: favour resilience over a binary bubble call. For a concentrated investor, rebalancing toward an intended risk budget is more defensible than selecting a crash date from a video. For a new purchase, require a valuation and cash-generation case that survives less generous refinancing. This report does not provide current prices or a security-specific buy/sell target.

What would change the view? More revenue collected from independent end users, stronger cash generation after capital spending, less reliance on supplier support, and improving financing terms would weaken the cautious case. Rising guarantees without commensurate cash generation would strengthen it.

Client-ready explanation

AI suppliers increasingly help finance or guarantee their customers. That can accelerate genuine growth, but it also means one customer problem can hurt sales and create financial losses at the same time. Credit markets are charging more for some of these risks. The sensible response is to check cash generation, guarantees and portfolio concentration—not assume that every AI company is about to fail.
