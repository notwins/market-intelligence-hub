CPF OA and Temasek 2070

Spend the income. Leave the capital. Testing the “2× income, 2× capital” idea with S$200,000, seven USD/SGD scenarios, verified bond terms and evidence from CPF, GIC, MAS and Temasek.

Latest research papersStatic research explained

Five takeaways

  1. Price drives the pitch: a 2.5% coupon at price 50 is a 5% cash yield on clean cost.
  2. Same S$200,000 budget: the lot-sized illustration pays S$9,945 a normal year versus S$5,000 OA base interest, before unknown costs.
  3. FX matters: every requested 1.20–1.35 scenario stays ahead on gross income and par-redemption capital; this is not a forecast or an FX floor.
  4. Family inheritance is market value: nearly 2× capital requires full par payment in 2070 at unchanged FX, not an early sale.
  5. Different protections: CPF is Government-backed in SGD; Temasek’s corporate guarantee does not protect the bond’s market price or SGD value.

Price 50 → Face 100

A bond is a loan claim on an issuer. This note is issued by Temasek Financial (I) Limited and guaranteed by Temasek Holdings. A secondary-market buyer pays the seller for that claim. Temasek owes payments under the terms regardless of whether the buyer paid 50 or 100.

Per US$100 of face valueAmountWhat it means
Clean purchase at price 50US$50Cash paid for the bond, excluding accrued interest
Annual coupon at 2.5%US$2.502.5% is applied to face value
Annual coupon / clean cost2.50 ÷ 50 = 5%Current cash yield at this price
Par redemptionUS$100Payment at redemption if obligations are met
Capital multiple100 ÷ 50 = 2×Cumulative repayment multiple, not an annual return

This is why a 2.5% bond can pay twice a 2.5% OA income rate on the same clean investment. The coupon percentage applies to face value, while the investor buys that face value at half price. With unchanged FX, S$200,000 of clean bond cost corresponds to S$10,000 annual coupons and S$400,000 par repayment.

Coupon yield and yield to maturity answer different questions. The uploaded calculator shows 5.551% YTM at price 50 and its settlement date. YTM discounts the dated USD cashflows to the dirty purchase cost; it is not the amount paid as cash each year and does not fix the SGD return. Realising an equivalent compounded outcome depends on reinvestment and the actual cashflow path.

The purchase discount is compensation for waiting and taking risk. Long-dated low-coupon bonds can trade far below par when market discount rates are higher. A strong credit rating does not force the market price back to 100 today.
Layman story. You pay 50 for a promise to receive 100 much later, with 2.50 a year in between. Your 2.50 payment is 5% of the 50 you paid. You still need to wait for the 100; it is not money already sitting in your account.

CPF → Interest → Family

The OA base-income calculation is S$200,000 × 2.5% = S$5,000 a full year. The comparison holds the balance at S$200,000 and treats eligible interest withdrawals as spending. It does not assume the interest compounds while simultaneously being spent.

CPF questionMeaning for this comparison
Is 2.5% fixed to 2070?No. It is the current legislated floor and current base rate. The model holds it at 2.5%; future rates/rules can differ.
Is interest paid to a bank account?It is credited within CPF. Spending requires an eligible withdrawal.
Can every S$200,000 OA balance be withdrawn?No. Age, retirement-sum requirements and individual circumstances matter.
Can this USD bond be bought directly through CPFIS?It does not meet the corporate-bond criterion requiring SGD trading. The comparison needs withdrawable cash.
What happens to extra interest?It depends on age and combined balances, with OA participation capped at S$20,000. Extra interest attributable to OA goes to SA/RA.

Leaving the interest in OA is a different strategy. If interest is retained, the account grows. The exact fixed-rate formula after n full years is S$200,000 × (1.025)n. A growth comparison must also specify what happens to every bond coupon and the FX path; it cannot fairly compare compounded CPF with spent bond coupons.

CPF nomination offers a straightforward route to beneficiaries. A valid nomination covers CPF account savings, which are paid in cash outside the estate governed by a will. A bond instead follows the relevant custody and estate arrangements. This operational difference matters when comparing legacy plans.

Layman story. If I take the fruit from a savings tree each year, the tree stays the same size in this simple model. If I leave the fruit to grow more trees, it compounds. I cannot count the same fruit as both dinner and new trees.

CPF → Government → GIC

CPF savings reach global investments through the Government's balance sheet. CPF Board invests in Special Singapore Government Securities. The Government pools the proceeds with its other funds, and GIC manages the Government portfolio for long-term returns.

Who carries which exposure?Plain meaning
CPF memberReceives the applicable SGD CPF interest and account entitlement
Singapore GovernmentIssues/guarantees SSGS and stands between CPF obligations and investment performance
GICInvests the pooled Government assets across global markets
TemasekSeparate company with its own assets and liabilities; has not managed CPF or SSGS proceeds

GIC does not have to earn exactly the OA rate each year for CPF interest to be paid. Investment returns fluctuate, while the CPF obligation is governed by the CPF framework. A member does not personally bear GIC's USD translation moves on an OA balance.

Layman story. CPF is a SGD promise supported by the Government. The Government puts its wider assets to work around the world. The member receives interest under CPF rules while the Government absorbs the difference between good and bad investment years.

The purchase discount creates a measurable FX buffer. In the ideal price-50 example, the same S$200,000 clean outlay buys twice its USD equivalent in face value. Annual coupon income and par repayment, expressed as multiples of the OA base comparison, both equal 2 × future FX / 1.275.

One FX move → Decades of FX

A large cumulative FX decline can build from modest yearly changes. The seven requested rates are useful conversion scenarios, but they are not a complete stress test for a bond due in 2070. Holding a USD principal promise for decades creates cumulative currency exposure.

Exact long-horizon identity: a USD decline that halves its SGD value over 44 full years has annual compound depreciation of 1 - 2-1/44. This is less than 2% a year. A 50% cumulative fall therefore need not arrive as one dramatic shock.

My thoughts on the comparison

A concise narrative ready to use in the My Thoughts section of the HTML report

If the intention is to spend the income and leave the capital invested, I think a discounted Temasek USD bond deserves a serious comparison with CPF OA. The case is the relationship between what I pay, the cash it pays me and the principal due later.

The clean mathematics explains my “2× income, 2× capital” idea. At a price of 50, every US$50 of clean purchase cost buys US$100 of face value. A 2.5% coupon on that face pays US$2.50, which is 5% of the clean cost. If repaid at par and FX is unchanged, the principal is twice that clean purchase cost.

Using S$200,000 properly brings the result close to, rather than exactly, 2×. At FX 1.275, the US$312,000-face illustration includes US$650 accrued interest and leaves S$271.25 before fees. It pays S$9,945 in a normal coupon year, compared with S$5,000 OA base interest. Full par repayment plus that residual is S$398,071.25 at the same FX.

The income comparison remains favourable at each of the seven FX rates requested. Even at 1.20, annual coupons are S$9,360 and redemption capital plus residual is S$374,671.25. The investment therefore does not require a stronger USD to work within this tested range.

I would describe the bequest carefully. Before redemption, my family inherits a bond at its market value with future payment rights. It does not automatically receive the 2070 face value on my death. If my family needs immediate SGD certainty, that changes the decision.

GIC's global approach supports thinking beyond domestic cash, but it does not guarantee USD. CPF reaches global investments through the Government and GIC; GIC's USD reporting and regional allocations do not prove a floor under USD/SGD. MAS's objective is price stability through a currency basket.

My position: I would consider the bond for withdrawable money genuinely intended for long-term income and legacy, after checking the all-in price, fees and family liquidity needs. I would retain enough SGD certainty for expenses and obligations that cannot wait. The bond's higher income is meaningful; the different risks must be acceptable too.

Scope. This is a personal investment thought supported by conditional gross calculations, not a claim that one asset is suitable for every CPF member. The current CPF floor, future exchange rates and issuer payment assumptions are explicitly separated throughout the report.
