Risk diagram

Assignment obligation, broker margin and discretionary downside reserve are different quantities and must not be added together as though they were the same thing.

Eight short puts → 800 shares on full assignment → US$484,500 combined strike purchase obligation.

A hypothetical additional US$50 fall per assigned share → 800 × US$50 = US$40,000 further share-price loss.

Why close early?

The six puts captured 28.84% to 46.38% of their starting credit in approximately 27 to 50 hours. Buying them back crystallised US$417 of gross put gains and ended those positions' future assignment exposure. Pursuing the remaining US$719 of put premium would have kept downside and margin risk open.

The US$805 call was a separate, later trade and captured only 12.08% of its starting credit. It opened after the puts had closed. It must not be described as part of a simultaneous short-strangle position, and an uncovered short call can have unlimited upside loss.

Margin and evidence limits

The exact return on margin cannot be established from these screenshots. Historical peak broker buying-power reduction is missing. Do not use the earlier ten-put proposal's buying-power figure for the different executed trades, and do not add the later call's requirement to the puts' peak as though they overlapped.

These listed trades were already closed at the review date. Hypothetical hold-to-expiry figures are counterfactuals, not outstanding positions or promises of further income. Gross P/L excludes fees, financing and taxes not identifiable in the screenshots. Account balances and raw broker screenshots are not included in this public summary.
