Historical market events | A source-linked tree for rates, bonds, FX and portfolio context

CENTRAL CONCLUSION

Build one reusable event library, with a hierarchical tree for browsing and independent tags for filtering. Keep each dated milestone connected to its source and to an honest data frequency.

  1. Group by mechanism, not by headline alone. A primary folder helps navigation; it is not proof that an event had only one cause.
  2. Keep episodes and milestones separate. A multi-year crisis is not a one-day announcement.
  3. Use more than one time scale. Monthly averages explain context; daily observations expose abrupt moves.
  4. Show facts, interpretation and gaps separately. A marker beside a price move does not establish causation.
  5. Implement in stages. Start with verified records and source links before adding rankings, filters or portfolio simulations.

Scope: selected episodes from 1929 to April 2025. This is a historical research and design report, not a current market bulletin, trading recommendation or deployed chart feature. No client information is used.

EVIDENCE KEY

REPORTED = source-backed historical statement. ANALYSIS = interpretation or design recommendation. MISSING = unavailable observation, never zero. A source link is evidence for the stated claim, not a blanket endorsement of everything written nearby.

01 • INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Use five levels, but store each fact only once

The tree is the route into the library, not the database itself.

A level-five annotation should reference a milestone ID, not copy its facts. The same policy decision can appear on a gilt-yield chart and an FX chart, while its date and source remain maintained in one place. Correct the underlying record once and every linked view can update.

One episode can contain different kinds of milestone. Trigger, market dislocation, institution failure, policy announcement, policy effective date and programme end are separate types. Do not force a “recovery” marker unless recovery is defined for a particular series and horizon.

Sources: S30. Design interpretation is this report’s recommendation.

A price fall does not identify its own mechanism. A valuation reset can occur without a funding run. A financing constraint can force a sale even when an investor continues to believe the asset is worth more. The catalogue should make those questions visible rather than assign every episode a generic crash label.

KEEP SECONDARY PATHS SEARCHABLE

An episode placed under liquidity can still carry equity, FX, policy and regional tags. The primary path is an editorial route into the evidence, not an exclusion of other channels.

Link neighbouring episodes without merging them. The 1929 crash and the early-1930s banking panics are related but distinct records. Likewise, COVID-19 is a broad episode with a market-liquidity phase; it does not need a duplicate copy in every branch.

Coverage should expand without breaking the IDs. Japan’s asset-price collapse, the 1994 bond sell-off, the 2010 flash crash and Brexit are sensible future additions, but are not presented as source-audited entries in this starter catalogue.

PRACTICAL RULE

Every episode has one primary path. Secondary mechanisms and related episodes are links or tags. A search result must deduplicate by the canonical episode ID, not by the visible title.

Follow the workbook’s actual matching order. Validate context first, choose the longest valid whole phrase, then use priority for a genuine equal-span conflict. Do not match “US” inside “USD” or “Fed” inside “FedEx”. Ambiguous aliases remain unresolved until the context establishes identity.

Keep direction separate from identity. A bond term can remain Bonds Green while its price falls. A yield statistic uses Rates Blue rather than automatically inheriting the bond-instrument colour. Evidence uncertainty uses Status Gray, not a loss signal.

05 • DATE AND EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE

A historical event has more than one relevant date

A precise-looking timestamp is not useful when it describes the wrong thing.

Date fieldMeaningExample or rule
Event / announcementWhen the development was announced or occurredFed decision: 16 March 2022
Effective dateWhen the operational change took effectTarget range: 17 March 2022
Market observationWhen the displayed price or rate was observedH.10: New York noon, not session close
Publication dateWhen the source was releasedSVB review: 28 April 2023
Retrieval dateWhen the source was checked for this report5 November 2026
Date precisionDay, month, year or broader periodS&L crisis: multi-year episode
Display / study windowChosen analytical period around an anchor12 months before and after, where available

Preserve local time and the trading calendar. Store an exact time and time zone only when supported. A Sunday announcement can influence Monday trading; after-close announcements can belong economically to the next session. Date-only records should remain date-only, not silently become midnight UTC.

A monthly label is not an executable price at the start of the month. FRED’s monthly Treasury observations are averages. The month label should be displayed as a period, and an event-month band should not pretend to identify the announcement-day reaction.

07 • COMPARATIVE CASE: AFC AND LTCM

Currency mismatch and forced selling are different risks

Two episodes can damage many markets without sharing the same balance-sheet weakness.

The borrowing rate is only one part of a financed position. For analytical comparison, inspect currency mismatch, refinancing dates, collateral rules and how quickly assets can be sold. These are distinct from an asset’s expected long-term return; a favourable return thesis does not eliminate a near-term funding constraint.

A policy response does not guarantee protection for every investor. The LTCM recapitalisation was private-sector funded with New York Fed facilitation. Do not code it as a direct Fed capital injection, and do not turn an historical intervention into an assumed rescue rule for future portfolios.

CHART ACTION

Use linked but separate panels: asset price or spread, funding conditions, FX and policy milestones. Avoid compressing all four into an unlabeled “crisis severity” number.

Sources: S10, S11.

08 • WORKED CHART: GLOBAL FINANCIAL CRISIS

GFC yields fell overall, but not in a straight line

A monthly view gives context around September 2008; it does not isolate Lehman’s daily effect.

ObservationYieldWhat the comparison does and does not show
September 20074.52%Context 12 months before the event month
September 20083.69%Average contains observations before and after Lehman
October 20083.81%Higher than September despite crisis stress
December 20082.42%Later lower yield; not a single-day event return
September 20093.40%12-month-after context, not a crisis-end definition

The intermediate path matters. September to October 2008 shows a rise of 12 basis points, even though the broader period includes a large decline. A two-point “before versus after” comparison can conceal the sequence that matters to an investor facing collateral calls or an urgent sale.

The milestone layer must remain distinct from the data. September 15 marks Lehman’s filing; September 16 marks AIG support; December 16 marks the Fed’s 0–0.25% target-range announcement. These do not convert a monthly yield chart into a causal estimate.

09 • WORKED CHART: TAPER TANTRUM

Markets can reprice a policy before it happens

Communication, implementation and market anticipation belong in different fields.

OBSERVED CHANGE

May 2013: 1.93%. September 2013: 2.81%. Difference = (2.81 − 1.93) × 100 = +88 basis points. This is a monthly-average yield change, not an 88% move and not a bond return.

The useful event is a sequence of communications. The Fed’s retrospective research identifies May testimony and June communication as important to the market’s interpretation of future tapering. The chart should not label the May milestone “QE ended”, because that would confuse expectations with implementation.

The same announcement can transmit through several assets. A Treasury chart shows one channel; emerging-market currencies and funding conditions show others. The classification belongs under policy communication, with FX and region tags available as secondary filters rather than duplicate event entries.

Analytical chain: communication → revised expectations → market repricing. Neither the direction nor the size of the reaction is assumed for every announcement.

Sources: S20, S36.

11 • WORKED CHART: SWISS FRANC, DAILY VIEW

FX quote direction changes the liability calculation

Use the quote’s units before interpreting the sign of a return.

Exact calculation, 14 to 15 JanuaryResult
CHF per USD: (0.8930 / 1.0172 − 1) × 100−12.209988…%
USD value of a fixed CHF amount: (1.0172 / 0.8930 − 1) × 100+13.908174…%
SGD per CHF uses (SGD per USD) / (CHF per USD)Cross-rate, not subtraction

The reciprocal is the important distinction. Let L be an unchanged CHF liability and q be CHF per USD. Its USD equivalent is L ÷ q. Comparing the two sourced observations gives a 13.91% increase in that USD equivalent, while the quoted USD/CHF rate falls 12.21%. Display values are rounded; the formula preserves the exact inputs.

This is an exchange-rate translation, not a portfolio simulation. No loan amount, margin requirement, financing cost or asset gain is assumed. Liquidation risk requires those inputs and the lender’s contractual rules.

WHAT THE CHART CANNOT SHOW

The H.10 snapshot does not measure the worst intraday executable rate, spread widening or a particular investor’s fill. Do not substitute it for a stress-test loss estimate.

12 • WORKED CHART: COVID-19

A falling monthly yield does not prove daily liquidity

Broad yield direction and market functioning are different questions.

Monthly averages are insufficient for a liquidity claim. To examine a dash-for-cash episode, add daily or intraday observations, bid–ask spreads and funding indicators where reliable data are available. This report deliberately does not invent an intramonth yield path from monthly values.

Keep the response separate from the trigger. The Sunday Fed statement is a policy milestone within a broader health and economic episode. It should not be coded as the date COVID-19 began, nor should later easing be treated as the sole explanation for each market movement.

The complete sequence matters. Ending the narrative in May 2023 misses the later rise to 5.25–5.50%. The difference in upper bounds is 5.50 − 0.25 = 5.25 percentage points, or 525 basis points. This historical endpoint says nothing about the current policy rate.

Do not mechanically translate policy tightening into a bond return. A specific bond’s outcome depends on its yield curve, coupon, maturity, spread, cash flows and holding period. A funding rate can also reset on a different schedule from the asset’s income.

18 • EVENT-STUDY METHOD

Measure changes honestly before discussing causes

A before/during/after panel should answer a defined question, not choose the most dramatic endpoints.

MeasureCalculation or ruleDo not confuse it with
Yield change(Ending yield % − starting yield %) × 100 = basis pointsBond price return
Price return(Ending price / starting price − 1) × 100Total return including distributions
Total returnUse a valid total-return series or include cash-flow reinvestment explicitlyA price-only line
FX translationWrite units first; invert the quote where necessarySymmetric percentage changes
Study windowDefault context: 12 months before / after where available; add daily zoomA claim that the event caused the entire window
Benchmark comparisonUse aligned periods and define the comparator in advanceA controlled causal estimate

There is no single clean “event effect” in these descriptive charts. Expectations can change beforehand, other news arrives during the window, and policy responds to the same deterioration affecting prices. A descriptive overlay is valuable precisely when it makes these limitations visible rather than dressing them up as a model result.

Do not fill a long data gap just to complete the picture. DGS10 does not provide a 1929 daily history. DGS30 was discontinued in February 2002 and reintroduced in February 2006. Missing observations should remain gaps, with an alternative series introduced only under a separate label and methodology.

Point-in-time analysis needs point-in-time information. A historical backtest should use information available then, not revised macro data or an ex-post list of winning events. ALFRED provides vintages for suitable series; eligibility and release timing still need to be checked for each dataset.

21 • STRATEGY, RISKS AND NEXT ACTIONS

The result should improve judgement, not manufacture certainty

The best historical overlay answers “what was happening?” before it claims “why did it move?”.

UseValueMain limitation
Investment discussionExplain mechanisms behind a period of stressHistorical similarity does not establish the next outcome
Risk reviewSeparate rates, credit, FX, funding and liquidityA chart cannot replace contractual margin and cash-flow inputs
Research navigationReach official evidence from the plotted dateOfficial sources can be retrospective and incomplete
Product designReuse one library across several chart typesThe taxonomy still needs editorial ownership
RECOMMENDED FIRST RELEASE

Start with the six demonstrated contexts: the GFC, taper tantrum, CHF shock, COVID-19, Fed tightening and UK gilt stress. Retain the wider 26-episode catalogue as searchable context, with coverage and data availability shown explicitly.

Use history as a set of mechanisms, not as a set of promises. A funding mismatch, a policy-regime change and a valuation correction require different questions even when each is followed by a falling chart. The tree is valuable because it helps the reader distinguish them.

The most important risk is false precision. Exact-looking dates, return figures and recovery bands can be misleading when they mix periods, frequencies or definitions. This report preserves month and year precision, leaves unavailable inputs blank and identifies analytical choices rather than presenting them as historical facts.

The immediate action is data integration, not a visual redesign of everything. Inspect the existing chart interface, map its series IDs to the research starter, and validate a small read-only overlay. Add deeper comparisons only after source links, dates, units and mobile behaviour pass review.

Source → mechanism → decision

Ask what is documented, how it could transmit and which exposure should be checked. That sequence makes the historical library useful without turning it into a forecast.
