Three different ways to own bond risk

  • A bond fund is not a bond held to maturity. A rolling portfolio does not promise repayment of your purchase price on a single maturity date.
  • Income paid out is not the same as investment return. Compare the change in investment value plus distributions, after applicable costs.
  • A currency hedge does not remove bond-price risk. Interest rates, credit spreads, fees and the terms of the hedge still matter.
  • Temasek debt is not Singapore government debt. The issuer explicitly states that its debt is not guaranteed by the Singapore Government. [4]

Verified contractual fact

Temasek Series 21 has a 2.50% annual coupon. For each US$100 face value, that corresponds to US$2.50 annual coupon cash before tax or costs, subject to the note terms and issuer performance. This is arithmetic from the official coupon, not a current yield quotation. [3]

Pricing principle

The purchase price changes the yield. TreasuryDirect explains the relationship between a fixed coupon, the price paid and yield to maturity. A bond bought below face value does not thereby have a larger contractual coupon. [2]

Original analysis: yield to maturity is a discounted-cash-flow measure, not a promise of the realised compounded return. Early sale, reinvestment terms, default, costs and currency conversion can change the investor’s outcome.

Separate income, capital and currency effects

Fund cash distributions

Distributions alone do not establish profit. A fall in NAV can offset cash paid out. A proper historical reconciliation needs dates, units held, distributions and the reinvestment convention. Restricted historical series have not been republished or reconstructed.

Full-hedge assumption

Under a perfectly matched full hedge, USD spot gains and losses on the hedged amount are offset. This is an idealised explanatory assumption, not a claim about any share class. Hedge carry, roll dates, changing portfolio values and implementation costs still affect results.

No “net yield” shortcut is endorsed. Subtracting a single assumed annual hedge charge and one fee from a portfolio yield does not establish a realised SGD return. Share-class expenses, turnover, defaults, derivatives, timing and the precise yield definition must be reconciled first.
