Two holds, different policy settings

MAS keeps an appreciating currency path; the SNB keeps a low policy rate. The distinction helps explain CHF/SGD, but does not determine its next move.

Latest verified snapshotSingaporeSwitzerland

The central issue is the inflation process, not which country has the lower interest rate. Singapore is leaning against imported costs through the exchange rate. Switzerland can retain a low policy rate while its conditional inflation outlook remains consistent with price stability. Both economies are expanding, but neither country's labour market can be reduced to its headline GDP number.

The bilateral currency conclusion is conditional. The SNB documented franc weakness and carry-trade incentives; MAS retained the currency path strengthened earlier in the year. Together these are consistent with downward pressure on CHF/SGD. They do not establish a precise contribution to a particular historical FX return or rule out a reversal.

Read the dates and definitions with every value. The unemployment measures and core inflation measures above are not harmonised. October Swiss CPI and September Singapore CPI were released after the respective policy decisions.

The MAS report matching your request is the Macroeconomic Review. Its October edition uses information available by 13 October, unless otherwise stated. The Swiss counterpart combines a monetary-policy report with company interviews in the Quarterly Bulletin. The 22 October SNB discussion summary explains the September decision; it is not a new October rate decision. [MAS1, SNB1, SNB5]

A forecast belongs to a forecaster and a publication date. The SNB's inflation projection assumes its policy rate stays constant across the horizon. SECO's government forecast is a separate exercise. MAS inflation projections and the ministry's GDP forecast should also retain their identities. A later data release can change our assessment without changing the historical policy decision.

This report uses an information-set approach. First ask what each central bank knew, then what changed, and finally whether the new evidence threatens its explanation. This avoids judging a September decision as though October inflation were already available.

PUBLIC EDITION

Read CHF/SGD in the right direction

CHF/SGD means Singapore dollars per Swiss franc. A lower exchange rate reduces the SGD value of a fixed CHF asset and a fixed CHF liability. Interest, fees, hedges and collateral are separate.

Two tools for the same objective

Both seek price stability. MAS manages a currency basket; the SNB primarily sets a short-term policy rate and can also intervene in FX.

Policy featureMASSNB
Main settingS$NEER slope, centre and widthSNB policy rate
Operational transmissionTrade-weighted currency conditionsMoney-market rates, wider financing conditions and CHF
FX roleCore monetary-policy instrumentAdditional instrument when needed
What is not targetedA fixed CHF/SGD levelA guaranteed CHF/SGD floor or ceiling

MAS chooses the exchange rate because imported prices matter greatly. Its framework explains that imports account for almost 40 cents of each dollar spent domestically. A stronger trade-weighted SGD lowers local-currency import costs; effects reach consumer prices with lags. Domestic interest rates respond largely to international rates and currency expectations. SORA is therefore not an MAS-set policy rate. [FW1]

The SNB considers both rates and the franc. Its strategy defines price stability as CPI inflation below 2% a year while also rejecting sustained deflation. It looks through some temporary shocks rather than reacting mechanically to every monthly number. The constant-rate projection is a diagnostic exercise, not forward guidance promising an unchanged rate. [SNB6]

September brought a change in conditions without a rate change. Compared with June, the SNB raised its annual inflation forecasts by 0.1 percentage point for 2026, 0.2 for 2027 and 0.1 for 2028. Oil prices and the weaker franc explained the upward pressure. Its 2026 growth forecast rose from around 1% to 1.5-2%, while 2027 remained around 1.5%. [SNB3, SNB4]

The intervention language also matters. March and June stressed heightened readiness to counter excessive CHF appreciation. By the September discussion, heightened readiness was no longer judged necessary, although intervention remained available. MASSingaporeInflationEnergyANALYSIS

Singapore faces imported cost pressure

Energy and food costs push upward, while productivity, subsidies and competition restrain domestic services prices.

“MAS Core Inflation eased to 2.0% year-on-year (y-o-y) in September, from 2.2% in August.”

MAS / METI, September CPI release, 23 October 2026 · SG1

Energy reaches consumers with a lag. July's electricity-tariff increase reflected natural-gas prices during the first two and a half months of the previous quarter. Electricity and gas inflation moved from -2.9% in June to 8.7% in July. A temporary improvement in spot energy prices therefore need not immediately remove pressure from household bills. [SG2]

Domestic costs do not mechanically follow headline wage growth. MAS reported resident average monthly earnings growth of 3.3% in Q1 and 4.0% in Q2, while economy-wide unit labour cost growth averaged 0.3% in H1. Its analysis points to productivity, healthcare subsidies and telecommunications competition as restraints on persistent inflation. [MAS1]

Policy analysis: the relevant test is persistence. A one-off energy shock changes relative prices. It becomes harder to look through when wage settlements, margins, rents and expectations propagate it. The appreciating currency path directly addresses import prices, while the labour-cost evidence helps MAS judge whether a wider inflation process is taking hold.

MAS core excludes accommodation and private transport. It does not exclude all food and energy. Treating it as a standard ex-food-and-energy index would misread Singapore's inflation process.

The earlier rise was concentrated in imports. The Bulletin's May-to-August comparison shows domestic inflation unchanged while imported prices accelerated. Its August TM15 core measure was 0.6%. October's release identifies higher oil-product, footwear and hospital outpatient prices, with declines in other medical services, travel packages and hotels. [SNB1, CH7]

Policy analysis: 1.5% does not itself establish a need to hike. The SNB must judge the duration and breadth of the increase. A persistent oil shock plus currency weakness could lift underlying inflation; an energy reversal or renewed franc appreciation could do the opposite. The useful question is whether later data shift the medium-term forecast enough to change the policy judgment.

The annual-average forecast and one month are different objects. October CPI of 1.5% y/y is not a direct contradiction of September's 0.7% forecast for the average of 2026. The annual average includes earlier months. It may nevertheless provide evidence that the next forecast needs revision; that is an assessment to make with the complete release history.

Why Switzerland often has low inflation

Currency behaviour and price formation matter; differences in consumption weights alone do not explain the entire Swiss inflation advantage.

EvidenceWhat it supportsWhat it cannot prove
SNB March discussion: lower energy exposureAn energy shock can affect Switzerland differentlyImmunity to a prolonged energy shock
SNB research: smaller price rises drive most of the gapActual product-price behaviour mattersA permanent gap versus Singapore
Safe-haven demand for CHFInvestors may accept lower returns for perceived safetyCHF appreciates in every crisis
Currency pass-throughAppreciation can restrain import pricesA fixed bilateral FX outcome

The structural explanation is more nuanced than a small energy basket. A September SNB research note compares Switzerland with the euro area over the preceding 15 years. Goods, especially food and energy, account for two-thirds of the inflation gap; services account for the remaining third. Smaller price increases explain more than different consumption weights. This is evidence about the euro area comparison, not a measured decomposition against Singapore. [SNB8]

Exchange-rate credibility can lower required nominal returns. The SNB describes investors' willingness to hold CHF for safety despite lower yields. Currency appreciation can then dampen imported inflation. Its March discussion also noted Switzerland's lower energy exposure in consumption and manufacturing. These channels help explain why a large foreign policy-rate gap can coexist with credible Swiss price stability. [SNB7, SNB9]

Analysis: distinguish a long-run property from a short-run trade. Persistent low inflation can support a currency's purchasing power over long periods, while an unfavourable interest differential weakens it during a particular carry-trade episode. These statements operate on different horizons and need not conflict. Neither is a guarantee about the next quarter.

Net employment expansion strengthened, while retrenchments and resident unemployment still warrant attention.

More jobs do not mean every worker is better matched. Q3 employment expansion was predominantly nonresident, especially in construction and manufacturing. Resident gains concentrated in essential and public services. The resident/nonresident numerical split was not yet published in the advance release. [SG3]

Two Q2 indicators reveal adjustment costs. Vacancies fell from 73,300 to 68,600, and the six-month re-entry rate for retrenched residents fell from 60.7% to 54.9%. The twelve-month re-entry rate was broadly stable. The pattern points to longer job searches rather than a simple collapse in labour demand. [SG4]

Policy analysis: aggregate demand is only part of the problem. Skills mismatches, sectoral restructuring and ageing can coexist with strong GDP. A broad monetary easing may not solve these frictions and could worsen imported inflation. The policy response therefore depends on whether weakness is broad and cyclical or concentrated and structural.

Seasonality changes the October diagnosis. SECO reported 141,015 registered unemployed, 535 more than September, but its seasonally adjusted count fell by 1,328 to 145,949. Both published rates were unchanged. The raw rise alone is not evidence of an unambiguous monthly deterioration. [CH8]

Survey unemployment has wider coverage than registration. The FSO's Q2 ILO rate was 4.9% NSA, versus 4.6% a year earlier. Employed persons increased 0.8% y/y. Policy analysis: the labour market leaves room for a patient SNB. Hiring recovery does not establish a nationwide wage-price spiral. Equally, pockets of slack do not justify ignoring a persistent import-price shock. Evaluate the breadth of hiring, unemployment duration, pay settlements and price-setting behaviour alongside the headline rate.

Swiss registered rates and Singapore survey rates are not an apples-to-apples ranking. The Swiss ILO series is conceptually closer, but population coverage, adjustments and observation periods still need alignment.

Wages need a productivity denominator

The policy signal is the cost of producing a unit of output, not wage growth viewed in isolation.

ObservationEvidence statusPolicy interpretation
Singapore AME: 3.3% to 4.0% y/y, Q1 to Q2Published realised earnings measureSome wage acceleration
Singapore H1 unit labour cost: +0.3%MAS reported economy-wide aggregateProductivity restrains cost per unit
Swiss interview pay rises: 1.5% in 2026; 1.3% expected in 2027Company-survey indicationsContained pay intentions, not a national realised wage series

“unit labour cost growth is expected to be subdued”

MAS Macroeconomic Review, October 2026, printed p. 28 · MAS1

A higher wage can reflect higher productive capacity. If output per worker rises alongside pay, an employer need not increase selling prices by the wage-growth rate to maintain its cost position. Conversely, weak productivity makes even moderate wage gains harder to absorb. Margins and competitive pressure affect how much cost is passed to customers.

The country comparison must preserve measurement differences. Singapore's AME is not the Swiss interview measure. The first reflects earnings, including variable components; the second reports businesses' wage indications. They provide separate evidence about persistence. Subtracting one from the other would create a spurious international wage gap.

Analysis: this is why employment belongs in an inflation report. Jobs, hours, vacancies and turnover help reveal bargaining pressure. Productivity, utilisation and margins show whether that pressure becomes consumer-price inflation. The useful dashboard joins these mechanisms rather than treating unemployment as a single trigger for rate changes.

The SNB explains the franc weakness

Its September discussion links CHF depreciation to interest differentials, low volatility and limited safe-haven demand.

“there had been little safe-haven demand for the Swiss franc.”

SNB September discussion summary, published 22 October 2026 · SNB5

The direct official observation is trade-weighted. The SNB described around 3% CHF depreciation since its June assessment, wider foreign-versus-Swiss interest differentials and low currency volatility. It judged that currency depreciation more than offset higher Swiss yields, easing overall monetary conditions. The reported 3% is the SNB's stated observation, not a reconstructed bilateral return. [SNB5]

Analysis: carry demand is strongest when the funding currency looks stable. An investor who borrows CHF and buys a higher-yielding foreign asset sells CHF initially. A wider rate gap improves the interest differential before fees and hedging. Lower perceived currency risk can encourage the position. This describes a mechanism, not evidence of the size of any individual investor's trade.

The reversal mechanism is equally important. If volatility rises or the financed asset loses value, closing the position may require buying CHF. A funding currency can therefore strengthen precisely when risk assets weaken. A calm historical chart is not proof that the liability is safe. The balance between carry demand and demand for safety changes over time.

A basket is not a bilateral promise

CHF/SGD moves with both currencies. MAS can retain an appreciating basket path while SGD weakens against CHF.

Identity or conditionMeaning
CHF/SGD = (USD/SGD) / (USD/CHF)SGD per USD divided by CHF per USD gives SGD per CHF
CHF strengthens faster than SGDCHF/SGD can rise even if both appreciate against USD
SGD strengthens faster than CHFCHF/SGD can fall even if both appreciate against USD
MAS holds an appreciating S$NEER pathThe basket path remains positive; no bilateral guarantee

The economic comparison is relative. A view on USD/SGD alone is not a complete CHF/SGD view. Use matching timestamps, price conventions and sources for both legs of a cross-rate calculation. Dividing observations from different times can produce a number that never represented an executable cross rate.

Interest differentials also affect forward pricing. A hedged foreign-currency return includes forward points and transaction costs. It cannot generally be assessed by subtracting only the visible cash borrowing rate from the foreign asset yield. An unhedged position retains currency risk; a hedged position changes the economics. No current forward-price quote is assumed here.

Analysis: evaluate the whole set of possible paths. The most helpful decomposition is SGD policy support, CHF monetary conditions, global risk appetite and the two currencies' responses to external rates. Assigning the entire cross-rate move to one central bank obscures the other currency and encourages overconfident forecasts.

No daily FX history or October rebound attribution has been fabricated. Screenshot values retain their displayed precision; they are not labelled as a current executable dealing price.

Implications for CHF-funded portfolios

Keep currency translation, financing cost and asset performance separate when applying the macro view.

The financing rate is contractual. A 0% SNB policy rate is not a 0% client loan. A facility depends on its benchmark, spread, tenor, reset date and other terms. The low policy rate also says nothing by itself about collateral recognition, margin requirements or currency losses. No current OCBC loan pricing has been assumed.

Apply an exposure map before an interest-rate view. Identify the asset's currency, the liability's currency and the currency in which future expenses are paid. Then measure each leg. If NAV is already net of CHF debt, do not subtract the debt a second time. Treat cash income, asset-price P&L, FX translation and loan interest as separate lines.

Historical perspective: policy regimes can change abruptly. The SNB ended its EUR/CHF minimum exchange rate on 15 January 2015. That is not a prediction of a repeat, but it shows why a central-bank preference cannot be treated as a permanent protection for an unhedged liability. [H1]

Action: use the macro report to select stresses, then use actual facility inputs. Review the effect of simultaneous asset weakness and CHF strength, available liquidity for margin calls and the timing of cash needs. A personal loan or portfolio calculation would require the exact amounts and contract terms; this report supplies no invented principal, spread or return.

Method, limits and final judgment

The evidence supports a relative-policy explanation for recent CHF weakness. It does not support a guaranteed FX path.

The strongest interpretation combines the two countries. MAS is countering imported inflation with an appreciating currency path. The SNB has judged low rates compatible with medium-term price stability, while the franc's weakening has itself eased financial conditions. Employment evidence in both countries argues for attention to composition and productivity rather than a mechanical reaction to strong GDP.

The most important new challenge is Swiss inflation persistence. October's higher headline reading raises the value of watching underlying prices and the next conditional forecast. Singapore's September easing is encouraging but does not remove the pipeline from energy and food costs. These developments narrow the case for extrapolating a historical CHF/SGD decline without reassessment.

The practical stance is conditional and auditable. Use the policy divergence as a research hypothesis. Define the counter-evidence, maintain separate financing and FX calculations, and update the view when the relevant information changes. A lower borrowing rate is a funding attribute, not a complete investment thesis.
