CPF LIFE / A clear explanation

Savings become income for life

CPF LIFE converts retirement savings into monthly income that continues while you live. To judge it fairly, separate the earlier savings growth, the money paid back over time, and the protection against living longer than your savings last.

Five points to keep in mind

  1. Before payouts, the Retirement Account earns interest. In the published example, S$220,400 at 55 becomes an estimated S$330,100 at 65.
  2. Early payouts reduce the remaining death refund. While that refund is positive, income received plus the refund equals the starting premium in this Standard Plan model.
  3. The payout rate is different from an investment return. A 6.47% annual payout ratio does not mean every dollar earns 6.47% a year.
  4. Lifetime protection begins when you join. It remains valuable even before the premium refund runs out.
  5. Compare the complete retirement outcome. Income, access to capital, money for beneficiaries and purchasing power all matter.

Reference case, not a personal quote: a male member turning 55 in 2026, reaching 65 in 2036. CPF's table was updated 16 Jun 2026. Calculations use one S$330,100 premium, S$1,780 each month, no later inflows, and a first payout one month after 65. Actual payouts can be adjusted. [S02] [S11]

01 / START WITH THE PURPOSE

Three stages answer three different questions

An annuity turns a sum of money into regular payments. CPF LIFE is a life annuity: its payments continue while the member lives. The Retirement Account, or RA, holds retirement savings before those savings are committed as the premium. A premium is the amount used to buy the cover. [S03] [S15]

02 / INCOME AND THE DEATH REFUND

A payout and a refund are two parts of the same premium

The remaining premium refund is what is left after payouts have been deducted. For this single-premium Standard Plan example, it equals S$330,100 minus payouts received, with a minimum of zero. Other CPF balances may also pass to beneficiaries; this report's model includes only the premium refund. [S06]

CALCULATION · WHAT “0%” MEANS

Zero on this cash-flow measure has a precise meaning

At age 80:
S$320,400 income + S$9,700 refund
= S$330,100 premium

The household has received its premium back in nominal dollars. “Household return” here includes cash paid to the member and the refund paid to beneficiaries at the selected death age. While their total equals the initial premium, the nominal annualised return from age 65 is 0%.

This does not mean the premium earns no interest. CPF LIFE premiums continue earning interest. That interest is pooled across members and factored into the income set from the beginning; it does not build a separate, fully inheritable interest account.

The insurance protects a risk that an early-death return cannot measure. A member does not know in advance whether income will be needed to 80, 90 or beyond. Lifetime coverage is already in place while the refund remains positive.

In plain English: the early cash flows look like the premium being returned a little at a time. The policy also promises that the payments will keep coming if the member lives much longer.

Sources: CPF refund definition, 9 Jul 2026; premium-interest FAQ, 10 Feb 2025; pooling article published 6 Sep 2024 and updated 23 Apr 2026. Calculations are this report's analysis. [S06] [S21] [S07]

WHAT THE REFUND CANNOT DO

A death benefit is different from accessible cash

ItemWhat it represents
Payouts already receivedIncome delivered over time; it may have been spent.
Remaining refundA benefit payable on death, not a freely withdrawable investment account.
Accessible assetsMoney available for living expenses or emergencies, subject to each asset's terms.

How to read: keep these categories separate. Adding past income to a death refund does not tell you how much cash is available today.

03 / MEASURE THE RIGHT RETURN

The payout ratio and the investment return answer different questions

A payout ratio measures cash paid against capital committed. An internal rate of return, or IRR, also considers when money is paid and what comes back at the end. The report calculates an effective annual IRR from monthly cash flows.

04 / MATCH THE MONEY AND THE INCOME

An alternative must fund the same spending

Compare equal starting capital and equal withdrawals. A bond coupon, a fund distribution and a CPF LIFE payout can all arrive as cash, but they can leave very different amounts of capital and different risks behind.

MATHEMATICAL HURDLE · NOT A PRODUCT OFFER

Preserving all the capital requires more return

S$1,780 each month, indefinitely,
while preserving S$330,100:
6.67% net effective annual return

This is the smooth-return hurdle for a perpetual nominal income stream. The formula is (1 + 1,780 ÷ 330,100)12 − 1. It assumes the monthly return can be earned continuously without loss or interruption.

The formula does not create an available safe investment. Real portfolios have fluctuating returns, fees and reinvestment risk. Even preserving the original number of dollars leaves those dollars exposed to inflation.

How to read: the 6.67% is a comparison requirement, not a forecast or expected return. Inputs [S02]; monthly perpetuity calculation, 6 Nov 2026.

05 / WHAT COULD CHANGE THE CHOICE?

Plan for long life, rising prices and money needed sooner

There is more than one retirement objective. A household may need dependable monthly income, an emergency reserve, protection against rising prices and an intended inheritance. The suitable mix depends on the size and urgency of each need.

COUNTERARGUMENTS · WHAT WEAKENS A SIMPLE “MORE IS BETTER” VIEW

More lifetime cover is not every household's first need

  • Existing lifetime income may already cover essentials. Additional flexibility or estate value may then matter more.
  • Early-death opportunity cost is real. Recovering the same nominal premium over time is different from earning a positive return on accessible assets.
  • Level payments lose purchasing power. A larger nominal payout does not remove inflation exposure.
  • Investment alternatives have their own risks. Market losses, poor early returns, reinvestment, fees and restrictive surrender terms can reduce the income available.

What changes the conclusion: the actual quotation, other dependable income, liquidity needs, family objectives, inflation and the guarantees available from alternatives. No bull/bear targets or probabilities are invented for these qualitative choices.

Evidence and analysis: CPF payout-adjustment rules and MoneySense's annuity guidance; portfolio and inflation implications are this report's analysis. [S11] [S15]

A useful question is: how much dependable income will this money provide, for how long, with what access to capital and what remaining benefit for the family?

The formulas and the opportunity cost

The refund formula is specific to the modelled single Standard premium. Let P be the S$330,100 premium, M the S$1,780 monthly payout, and n the number of months after 65.

Cumulative income Cₙ = n × M
Remaining refund Bₙ = max(P − Cₙ, 0)
Total cash delivered = Cₙ + Bₙ = max(P, Cₙ)

IRR uses the dates as well as the amounts. The monthly rate i is the rate that makes the discounted value of the payments and terminal refund equal the initial outlay. Annual IRR is (1 + i)12 − 1.

From age 65:
P = Σ[k=1…n] M / (1+i)ᵏ + Bₙ / (1+i)ⁿ

From age 55:
220,400 = Σ[k=1…n] M / (1+i)^(120+k) + Bₙ / (1+i)^(120+n)

At age 65 itself, an age-65 IRR is undefined. No time has elapsed. For positive elapsed periods before premium exhaustion, the household IRR is exactly 0%. Income-only IRR omits the refund. At the first month its annualised value is extremely close to −100%; stored floating-point precision rounds it to −1.0.

A required return makes opportunity cost explicit. Net present value, or NPV, discounts future cash flows at a chosen hurdle rate and subtracts the premium. A positive figure means the selected cash flows exceed that hurdle, not that the outcome is guaranteed.

The same-income portfolio grows before withdrawing. Its monthly rate is (1 + annual net rate)1/12 − 1. When the balance cannot fund the full withdrawal, the final payment is reduced and the shortfall is recorded. Annual chart points also include the exact first-shortfall month.

Terms used in this report
Retirement Account (RA)
The CPF account holding retirement savings before they are committed as the annuity premium.
Premium
The amount committed to buy the annuity's payment promise.
Bequest / death refund
A benefit paid to beneficiaries on death. Here the calculation isolates the remaining CPF LIFE premium refund.
IRR
An annualised return calculated from the money paid in, money paid out and their timing.
Nominal / real
Nominal means before allowing for inflation. Real means adjusted for purchasing power.
Longevity risk
The risk that life, and the need for income, lasts longer than the savings available to fund it.
Sequence risk
The risk that poor investment returns early in retirement cause lasting harm while withdrawals continue.
Liquidity
How readily an asset can provide spendable money while its owner is alive.
