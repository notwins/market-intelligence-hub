# Research and build brief: CPF LIFE after 55

## Working title
**The next dollar after 55: CPF LIFE top-up, investment portfolio, or private lifetime-income policy?**

## Research question and stance
Investigate the claim that topping up CPF after age 55 is not worthwhile. Treat it as a hypothesis, not the required conclusion. Separate the value of additional lifelong SGD income from the value of liquidity, investment flexibility and inheritance. Compare the same amount of capital on the same date, and distinguish an existing CPF LIFE entitlement from the incremental effect of a new top-up.

## Source work
1. Verify the rules current at publication using CPF Board and IRAS: prevailing ERS and top-up headroom; RSTU withdrawal restrictions; cash top-up relief and its FRS boundary; MRSS eligibility; payout start and deferment rules; Standard, Basic and Escalating plans; bequests; interest pooling; and possible payout adjustments.
2. Recover relevant prior IFI and CPF research only as background. Original policy wording, personalised benefit illustrations and CPF projections outrank previous AI answers. Do not publish private client examples, screenshots or financial details.
3. For HSBC Life Indexed Flexi Income, distinguish lifetime-income benefit, policy value, surrender value and death benefit. Verify currency, minimum premium, charges, crediting floor/cap/participation rates, withdrawal consequences, income lock-in and continued income after account depletion. Do not treat an index-crediting floor as protection of net policy value. Do not claim an earlier model is insurer-reconciled unless all material formulas and schedules actually reconcile.

## Matched comparison
Use two layers:
- **Public reference:** show CPF Board's published age-55 Standard Plan examples with cohort, sex, assumptions and date. Differences between rounded examples are illustrations, not personalised top-up quotations.
- **Interactive model:** use a conspicuously labelled synthetic scenario until a genuine incremental CPF quotation and an equivalent insurer illustration are available. Default: S$100,000 committed at age 60 on 1 November 2026; income starts at 65; assumed 4% pre-payout accumulation; hypothetical S$650 monthly payout; no tax benefit; no inflation escalation. None of these are product predictions.

Keep any product comparison blank or qualitative where the necessary source schedule is missing. Do not manufacture an IFI payout from a different entry age, currency, premium, withdrawal mode or policy version.

## Required outputs
1. A detailed, readable research article that starts with the decision and explains the mathematics without selling a universal answer.
2. A dynamic table showing age/date, nominal payout, cumulative payouts, remaining death benefit or assets, and real purchasing power.
3. Charts for cumulative income, remaining inheritance, income shortfall after portfolio depletion, and death-age-sensitive XIRR. Keep nominal receipts separate from terminal wealth.
4. Downloadable Excel with editable inputs, transparent monthly cashflows, exact-date XIRR formulas, source URLs, visible assumptions, and checks. Show both income-only and income-plus-bequest XIRR. Undefined XIRR must remain undefined, never zero by default.
5. Responsive HTML for the existing Hub, plus a short research pitch and a downloadable reusable research prompt. Keep all calculator data local in the browser, without uploading visitors' entries.

## Modelling rules
- Calculate marginal CPF benefit as the difference between matching with-top-up and without-top-up projections when those are available.
- Capital starts on the same date for every alternative. Match payout start dates, spending and SGD cashflows; include FX, fees and taxes where relevant.
- A rate of payout is not an investment return. An annuity can return capital while also providing longevity insurance.
- After payouts begin, the simplified refundable-premium balance is the starting premium minus cumulative payouts, floored at zero, only for the applicable refund-style plan assumption. This is not a Basic Plan model and is not a live surrender value.
- A portfolio must deduct withdrawals. Include terminal market value exactly once. Never count coupons/dividends twice or add a death benefit to surrender value. Include poor early returns when examining sequence risk; a constant-return path cannot establish safety.
- Put any actual tax saving on its actual receipt date. For a same-net-cost comparison, invest equivalent available cash or show the extra budget explicitly. CPF transfers are not cash-top-up tax relief.
- Report results at ages 70, 75, 80, 85, 90, 95 and 100 without assuming any one death age. XIRR is conditional on survival; it does not price the value of not running out of income.
- Use inflation scenarios, not an invented MAS inflation target. A fixed 2% escalation is not full CPI insurance.
- Confirm official member-specific inputs before any personal suitability conclusion. This is education, not a recommendation to transact.

## Editorial spine
1. What exactly is the next dollar buying?
2. What changes after 55, and what does not?
3. Why 4% interest, payout yield and lifetime XIRR answer different questions.
4. A fair same-capital comparison, including early death and very long life.
5. Liquidity, bequest, inflation, sequence risk, insurer risk and FX.
6. Where IFI may fit, with verified mechanics and missing numbers stated clearly.
7. A practical decision checklist: protect essential income, retain a cash buffer, assess marginal tax/matching benefits, then compare discretionary capital.

## Verification and publication gate

Change inputs and confirm recalculation. Check desktop/mobile readability and download links. Publish only the verified article and synthetic calculator with their labels intact. Never present the illustrative model as a CPF Board calculator or HSBC quotation.
