Overview / 01

The price of growth. The value of income.

A decision-led analysis of the JPM Guide to the Markets | U.S. 4Q 2026

This report connects the slides to portfolio decisions. It asks what the evidence says about earnings, valuation, inflation, income and the ability to remain invested through an adverse outcome. The highlighted slides form the core; selected adjacent slides explain the links between them.

The central conclusion is to separate the opportunity from the amount of risk taken. Strong AI earnings and higher bond yields can both be useful. Neither removes valuation risk, concentration, credit losses or currency exposure. A resilient implementation gives each asset a defined job and tests the conditions that would make it fail.

Read the numbers as a dated research snapshot. The source is the uploaded 71-page edition dated 30 September 2026. This report was prepared on 5 November 2026, Singapore time. It does not update the source to current market prices or represent J.P. Morgan's own investment recommendation.

Decision: Start with the investment objective, then choose the exposure, entry valuation and risk budget.

Overview / 02

Five decisions matter more than one market forecast

Executive synthesis | conclusions drawn from the supplied evidence

Stay selective within equities. The market can grow while individual stocks disappoint. The guide combines a 19.0x index forward P/E with substantial earnings expectations and a 40.6% top-ten weight. Review what is already priced in before adding another position with the same underlying exposure.

Use bonds for a defined purpose. A higher starting yield supports future income, but the source's rate-shock outcomes differ sharply by maturity. Choosing a 30-year Treasury is also a decision to tolerate large price movements. Choosing corporate credit adds a separate default and spread decision.

Monitor inflation and household stress together. Wage pressure has eased, yet core PCE remains 3.0% in the source. Aggregate household wealth looks strong while delinquency flows identify weaker borrower groups. Neither a single inflation release nor the aggregate balance sheet settles the macro outlook.

Make diversification and rebalancing operational. Geography, issuer, industry, currency and funding are separate dimensions. Document the intended mix, set review conditions and retain liquidity so that an investment thesis can survive an inconvenient market path.

Decision: Prioritise concentration, duration, credit quality, currency and liquidity in that order of review.

Method / 04

Several plausible comparisons fail unless the definitions match

Measurement discipline | the most consequential footnotes

Source data are transcribed from printed values; independent validation of every underlying vendor series is outside this report.

Price return and total return answer different questions. The rounded 12% S&P 500 figure on slide 10 is price performance; the 12.7% figure on slide 15 includes dividends. The difference is not evidence that one slide is wrong. Rounding also prevents deriving an exact dividend contribution from the two displays.

Forward, trailing and calendar-year earnings are separate measures. Top-ten earnings weight on slide 8 uses the last 12 months; its valuation uses the next 12 months. Slide 10's 2026F growth is a calendar-year forecast. Slide 15 reports next-12-month growth. Do not combine them into an exact reconciliation.

Yield is not the same as a realised return. TIPS have a real yield; most credit sectors show yield to worst; leveraged loans use a three-year takeout convention. The historical return column and the future rate scenarios refer to different calculations.

Forecast labels and source limitations travel with the number. The household debt-service estimate, AI forecasts and fiscal projections retain their labels. No unlabelled historical points are digitised, and no missing regression is reconstructed.

Decision: Check the unit, date, denominator, return basis and estimate status before drawing a comparison.

Equities / 05

An all-time high is a price observation, not an investment conclusion

Highlighted slide 4 | S&P 500 inflection points

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

The same index level can carry very different fundamentals. The source records 1,527 at the March 2000 peak and 1,565 at the October 2007 peak, but their forward P/E ratios were 25.2x and 15.1x. An index level alone tells us little about the profits being purchased or the competing yield on bonds.

The latest observation combines a higher index with a lower multiple than some earlier peaks. At 30 September 2026, the source shows 7,652 and 19.0x. That P/E is below 2000's 25.2x and January 2022's 21.4x, while the 10-year Treasury yield in the comparison table is 5.3%, versus 1.6% at the 2022 peak.

A lower relative multiple does not make the market cheap or safe. Earnings forecasts can fall and required returns can rise. Historical peaks were reached under different business mixes, margins and interest rates, so the comparisons provide context rather than a trading rule.

The useful question is whether the portfolio can absorb another drawdown. The guide illustrates substantial falls between selected turning points. Liquidity and position size determine whether an investor can remain exposed while the long-term business case develops.

Decision: Evaluate valuation and earnings together; do not exit or add solely because the index reaches a record.

Equities / 06

Valuation is more useful for return discipline than for a one-year timing call

Highlighted slide 6 | what the scatter plots can and cannot establish

Slide 6 uses monthly starting observations from 31 December 1993 and subsequent total returns.

The one-year scatter is wide at almost every valuation. In the source chart, similar starting P/E ratios precede very different one-year total returns. Earnings surprises, changes in risk appetite and macro shocks can dominate a valuation signal over that horizon.

The five-year chart displays a stronger downward association. Paying more for an earnings stream generally leaves less room for future return unless growth offsets the entry price. The chart supports disciplined long-term expectations; it does not provide an exact five-year return forecast for an investor buying at 19.0x.

The source does not provide the underlying observations or regression equation. This report therefore explains the relationship without recreating a fabricated scatter, estimating a slope by eye, or attributing a precise expected return to the red marker. Overlapping five-year observations also reduce the independence of the plotted sample.

Translate valuation into a hurdle rate and a stress test. Ask how much earnings growth is needed if the multiple stays unchanged or contracts. That question is actionable because it exposes the assumptions embedded in the purchase, even when the market's next move remains unknowable.

Decision: Use valuation to set expectations, sizing and review conditions; keep near-term timing claims modest.

Price is the product of earnings per share (EPS) and the multiple paid for them. With a consistent EPS definition, the price-return identity is (1 + EPS growth) multiplied by the ending-to-starting P/E ratio, minus one. The matrix uses the source's 19.0x starting multiple and clearly stated hypothetical changes.

A 10% earnings increase can be offset by a 10% multiple contraction. The exact result is 1.10 x 0.90 - 1 = -1%, not zero. Conversely, 10% earnings growth with a 10% expansion produces 21%. The interaction matters whenever two percentage changes are compounded.

These are conditions, not forecasts. The matrix excludes dividends, fees, taxes, currency changes and any change in the forecast earnings definition. Its purpose is to show what must happen for a valuation thesis to work, not to estimate the probability of each cell.

Separate business conviction from purchase-price discipline. An excellent company can deliver poor shareholder returns if expectations are too demanding. A research process should document both the earnings case and the multiple that can reasonably survive disappointment.

Decision: Write the earnings assumption and exit-valuation assumption separately before approving a purchase.

Equities / 08

The index is concentrated, but the valuation premium is not confined to the leaders

Supporting slide 8 | concentration, earnings and valuation

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

Ten companies account for 40.6% of index market capitalisation. They account for 36.1% of trailing earnings in the source. The 4.5 percentage-point gap signals a larger price weight than earnings weight, but it is not an exact forward-valuation decomposition because the earnings horizons differ.

The remaining companies also carry an above-average valuation. Their 17.9x forward P/E compares with a 16.0x historical average. The top ten stand at 20.9x versus 20.8x. Simply removing the largest names does not automatically remove valuation risk.

Holdings count can disguise economic concentration. An investor may own a broad U.S. fund, a technology fund and several individual leaders and still depend on the same earnings cycle. Review the combined company exposure and the common demand, spending and discount-rate drivers.

Diversification should be tested against the investment thesis that could fail. Adding a different security label is insufficient if the new asset relies on the same AI investment budgets or growth expectations. Geographic, industry and factor exposures deserve separate checks.

Decision: Look through funds and direct holdings; measure combined exposure to dominant issuers and shared drivers.

Equities / 09

Similar 2026 returns conceal a very uneven multi-year journey

Highlighted slide 10 | Magnificent 7 price performance

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

The source's rounded 2026 price returns converge at 12%. The S&P 500, the Magnificent 7 and the remaining 493 companies all show 12% year to date. That does not mean every company performed similarly, or that concentration stopped mattering. The S&P 493 is a weighted residual aggregate, not an equal-weight index.

The preceding years show large differences in both directions. The Magnificent 7 fell 40% in 2022 while the remaining group fell 12%. In 2023 the respective gains were 76% and 11%. A portfolio concentrated in the winners needed the ability to withstand the earlier losses.

Contribution share is different from group return. The guide attributes 34% of the 2026 index return to the Magnificent 7, compared with 63% in 2023. This is the group's share of the aggregate return, not its portfolio weight, and the rounded inputs should not be reverse-engineered into exact company exposures.

A good allocation must survive dispersion within the theme. The source's individual-company chart reinforces that one label does not create one return path. Size the aggregate theme first, then examine the individual company's operating and valuation risks.

Decision: Review both the upside thesis and the loss that the same exposure has historically required investors to bear.

Equities / 10

The earnings story is strong, and the forecast hurdle is high

Highlighted slide 10 | Magnificent 7 earnings growth

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

The guide forecasts a sharp 2026 earnings advantage for the Magnificent 7. Growth is shown at 57%, compared with 23% for the remaining 493 companies: a 34 percentage-point gap. Both numbers are forecasts rather than completed full-year outcomes.

Broader earnings growth still matters. The remaining group moves from 4% in 2024 and 10% in 2025 to a forecast 23% in 2026. If delivered, this would support a wider set of businesses. It does not prove that a catch-up trade will outperform, because valuations and revisions also matter.

Forecast strength raises the cost of disappointment. A company can report growing profits and still lose market value when growth falls below expectations. Review revisions, margins and cash conversion alongside the headline growth rate rather than treating a large forecast as a sufficient buy signal.

Calendar-year and next-12-month estimates must stay separate. The 2026F comparison here cannot be substituted directly into slide 15's NTM sector table. The practical approach is to compare revisions within the same estimate series and reporting period.

Decision: Monitor the change in expectations as closely as the expected growth level.

AI / 11

AI is already a large index exposure, and its buckets overlap

Highlighted slide 11 | mapping the exposure before choosing securities

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

The guide defines the composite groups and explicitly removes double counting in its total.

The source reports 53.2% unique S&P 500 exposure across its AI-related buckets. The five displayed industry weights add to 59.4%, but some hyperscalers also fall into other categories. The 6.2 percentage-point difference is the excess of the bucket sum over the deduplicated total; it is not a sixth industry. Coverage measures the index weight of companies in these industry definitions, not the share of S&P 500 revenue generated by AI.

The categories describe different business models. Hyperscalers buy infrastructure and sell computing services. Semiconductor businesses supply key equipment. Hardware, power and software participate through different demand, capacity and pricing mechanisms. Exposure to AI does not make their economics interchangeable.

Measure how revenue becomes cash before accepting the theme label. Capacity spending can support suppliers before the end-user revenue arrives. A beneficiary of construction today may face order normalisation later; an infrastructure owner must earn a return on the capital already committed.

The portfolio question is marginal exposure. A broad index position may already supply considerable participation. Adding a specialist holding should have a clear purpose, whether a differentiated earnings driver or an intentional overweight, with its concentration cost recorded.

Decision: Deduplicate overlapping holdings and assess the economic role of each AI exposure.

AI / 12

The largest earnings forecast does not automatically offer the best return

Highlighted slide 11 | price performance, earnings expectations and valuation

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

Semiconductors carry the strongest 2026 earnings-growth forecast in the slide. The figure is 109%, alongside a 44% year-to-date price gain and an 18.2x next-12-month P/E. The three numbers measure different things and should not be collapsed into a simple attractiveness score.

Other AI-linked groups show different combinations. Hyperscalers show 58% forecast earnings growth, a 7% price gain and 22.2x P/E. Software shows 21%, 4% and 25.9x. Hardware has 36% forecast growth, a 37% price gain and 27.1x P/E. These contrasts are prompts for research, not proof of mispricing.

Rapid growth can reflect a cyclical base as well as durable demand. Assess the level and quality of earnings, customer concentration, reinvestment and the persistence of margins. A low forward P/E built on unusually high expected profits may rise if those profits normalise.

Use a common time basis when comparing companies. Year-to-date prices, calendar-year growth and next-12-month valuation do not share a single horizon. A proper security comparison needs aligned earnings periods and company-level evidence beyond this industry overview.

Decision: Use the table to select research questions; validate company cash flows and estimate durability before selecting winners.

Equities / 13

Sector valuation differences matter more than a simple growth-versus-value label

Highlighted slide 15 | forward P/E against each sector's own history

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

Valuations vary materially across the index. Energy is at 13.0x forward earnings versus a 13.9x 20-year average; financials are at 14.1x versus 13.0x. Industrials are at 22.5x versus 17.1x, while technology is at 21.5x versus 18.8x. The relevant question is what explains each gap.

A sector's own history is a starting point, not a fair-value verdict. Business mix, returns on capital, balance-sheet structure and earnings cyclicality change over time. A premium can be justified, and a discount can correctly anticipate weaker profits.

Low beta and low valuation are separate characteristics. Consumer staples have a 0.6 beta but a 20.7x forward P/E, above their 18.1x average. Paying a premium for stable earnings can still create valuation risk. Defensive business demand does not guarantee a defensive purchase price.

The comparison helps identify what requires further explanation. A premium warrants stronger evidence of durable growth or lower risk. A discount warrants an examination of whether the earnings denominator is unusually high or structurally vulnerable.

Decision: Ask why the multiple differs from history before deciding whether the difference is an opportunity.

Equities / 14

Sector selection should combine growth, valuation and sensitivity

Highlighted slide 15 | a multi-dimensional research screen

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

One attractive column can hide an unattractive trade-off. Technology combines 43.4% NTM earnings growth with a 39.7% S&P 500 weight, a 21.5x forward P/E and a 0.6% dividend yield. It offers a different proposition from real estate, with 6.3% growth and a 3.8% dividend yield.

Recent returns do not identify future bargains. Energy's 40.3% YTD total return is high, but its NTM growth is 4.3%. Communication services have a 4.5% total return and -0.3% forecast growth. Each combination needs an earnings and industry explanation rather than extrapolation.

Rate sensitivity is evidence about a period, not a fixed property. The source's sector correlations to Treasury-yield moves use trailing two-year monthly observations, while beta uses ten years of monthly price returns. Treat these as different summaries and avoid presenting them as structural causal coefficients.

Build a shortlist from a consistent set of questions. What growth is priced in, how cyclical is it, what income is received, and what can force investors to sell? Security-level balance sheets and cash flows are the next step; the sector table cannot replace them.

Decision: Use multiple columns to frame due diligence, then choose instruments with current prices and complete terms.

Economy / 15

The labor market is adding jobs, but the trend is weaker than the latest month

Highlighted slide 18 | distinguish a release from a trend

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

The August payroll gain is materially above the recent average. The source shows 162,000 jobs added in August against a three-month average of 71,000. The exact gap is 91,000. One stronger month does not by itself reverse a weaker hiring trend.

Unemployment and wages tell different parts of the story. Unemployment stands at 4.1% versus a 30-year average of 5.5%. Wage growth for production and non-supervisory workers is 3.3%, just below its 3.4% long-run average. Those readings do not describe the same population or mechanism as payroll additions.

A cooling labor market can help inflation while pressuring demand. Slower pay growth can ease business cost pressure, but weaker employment or income growth can also restrain household spending. The balance matters for both corporate revenue and credit performance.

Look for confirmation across releases and revisions. Payroll estimates are revised, and aggregate averages can conceal industry differences. A sensible monitoring process checks the sustained direction of hiring, unemployment and pay instead of reacting to a single headline.

Decision: Use the three-month trend and subsequent revisions as a cross-check on the latest payroll release.

Economy / 16

Lower core CPI does not establish that the Fed's inflation task is finished

Highlighted slide 20 | use the right inflation measure

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

S3-S4 explain measurement. Source PCE rates use seasonally adjusted data; the original CPI contribution chart is non-seasonally adjusted.

The source shows an incomplete and uneven inflation slowdown. Headline CPI is 3.4% in August and core CPI is 2.4%, down from 2.5% in July. Headline PCE is 3.4% and core PCE is unchanged at 3.0%. These figures describe related but different price baskets.

The Fed's longer-run 2% goal is defined using PCE inflation. Core PCE helps assess underlying pressure, while the stated target refers to overall PCE. A core-CPI reading closer to 2% therefore cannot be treated as proof that the official objective has been achieved. [S3]

Differences between CPI and PCE have methodological explanations. They cover different spending, weights and formulas. The gap need not be a data error or a signal that one is the only valid measure. The relevant measure depends on the question being asked. [S4]

Separate a temporary price shock from persistent pressure. The source component chart illustrates why energy, shelter and services can move the headline differently. Exact component contributions are not printed for every month, so this report compares the reported headline measures instead of inventing a recreated stack.

Decision: Track PCE, wages and the persistence of services pressure before turning disinflation into a duration bet.

Data / reference period: July-August 2026; guide dated 30 Sep 2026. Original analysis and original exhibit design.
Economy / 17

A strong aggregate household balance sheet does not protect every borrower

Highlighted slide 26 | wealth and repayment capacity are different

Public edition: the detailed numerical exhibit is not reproduced. The dated findings and analysis below refer to the source slides linked at the end of this section.

Aggregate assets substantially exceed aggregate liabilities. The guide reports USD217.8 trillion of household and nonprofit assets and USD21.9 trillion of liabilities in 2Q26. Their difference is USD195.9 trillion, calculated directly from the printed totals. This is an aggregate stock, not a cash buffer available to each family.

Much of the asset base cannot immediately fund a missed payment. Homes account for 25% of assets and pension funds for 16%; other financial assets account for 47%. Deposits represent 7%. Asset ownership, liquidity and income are unevenly distributed.

The debt-service ratio is lower than its pre-crisis peak but remains an estimate. The guide shows 11.1% for 3Q26, versus 15.8% in 4Q07. The latest point is a J.P. Morgan estimate. It is a payment-to-income flow measure, not liabilities divided by assets.

The portfolio implication is to distinguish resilience from vulnerability. Aggregate wealth can support spending and confidence while specific households face refinancing or income pressure. Consumer credit exposure therefore requires borrower and collateral analysis in addition to the macro headline.

Decision: Assess liquid resources and income at the borrower level; do not infer repayment capacity from aggregate net worth.
