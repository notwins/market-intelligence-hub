Who is buying gold?

Central-bank buyers, sellers and what drives a Singapore investor’s return.

Market Intelligence Hub  ·  Research edition  ·  6 November 2026
Full report · PDF · 24 pagesExecutive briefing · PowerPoint · 5 slidesVisual story · PowerPoint · 5 slides
  1. China and Poland led disclosed Q3 buying. Roughly 63% of total estimated demand cannot be attributed to named buyers.
  2. A central bank may sell gold to raise currency, rebalance a portfolio or meet a policy obligation. The reason changes how to read the sale.
  3. For Singapore investors, both the gold price and USD/SGD determine the unhedged return. Bank forecasts also have different dates and horizons.
Market snapshot Spot gold traded at US$4,044.09/oz on 6 November at 13:01 Singapore time. Reuters
01Official demand

Central banks bought more gold as the price weakened

Official buyers stepped up sharply after a weak first quarter. The November data show a net 57.7 tonnes in Q1, 296.3 in Q2 and 321.2 in Q3. The last figure was 42% above a year earlier. Yet the World Bank reports that gold prices fell 5% from Q2 to Q3. A reserve manager can add to a holding while other investors sell enough to push the price down.

The estimate changed enough to alter the story of the year. The first Q1 release put buying at 244 tonnes. The Q2 release revised that sharply downward, and the November table puts it at 57.7. Combining April’s Q1 estimate with November’s Q2 and Q3 figures would exaggerate the level of demand and hide the rebound.

02How to read the data

Who bought the remaining gold?

Public filings identify only part of Q3 buying. The World Gold Council puts disclosed net purchases at 120 tonnes, compared with roughly 321 tonnes after Metals Focus incorporates estimates. The gap covers late reporting and trades that never appear in time for publication. It cannot be reliably assigned to China, a Gulf state or another named buyer.

The answer to “is buying up this year?” depends on the series. For January to September, reported purchases fell 7% year on year to 228 tonnes. Estimated demand rose 6% to 675 tonnes. Both comparisons can be right because their coverage differs. A monthly change in disclosed reserves is evidence about that balance sheet, while imports can also reflect households, jewellers, banks and funds.

03Disclosed buyers

China picked up the pace as Poland approached its target

China added 63 tonnes in Q3, more than half its purchases for the first nine months. Its September statement gives a direct check on the quantity. Holdings rose by 740,000 ounces to 77.47 million ounces, the 23rd consecutive monthly increase. The announcement gives no price target or motive for that month’s purchase.

Poland’s governor confirmed on 5 November that reserves had reached the 700-tonne goal. Quarterly additions slowed from 51 tonnes in Q2 to 40 tonnes in Q3. He left open further purchases as overall reserves grow. The next indication of demand will come from the board’s decision on how much gold to hold.

Uzbekistan illustrates how purchases can coexist with a willingness to sell. The central bank added 14 tonnes in Q3. Its governor had discussed selling at favourable prices as part of reserve management. A willingness to sell gives the bank flexibility and leaves the timing open.

04Disclosed sellers

Gold supplied cash when some official holders needed it

Russia’s governor linked recent reductions mainly to the finance ministry’s transactions under its fiscal rule. The National Wealth Fund holds assets that the ministry can use under that rule. The governor also cited metal used for coin production. The published explanation does not allocate the quarter’s 19-tonne decline across those uses.

Türkiye used gold to obtain foreign currency when demand for cash rose. In March, the central bank both disposed of metal and arranged gold-for-currency swaps. Under a swap, the bank agrees to reverse the exchange at maturity. Adding the swap amount to outright sales would overstate the gold permanently sold.

Azerbaijan’s sovereign wealth fund reported the same gold stock in March and September. Its year-end to September holdings fell by 21.9 tonnes. The fund states its diversification policy and allocation limits. Its holdings disclosures leave the specific reason for the reduction open.

05Reading the global register

The largest vaults tell a different story from the largest buyers

A ranking by tonnes held answers who owns the stock today. The World Gold Council’s register covers the top 100 disclosed holders and provides a separate file of changes. Most November entries refer to September, while some refer to June or earlier. The prior Hub report screened that roster. This update verifies selected moves and the cases below against their own disclosures.

Five legacy holders still account for about 52% of world gold reserves. Federal Reserve staff identify the United States, Germany, Italy, France and the IMF. Together they have added little since the 1970s. Their existing stock can rise by billions of dollars in value without producing a new purchase.

Gold can arrive in a country’s vault without the country buying it. The New York Fed stores metal for official account holders. South Korea illustrates a different issue: owning units in a gold fund gives the central bank exposure to the price, while the central bank reports a separate monetary-gold stock.

06Historical reversals

Czechia rebuilt its stock while Switzerland held steady

Czechia reversed an earlier decision to sell most of its gold. The Czech National Bank resumed more active buying in 2023. Its own analysis found that a holding near 100 tonnes would make its financial results less sensitive to market swings. That explains the target from the perspective of this particular institution.

The Swiss National Bank still holds the same 1,040 tonnes it held at the end of 2008. Its gold is stored in Switzerland, London and Canada to preserve access in a crisis. All three locations belong to the same Swiss stock. Counting the location of bars as a purchase would create a trade that never took place.

Europe’s coordinated sales wound down before the agreement expired in 2019. The ECB said signatories had sold little for nearly a decade. A world in which legacy holders sell less and other reserve managers buy more can show net purchases even when few institutions reverse their own policy.

07Case study · Ghana

A gold export programme can earn dollars and lose money

Ghana turned locally mined gold into foreign currency during a debt crisis. When access to external borrowing tightened, the Bank of Ghana could buy gold from local producers in cedis and export it for dollars. In 2025 it purchased and exported about 104 tonnes of artisanal and small-scale gold worth US$10.9 billion. Most of that metal passed through the programme.

The same programme generated losses of over US$1.7 billion in 2025, equivalent to 1.5% of GDP. The IMF identifies the biggest contributor as the gap between the exchange rate used to buy the gold and the reference rate used in the bank’s accounts. Assay fees, service charges and discounts to buyers added to losses. Part of the figure reflects accounting valuation; it cannot all be read as a cash loss.

GoldBod took over the programme’s operations and costs in July 2026. The central bank retained a role as fiscal agent and as recipient of monetary gold for reserves. A change in who buys raw metal can therefore occur alongside continued changes in central-bank bullion holdings.

08Reserve management

Why hold an asset that pays no interest?

A gold bar has no issuing government that must repay it. For a reserve manager, that feature can matter when access to foreign assets becomes uncertain. A bar held at home reduces exposure to a foreign custodian. It may still have to be moved, sold or pledged before the central bank can supply dollars to local banks. The IMF authors separate long-term reserves from the cash needed at short notice. In an illustrative two-day liquidation under stress, the IMF staff note suggests discounting the headline value by 6–12% to estimate usable liquidity.

More respondents expected global gold holdings to grow than expected their own bank to buy. Among respondents to the 2026 World Gold Council survey, 89% expected global official holdings to rise in the next year and 45% expected their own holdings to rise. Diversification, performance in crises and inflation protection featured in their answers. The survey records intentions. It cannot give the date or size of a trade.

09Gold and US Treasuries

A higher reserve share can come from the same bars

Gold’s share of reserves can rise before a central bank buys another ounce. The IMF authors found that between 2018 and 2025 the market value of official gold rose 268%, while the physical quantity rose about 8.5%. A reserve portfolio is like a household balance sheet. If a home appreciates, property becomes a larger share of wealth even when the family buys no property.

The ranking also changes when the investor group changes. A September Federal Reserve staff note points out that world gold includes US government gold, while foreign official Treasury holdings exclude the US. At end-2025, gold outside the US was worth US$4 trillion against US$3.9 trillion in foreign official Treasuries. By June 2026 Treasuries were ahead on that comparison even though gold ounces had increased.

10The price of gold

Funds bought gold in October and still lost value

Investors added US$2.7 billion to gold funds in October, yet the funds finished the month worth less. The World Gold Council’s specialised ETF report shows that physical holdings rose by 19 tonnes to 4,275 tonnes, while total assets fell 6% to US$542 billion. The lower price of the metal already in the vault outweighed the value of new subscriptions.

The next trade can come from a buyer with a very different deadline. OCBC’s 26 October tracker found investment managers had reduced their futures exposure and that protection against a fall had grown dearer relative to upside protection. It also observed a Shanghai physical premium of about 0.9%. A wholesaler paying extra for metal and a fund selling futures to raise cash can act on the same day.

Households responded differently again. The World Gold Council’s Q3 table showed bar-and-coin demand fell 19% year on year and jewellery consumption fell 17%. A higher price can discourage a wedding purchase even while an official buyer pursues a multi-year policy. The amount one group buys gives no mechanical price target for the next trade.

11Bank and agency outlooks

The World Bank and the Reuters poll disagree on 2027

Two October forecasts give opposite answers to the same question. For the average gold price in 2027, the World Bank estimates US$4,250 an ounce, US$350 below its 2026 figure. The Reuters poll puts 2027 at US$4,650, US$155 above its 2026 median. The gap between their 2027 forecasts is US$400. It reflects disagreement among forecasters, rather than a trading range.

An annual average can fall even if gold ends the year above today’s price. An average includes all the observations across the year. A high starting point or a few strong early months can lift it. Standard Chartered’s targets instead describe where the bank expects gold to trade at particular horizons. Combining all these figures into a single target would answer no clear question.

12Reading the bank papers

What happened to J.P. Morgan’s US$6,000 call?

The US$6,000 forecast in the supplied paper was published on 9 June. The detailed table defined it as a Q4 2026 average. On 3 July Reuters reported a new J.P. Morgan forecast of US$4,500 for the same quarter. Comparing the same horizon gives a US$1,500 reduction, or 25%. The November date on the saved copy records when the web page was printed.

The bank’s change of mind had a stated economic reason. By July it saw softer demand from key buyers and greater sensitivity to real yields. Real yield is the return a bond offers after allowing for inflation. When that return rises, investors give up more income by holding a bar that pays no coupon. Central banks can keep accumulating gold while private investors change what they are willing to pay for it.

13Singapore reserves

MAS rebuilt some of the gold it had trimmed

Singapore’s reported gold rose by 323,268 fine troy ounces in the first nine months of 2026. That reverses the direction of the two preceding year-end changes. The September balance remained below December 2024. Describing this as a partial rebuild fits the series. It also avoids guessing at MAS’s preferred entry price or the reason for each adjustment.

MAS gives readers both an accounting value and a physical volume. Its September return shows US$6,223.50 million for gold on its published book-value basis and separately lists 6,546,481 fine troy ounces. Using the ounce line avoids turning accounting changes into assumed purchases. The category includes gold deposits and, where appropriate, swapped gold.

14The Singapore investor

Translate the gold return into the currency you spend

A Singapore investor who owns unhedged gold has two prices to watch. XAU/USD quotes the US dollars needed to buy one troy ounce. USD/SGD quotes the Singapore dollars needed to buy one US dollar. Multiply the two at the same time to get Singapore dollars per ounce. A stronger Singapore dollar means each US dollar of gold proceeds converts into fewer Singapore dollars.

Use the actual instrument and matching timestamps at both ends. A physical bar has a dealer spread and may carry storage costs. An ETF has a fee and its trading price can differ from the value of its gold. A derivatives contract can add collateral, leverage or rolling costs. The earlier Hub study traced XAU39344-USD to a derivatives listing. Joining that series to a spot quote would give a misleading return.

15Portfolio decisions

What would change the investment case?

Choose the reason for holding gold before choosing an entry. If the objective is long-term diversification, ask whether the position still helps when it loses value at the same time as shares. If the objective is a short trade, the price of money, the US dollar and leveraged positioning can matter sooner than the next quarterly reserve release. A cash need in SGD requires a separate currency and liquidity decision.

A useful test is to specify what would change your mind. A stronger dollar and rising real yields can pressure gold even as official demand holds up. Conversely, easier real yields and persistent investment inflows can support a recovery. These are conditional paths. Data observed together can support a narrative without proving which trade moved the price.

16Reading guide

Which research paper answers which question?

The most useful papers look at different parts of the gold market. The IMF authors study what a reserve manager can do with bullion in a crisis. The ECB and Federal Reserve papers test how much of the rise in gold’s reported importance comes from prices and existing holdings. Bank outlooks focus more closely on the marginal buyer and the next few quarters.

The silver and renminbi papers help define the limits of the gold argument. J.P. Morgan’s silver research discusses solar manufacturers reducing silver use. That industrial channel is much less central to the official gold story. OCBC’s November renminbi note concerns its currency outlook, and its sustainability weekly covers other policy developments. Neither establishes the identity of an undisclosed gold buyer.
