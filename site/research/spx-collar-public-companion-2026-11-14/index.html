Protection has a price: less upside

SPX put-and-call collars · Educational companion

  • Hold equity exposure, buy a put and sell a call.
  • A 0% floor and 9% cap are a hypothetical expiry rule, not a verified zero-premium quote.
  • A maturity floor is not a guarantee of your daily account value.

The trade-off, in plain English

The put provides downside protection at expiry; the sold call gives away gains above its strike. Its premium can help fund the put. The actual net premium depends on market prices, chosen strikes, expiry and execution costs.

With a reference starting at 100, the illustration ends between 100 and 109. A reference ending at 70 gives a combined price payoff of 100; at 105 it gives 105; at 120 it gives 109. This is matched-notional, same-expiry payoff arithmetic before premiums, dividends and costs, not an available product offer.

What the historical illustration found

Across 65 complete calendar years, the floor affected 17 years and the cap affected 38. Ten years lay between 0% and 9%, inclusive. These are historical sample counts, not future probabilities.

Less annual downside did not mean more ending wealth. A starting 100 became 11,780.24 for the price-index path versus 3,871.85 for the hypothetical annually reset 0%–9% rule. Both exclude dividends and costs; the clipped path is not a historically priced options strategy.

Before treating it as an investment

Require real contract quotes and matching terms. Check the underlying vehicle, strikes, expiry, settlement, premium, spreads, fees and early-exit value. Each annual reset would need fresh market terms. The research does not validate an IFI product, guarantee a 0% net return, or establish zero-cost protection.
