Price, yield and return definitions

Bid prices are quoted per 100 face value in the bond currency. BOS and OCBC history uses the printed report date and reported yield to maturity. WSJ uses asked yields. Bondsupermart uses bid yields to maturity or to worst as labelled. A point represents the source observation only.

Choose a fixed-rate, noncallable U.S. Treasury note or bond to generate its history. TreasuryDirect supplies the coupon, issue date and maturity. The catalogue includes matured issues within the FRED archive, which starts in January 1995. TreasuryDirect auction data

The tenor picker uses the original term at auction, rounded to the nearest year for issues with an additional month component.

The model begins 30 calendar years before maturity or at the first available FRED date. It uses the last eligible observation in each month. The modeled yield discounts semiannual coupons and principal. The theoretical price is per 100 of face value and excludes accrued interest. Before issuance, both lines are hypothetical.

Each estimate uses the two published Treasury tenors that bracket the remaining maturity. The 15-year point sits halfway between 10 and 20 years. When the remaining maturity falls outside the available curve, the model uses the closest published tenor. The 30-year rate was suspended from February 2002 to February 2006. The price chart switches to the first saved bid. The yield chart switches to the first reported yield. A dotted connector marks each transition when the two dates are within seven calendar days. Larger gaps remain open.

Yearly price change uses the first and last available bid in each calendar year within your selected range. It excludes coupons, accrued interest, fees and FX. ACE Fixed AR is the workbook snapshot percentage and has no historical time series.
