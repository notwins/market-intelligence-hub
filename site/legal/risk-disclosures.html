Investment & modelling risks

Bond, leverage, FX, derivatives and scenario risks explained.

A financial model can be mathematically correct and still lead to a terrible financial decision. The calculator only knows what went into it; markets, borrowers and funding desks have no obligation to follow the spreadsheet.

1. Bonds: yield is not the same as a guaranteed profit

A fixed coupon does not make a bond's market price fixed. Duration measures sensitivity to yield changes, while convexity and non-parallel curve shifts alter the actual outcome. A long-dated Treasury or zero-coupon STRIP can fluctuate dramatically before maturity even when the issuer continues to meet its obligations.

Yield to maturity assumes specified cashflows and treatment of reinvestment, defaults and calls. Clean price, dirty price, accrued coupon and settlement cash paid are different amounts. Callable or structured notes may require yield-to-worst and a review of embedded options rather than relying on a headline yield.

2. Leverage and CHF funding: assets and debts must stay separate

A financed portfolio may look attractive when bond coupons exceed the initial borrowing rate. That comparison excludes what happens when the exchange rate changes, financing rolls at a different rate, collateral values fall or a lender changes lending ratios. Foreign-currency shocks can move faster than a bond's monthly coupon accrual.

Keep the underlying portfolio's market value, net asset value, liabilities, financing interest, currency translation, realised cashflow and investor capital outlay in separate lines. Do not double-count a currency adjustment already reflected in NAV. A positive expected carry is not a cap on losses or a guarantee against forced liquidation.

Historical stress events such as the Swiss franc's abrupt repricing in 2015 are useful risk scenarios, not predictions of the next move.

3. Options: premium received is not a free yield

A short put can lead to assignment at the strike price when the market price has fallen substantially. Cash-secured or margin-backed structures differ in capital requirements. A long put hedge costs premium and loses time value; option values also move with implied volatility, interest rates and remaining maturity. A simulated one-week result should not be confused with profit held to expiry.

Strategies involving spreads, covered calls, collars and structured options may have early exercise, gap, liquidity, counterparty or settlement risks. Profit-and-loss displays should identify assumptions and contract multipliers, not hide them behind a single annualised percentage.

4. Structured investments and insurance: read the contract

Knock-in and knock-out barriers, autocall terms, issuer credit risk, coupon conditions and observation dates matter as much as the advertised headline coupon. A step-down schedule changes possible redemption dates and subsequent cashflows.

Insurance projections, index floors and caps, policy fees, mortality charges where applicable, surrender conditions and income conversion mechanics must be based on the actual contract. Illustrations are not the same as guaranteed payouts. CPF and CPF LIFE scenarios must use the rules, rates and eligibility conditions applicable to the stated date and participant.

5. Model and market-data risks

Even a precise formula can produce misleading results if a quote is stale or the model mixes cashflows, FX, gains, debt balances and returns. Backtests may suffer from survivorship bias, look-ahead bias, selective history, curve-fitting and underestimated costs. AI-written explanations can introduce further mistakes.

Always verify source dates and sessions, units and quotation conventions, instrument identifiers, rounding and fee assumptions. Compare optimistic, base and adverse scenarios, including an explicit funding or liquidity shock where leverage is involved.

6. Your portfolio and privacy

A simulation may reveal more than you intend about your wealth, assets, borrowing and risk exposure. Never paste confidential client holdings, non-public investment instructions, passwords, access tokens or full account statements into an unverified online tool. Review the Privacy Policy before entering real figures.

Bottom line: A useful tool helps you understand a decision, not outsource responsibility for it. Consult current issuer terms and an appropriately qualified adviser before making a commitment.
